# What Is a RACI Chart? Definition, Template and Examples for Assigning Clear Owners

*Last updated: 24 November 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A RACI chart is a table that shows, for every task or decision, who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed. Tasks go in rows, people go in columns, and each cell holds one of the 4 letters. The 1 golden rule: every task has exactly one Accountable person.

A **RACI chart** ends the most expensive question in any project: "Wait, who owns this?" It is also called a responsibility assignment matrix or linear responsibility chart, according to [Wikipedia's entry on the model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibility_assignment_matrix).

Below you will find the definition, what each letter means and a free template. You also get 3 worked examples, 6 steps to build one and a way to keep it current.

## What is a RACI chart?

A RACI chart is a responsibility matrix that assigns 4 types of involvement to every task in a project: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed. Its purpose is to make ownership visible, so work does not stall while people wait for someone else.

The RACI chart definition is short, and in plain terms the RACI chart meaning is "who does what, who decides, who to ask and who to tell." Teams use it in project management, operations, HR, IT service management and anywhere several teams share work.

### What does RACI stand for?

The RACI chart acronym stands for 4 roles. Each person on a task gets one letter, and some tasks will not involve everyone.

| Letter | Role | What it means | How many per task |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | Responsible | Does the work | 1 or more |
| A | Accountable | Owns the result and signs off | Exactly 1 |
| C | Consulted | Gives input before or during the work (two-way) | 0 or more |
| I | Informed | Is told about progress or the decision (one-way) | 0 or more |

### What is the difference between Responsible and Accountable?

Responsible people do the work, while the 1 Accountable person owns the outcome and approves it. One person can hold both on small tasks. On larger tasks, splitting them adds a second check on the work.

A simple test: if the task fails, who answers for it in the review? That person is Accountable.

## What is a RACI chart used for?

It is used to clarify roles whenever 3 or more people or teams share a piece of work. Atlassian describes it as a way to [clarify roles and responsibilities](https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/raci-chart) so team members know who to go to for approvals, input and updates.

Common uses:

- **Project kickoffs.** Assign owners for each deliverable before work starts.
- **Cross-team launches.** Product, marketing, sales and support know their part.
- **Recurring processes.** Month-end close, hiring, incident response.
- **Reorganizations.** Show how responsibilities move between teams.
- **Meeting follow-ups.** Turn "someone should..." into a named owner.

### What are the benefits of a RACI chart?

The main benefits are fewer dropped tasks, faster decisions and fewer surprise escalations. With exactly 1 Accountable person per task, there is always someone who can say "yes" or "no."

- Clear owners for every task
- Faster approvals because the decision maker is named
- Less over-communication: Informed people get updates, not meeting invites
- Easier onboarding for new team members
- A record that resolves "I thought you were doing it" disputes

## RACI chart template

California's Child Welfare Digital Services defines RACI as [a responsibility assignment chart](https://cwds.ca.gov/glossary/111) that maps every task, milestone or key decision to the people involved. The US Department of Health and Human Services also published a [RACI diagram tool](https://www.opa.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/2020-12/short-lesson-learned-raci-diagram-tool.pdf) after using it on a multi-stakeholder program evaluation.

Copy this RACI grid into a spreadsheet or document. Replace tasks and names, and fill each cell with R, A, C, I or leave it blank.

| Task or deliverable | [Person 1] | [Person 2] | [Person 3] |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Task 1] | A | R | C |
| [Task 2] | R | A | I |
| [Task 3] | C | R | A |
| [Task 4] | I | C | A, R |

For a RACI chart template in Excel or Google Sheets, put tasks in column A, people or roles across row 1, and add a data validation drop-down with the 4 letters. Conditional formatting that colors each letter makes gaps easy to spot.

Teams with more than 4 people can list roles (for example "Product manager") instead of names, so the chart survives staff changes.

## What does a RACI chart example look like?

Each example RACI chart below covers a common situation. Each shows how 1 Accountable person anchors every row.

### Example 1: Website launch (project management)

This website launch has 5 tasks and 4 roles, with the project manager accountable for 3 of them.

| Task | Project manager | Designer | Developer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Define requirements | A, R | C | C |
| Design pages | A | R | C |
| Build site | A | C | R |
| Write copy | C | A, R | I |
| Launch and announce | A | I | R |

### Example 2: Hiring a new engineer (HR)

This hiring process has 4 steps, and the hiring manager stays accountable for 3 of them.

| Task | Hiring manager | Recruiter or HR | Interview panel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Write job description | A | R | C |
| Source candidates | I | A, R | I |
| Interview | A | C | R |
| Make offer | A | R | I |

### Example 3: Agile RACI chart for a sprint

This sprint example covers the 3 Scrum roles across 4 activities.

| Activity | Product owner | Scrum master | Dev team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prioritize backlog | A, R | C | C |
| Run sprint ceremonies | I | A, R | C |
| Build and test stories | C | I | A, R |
| Sprint review demo | A | C | R |

In an agile RACI chart, the team often shares "A" for delivery while the product owner keeps "A" for priorities.

## How do you make a RACI chart?

Here is how to make a RACI chart in 6 steps: list the tasks, list the people or roles, assign the Accountable person first, then Responsible, Consulted and Informed, review it with the team, and share it where work is tracked.

1. **List tasks or deliverables.** Use 8 to 20 rows. More than that, and nobody reads it.
2. **List people or roles.** Include everyone who touches the work, including approvers.
3. **Assign A first.** One Accountable person per row, no exceptions.
4. **Assign R.** At least 1 person who will actually do the work.
5. **Add C and I sparingly.** Every C slows the task down, and every I adds an update to send.
6. **Review and publish.** Walk through it in a 15-minute meeting, then pin it in your project tool.

### What are the rules of a RACI chart?

The 5 rules of a RACI chart are: exactly 1 A per task, at least 1 R per task, no row with only C and I, no person with A on everything, and no column that is empty. Breaking any of them signals a gap or a bottleneck.

| Pattern you see | What it means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No A on a row | No one owns the outcome | Name one owner |
| 2 or more A's | Decisions will stall | Keep one, move others to C |
| Lots of C's | Slow, consensus-heavy work | Move some to I |
| One person with many A's | Bottleneck | Delegate ownership |
| Empty column | Person may not belong on the project | Remove or confirm |

## What are the alternatives to a RACI chart?

The main alternatives are RASCI, DACI and RAPID, each adding or renaming roles for specific needs. Most teams start with RACI because 4 roles are easy to remember.

- **RASCI** adds S for Supportive: people who help the Responsible person.
- **DACI** uses Driver, Approver, Contributors and Informed, and suits single decisions.
- **RAPID** (Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide) suits complex decisions with many stakeholders.
- **RACI-VS** adds Verifier and Signatory for regulated, sign-off-heavy work.

### Are RACI charts still used?

Yes, they are still widely used, especially in project management, IT and operations. Teams sometimes find them heavy for fast-moving work, so they keep the chart to the 10 to 15 decisions that matter most rather than every task.

## When should you not use a RACI chart?

Skip the chart when a task has only 1 or 2 people, when the work changes every few days, or when the team already has clear owners. In those cases the chart costs more time to maintain than it saves.

It also fails when it is built once and never shared. A chart that lives in a forgotten spreadsheet gives false confidence. If you will not review it at least once a month, a simple owner column in your task tool is the better choice.

Watch for these 3 warning signs that the chart has become overhead:

- People ask "who owns this?" even though the chart answers it
- The chart has more than 30 rows
- Nobody has edited it since the kickoff

## How do you keep a RACI chart up to date after meetings?

Update it within 24 hours whenever a meeting assigns a new task or changes an owner. Most charts go stale because ownership changes in meetings, but the chart lives in a separate file nobody opens.

A simple habit: end every project meeting by reading out new action items with their owners and due dates, then copy them into the RACI chart and your task tool.

## FAQ

### What is a RACI chart in project management?

In project management, it maps every deliverable in the project plan to the people who do it, own it, advise on it and need updates. It is usually created at the project kickoff and reviewed at each of the major milestones.

### Can one person be both Responsible and Accountable?

Yes. On small tasks, 1 person is often both Responsible and Accountable, written as "A, R." On larger tasks, splitting the roles adds a second check on the work.

### How many people should be Accountable for a task?

Exactly 1 person should be Accountable for each task. Two or more Accountable people means nobody is clearly in charge, and decisions slow down.

### How do you pronounce RACI?

RACI is usually pronounced "RAY-see," like the word "racy." Some teams spell it out as 4 letters, R-A-C-I.

### What is the difference between Consulted and Informed?

Consulted is two-way: those people give input before the work is done. Informed is one-way: those people are told about the result. Keep Consulted to the 1 to 3 people whose input changes the outcome.
