# How to Write Action Items in Meeting Minutes (With Examples and a Free Template)

## A meeting minutes format with action items that anyone can follow, plus 8 examples you can copy

Minutes of meeting action items are the tasks a meeting agreed on, written so each one has a single owner, a due date and a clear finish line. They usually sit in their own section at the end of the minutes, numbered, so they can be checked at the next meeting.

The rest of this guide shows you where action items go in the minutes, how to word them, 8 examples from real meeting types, a template you can paste into any document, and a simple routine for carrying open items forward so nothing quietly disappears.

## Why action items get lost in meeting minutes

Most minutes fail in the same way. They record what people said, in the order they said it, and the tasks end up buried in the middle of a paragraph.

Picture a typical line: "The team discussed the vendor contract and Sam mentioned he could look into the pricing options." Is that a task? Who owns it? When is it due? Nobody reading the minutes a week later can tell.

Minutes have two jobs. The first is to record what was decided. The second is to record who will do what next. The first job is about the past. The second is about the future, and it needs its own section.

## Where action items go in a meeting minutes format

A clean meeting minutes format with action items has six parts, in this order:

- **Meeting details:** name of the group, date, time, place or link, and who chaired.
- **Attendance:** who was present, who sent apologies, and who took the minutes.
- **Approval of last minutes:** a one-line note that the previous minutes were approved, with any corrections.
- **Matters arising:** a review of open action items from the last meeting.
- **Discussion and decisions:** one short entry per agenda item, ending with the decision in a single sentence.
- **Action items:** a numbered list of every new task, with owner and due date.

Two parts in that list do most of the work. "Matters arising" makes the group look back at what it promised. "Action items" makes it look forward. Skip either one and tasks start slipping.

## How to write action items that actually get done

Good action items share four traits. Use them as a quick test before the minutes go out.

- **Start with a verb.** Send, book, draft, review, decide, fix. "Pricing" is a topic. "Send three pricing options" is a task.
- **Name one owner.** A person, not a team. "Marketing" cannot be chased. "Priya" can.
- **Give a date.** "By Friday, October 17" beats "soon" or "next week".
- **Make the finish line visible.** Someone outside the meeting should be able to tell when it is done.

One more rule matters for formal minutes: write decisions in the past tense and action items in the future. "The committee approved the budget" is a decision. "Dana will circulate the approved budget by October 20" is an action item. Mixing the two is how readers lose track of which is which.

## 8 examples of action items in meeting minutes

Here are eight minutes of meeting action items from four common meeting types. Each one passes the four-part test above.

**Board or committee meeting**

1. Treasurer to circulate the revised budget to all members by October 20.
2. Secretary to book the venue for the annual general meeting by November 1.

**Team meeting**

3. Lee to update the onboarding checklist and share it in the team folder by Thursday.
4. Ana to confirm the holiday cover rota with HR by October 24.

**Project meeting**

5. Raj to send the revised timeline to the client by Wednesday.
6. Mia to raise the API access request with IT by tomorrow.

**Client or vendor meeting**

7. Sam to send the signed statement of work to the vendor within 3 working days.
8. Priya to schedule the first weekly check-in with the client this week.

Notice the pattern: name, verb, object, date. It reads a little stiff, and that is fine. Minutes are a record, not a story.

## A meeting minutes template with action items you can copy

Paste this into Google Docs, Word or any notes app before the meeting starts, and fill it in as you go.

> **[Group name] meeting minutes**
>
> Date: [date] · Time: [start to end] · Location: [room or link]
>
> Chair: [name] · Minutes: [name]
>
> Present: [names] · Apologies: [names]
>
> **1. Approval of last minutes**
> [Approved / approved with corrections: details]
>
> **2. Matters arising (open action items from last meeting)**
> [Item number, owner, status: done / in progress / carried forward to (new date)]
>
> **3. Agenda item: [topic]**
> Discussion: [2 to 3 short points]
> Decision: [one sentence, past tense]
>
> **4. Agenda item: [topic]**
> Discussion: [2 to 3 short points]
> Decision: [one sentence, past tense]
>
> **5. Action items**
> 1. [Owner] to [verb + task] by [date]
> 2. [Owner] to [verb + task] by [date]
>
> Next meeting: [date and time]

Number action items continuously across meetings if your group meets often (for example 2026-14, 2026-15). It makes "matters arising" much faster, because people can refer to an item by number.

## How to carry open action items forward

Writing action items is half the job. The other half is checking them, and that happens at the start of the next meeting.

Run "matters arising" in under five minutes with three questions per open item:

- **Is it done?** If yes, mark it done in the new minutes and move on.
- **If not, what is blocking it?** One sentence, no discussion yet.
- **What is the new date?** Carry it forward with the new date, and keep the original item number.

If an item has been carried forward three times, stop and ask whether it is still needed. A task that keeps slipping usually needs a different owner, a smaller scope or a decision to drop it.

Send the minutes within 24 hours of the meeting. The longer you wait, the more people rely on memory, and memories of who agreed to what drift fast.

## Do you need consent to record a meeting for minutes?

Many minute-takers record the meeting so they can check the wording later. In the US, federal law allows recording when at least one participant consents ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)), but several states require everyone to agree. In the EU, a recording of identifiable people is personal data under the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

The simple rule works everywhere: say at the start that the meeting is being recorded, and why. This is general information, not legal advice.

## How to capture action items faster

The slowest part of taking minutes is catching tasks while also following the discussion. A few habits help:

- **Write tasks the moment they are agreed,** not at the end.
- **Read the action items aloud in the last 3 minutes,** so people correct owners and dates while they are still in the room.
- **Use a recording for wording,** not for the whole set of minutes.

## Checklist for minutes of meeting action items

- Action items have their own numbered section at the end of the minutes.
- Every item starts with a verb and names one person.
- Every item has a date.
- Decisions are in the past tense; action items are in the future.
- "Matters arising" reviews last meeting's open items first.
- Items carried forward three times get a fresh decision.
- Minutes go out within 24 hours.

Good minutes are not a transcript. They are a short list of what was decided and who is doing what next.
