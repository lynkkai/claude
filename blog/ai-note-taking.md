# AI Note Taking: How It Works, Where It Helps, and How to Choose a Tool

Taking notes in a meeting means doing two jobs at once: listening and writing. You do both badly. You miss the nuance while you type, and your notes miss half of what was said.

**AI note taking** splits those jobs. The AI listens and writes; you take part in the conversation. This guide explains how AI note takers work, what they are genuinely good at, where they still fall short, and how to pick one.

## What is AI note taking?

AI note taking is using software to **record a conversation, transcribe it, and turn the transcript into structured notes** (a summary, decisions, action items) without anyone typing them up.

It is most often used for:

- **Online meetings** on Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams
- **In-person conversations**: client meetings, interviews, workshops, [doctor visits](https://lynkk.ai/use-cases/doctor-visits)
- **[Lectures and classes](https://lynkk.ai/use-cases/lectures)**
- **[User and research interviews](https://lynkk.ai/use-cases/user-interviews)**
- **Voice notes and [dictation](https://lynkk.ai/features/dictation)**, where you think out loud and the AI writes it down

## How AI note takers work

Under the hood, almost every AI note taker follows the same five steps.

### 1. Capture

The tool gets the audio. There are three common ways:

| Method | How it works | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Meeting bot** | A bot joins the call as a participant and records it. | Reliable and hands-off, but everyone sees a bot in the participant list. |
| **Botless (on your device)** | A desktop app or browser extension records the call audio from your computer. | No bot in the call, but it only works on the device it is installed on. |
| **Phone or laptop mic** | Records the room. | Works for in-person conversations, where there is no "call" to join. |

Good tools offer more than one, because the right method depends on the meeting.

### 2. Transcription

Speech recognition turns audio into text. Modern models are accurate on clear audio, but accuracy drops with crosstalk, heavy accents, poor microphones and specialist vocabulary.

### 3. Speaker identification

The tool works out **who said what** (often called diarization). This matters more than people expect: "we'll ship by Friday" means something very different depending on whether the customer or your engineer said it.

### 4. Summarization and extraction

A large language model reads the transcript and produces:

- A **summary** of the main topics
- **Decisions** that were made
- **[Action items](https://lynkk.ai/features/action-items)** with owners and dates
- Sometimes **structured fields**, such as deal stage or next steps for a CRM

### 5. Storage and search

The notes are saved and indexed. The better tools let you **search across every conversation** and ask questions in plain language ("what did the client say about budget last month?") instead of scrolling through old documents.

## What AI note taking is good at

- **Getting everything down.** A transcript does not get tired or distracted at minute 50.
- **Freeing you to participate.** You can listen, ask better questions, and make eye contact instead of typing.
- **Consistency.** Every meeting gets the same structure, whoever ran it.
- **Speed.** Notes and action items arrive minutes (in some tools, seconds) after the call, while everyone still remembers it.
- **Memory across meetings.** Search turns a pile of past calls into something you can actually use.
- **Multilingual conversations.** Good tools handle meetings in several languages, including people switching languages mid-sentence.

## Where AI note takers still fall short

Being honest about the limits makes you use the tools better:

- **Bad audio in, bad notes out.** A laptop mic at the far end of a meeting room will produce a weak transcript, however good the AI is.
- **Context the AI never heard.** If a decision was made in a hallway, or relies on a document nobody mentioned, the AI cannot know.
- **Judgement calls.** The AI can tell you what was said. It is less reliable at telling you what mattered most to your team. Skim the summary and fix what it got wrong.
- **Names and jargon.** Product names, acronyms and people's names are the most common errors.
- **Consent.** Recording people has legal and social rules. An AI note taker does not remove the need to ask.

The best workflow is: **let the AI draft, and spend two minutes reviewing.** That is still far faster than writing notes from scratch.

## How to choose an AI note taker: a checklist

Use these questions when comparing tools.

**Capture**
- [ ] Does it work on all the meeting platforms you use (Zoom, Meet, Teams)?
- [ ] Can it record **without a bot** when a bot would be awkward, for example on customer calls?
- [ ] Can it record **in-person** conversations, not just video calls?
- [ ] Does it start automatically from your calendar, so you do not have to remember?

**Quality**
- [ ] How accurate is it in **your** meetings, with your accents and vocabulary? Test with a real meeting, not a demo.
- [ ] Does it label speakers correctly?
- [ ] Does it support the **languages** your team actually speaks, including mixed-language conversations?

**Output**
- [ ] Are the notes **structured** (summary, decisions, action items), or just a transcript with a paragraph on top?
- [ ] Do action items have **owners and dates**?
- [ ] How quickly do notes arrive after the meeting?

**Workflow**
- [ ] Does it send tasks where your team works, such as **Jira** or your CRM?
- [ ] Can you **search and ask questions** across all past meetings?
- [ ] Does it help **before** the next meeting, by surfacing what is still open?

**Trust**
- [ ] Is data encrypted? Is the vendor GDPR compliant?
- [ ] Can you choose **where your data is processed**, if your company or clients require it?
- [ ] Is it clear who can see which recordings?

**Price**
- [ ] Is there a free plan you can test properly?
- [ ] What does it cost per person once the whole team uses it?

## Getting the most out of AI note taking

1. **Tell people at the start.** "I'm using an AI note taker so I can focus on the conversation" works well and is usually welcomed.
2. **Improve the audio once.** A decent headset or a meeting room speakerphone does more for note quality than any setting.
3. **Say decisions out loud.** "So, to confirm: we're going with option B, and Priya owns the mockup by Thursday." AI note takers (and humans) pick that up perfectly.
4. **Review within the hour.** Fix names, confirm owners, delete anything sensitive that should not be shared.
5. **Share the notes, not the recording.** People read a one-page summary. Nobody watches a 60 minute video.
6. **Use search before your next call.** The biggest payoff is not one meeting's notes. It is remembering everything across all of them.

## Quick answers

**Is AI note taking accurate?**
On clear audio, modern AI note takers are very accurate for transcription. Summaries are usually good but should be skimmed for errors, especially names, numbers and nuanced decisions.

**Is it legal to use an AI note taker?**
It depends on where you and your participants are, and on your company's policy. Many places require consent to record. Always tell participants and follow local law. This article is not legal advice.

**Do AI note takers work for in-person meetings?**
Some do. Bot-based tools only join video calls. Look for a tool with a desktop or mobile app that can record a room, like Lynkk.

**Do I still need to take my own notes?**
Not full notes. Many people jot down a few words for their own thoughts or follow-ups, and let the AI handle the record of what was said.

**What is the difference between an AI note taker and a transcription app?**
A transcription app gives you the words. An AI note taker also gives you a summary, decisions and action items, and (in the better tools) lets you search and ask questions across all your meetings.
