# What Is a Discovery Call and How US Sales Teams Run One Well in 2026

*By [Samarth Wagh](https://lynkk.ai/blog/authors/samarth-wagh), Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** What is a discovery call? It is the **first real sales conversation** with a prospect, usually about **30 minutes**, where you learn their **problem, goals, buying process and timeline** to decide whether there is a fit, before you pitch or demo. It follows a short agenda and ends with an agreed **next step**.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant used on sales calls.*

New to sales, or moving from a non-sales role? Then "discovery call" can sound like jargon. Really it is just the meeting where you listen more than you talk, and a good discovery call saves both sides time: the buyer gets understood, and you find out whether a deal is real.

Below: the purpose of a discovery call, how long it should be, a simple agenda and script, 25 questions to ask, and how to follow up. If a search for "what is a discovery call" brought you here, the 30-second answer sits in the box above.

## What is a discovery call in sales?

In sales, a discovery call is the 1st real meeting after a prospect shows interest, where the seller asks questions to understand the buyer's situation and qualify the opportunity. Demo or proposal comes later ([Vidyard's sales glossary](https://www.vidyard.com/glossary/sales-glossary-discovery-call/) defines it the same way). [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales/what-is-a-discovery-call/) and most sales methodologies treat it as the foundation of the deal.

What a discovery call is not:

- **Not a demo.** You might show nothing at all.
- **Not a pitch.** [Gong's 2025 analysis of 326,000 sales calls](https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/) found reps who won deals talked 57% of the time, against 62% for reps who lost.
- **Not an interrogation.** Gong's same analysis found winners asked 15 to 16 questions, while reps who lost asked around 20.

## What is the purpose of a discovery call?

Ask 5 reps what is a discovery call for and you get 5 answers. The purpose of a discovery call is to answer 4 questions: Does the prospect have a problem you can solve? Does it matter enough to act on now? Who is involved in the decision? Is there a realistic path to buying?

| Purpose | What you learn |
|---|---|
| Problem | The pain, its cause and its cost |
| Priority | Why now, and what happens if nothing changes |
| People | Decision makers, influencers, users |
| Process | Budget, timeline, steps to purchase |

Many teams use frameworks such as BANT (budget, authority, need, timeline) or MEDDIC to structure these questions, and [preparing from past conversations](https://lynkk.ai/blog/meeting-preparation) beats improvising. Covering all 4 areas matters more than which framework you pick.

## How long should a discovery call be?

Most discovery calls should be 30 minutes. [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales/what-is-a-discovery-call/) puts it as under 60 minutes and 30 if you can: 20 to 30 minutes for a simple transactional sale, and up to an hour, often spread over several calls, for a complex enterprise deal. So what is a discovery call meant to cover in half an hour?

A 30-minute discovery call agenda:

1. **Introductions and agenda (3 min):** confirm time and goals.
2. **Their situation (10 min):** current process, team, tools.
3. **Pain and impact (10 min):** what is not working and what it costs.
4. **Decision process (5 min):** who decides, budget, timeline.
5. **Next step (2 min):** agree on a specific follow-up meeting.

## What is a good discovery call script?

Think of a discovery call script as a flexible outline, not lines to read. Salesforce suggests leaving a full 5 minutes at the end for the next step, which is why the close is the last block of this one:

```
OPEN
"Thanks for making time, [Name]. I'd like to spend most of our
30 minutes learning about [company] and what prompted you to reach out.
At the end, if it makes sense, we can agree on a next step. Sound good?"

SITUATION
"Walk me through how your team handles [process] today."

PAIN
"What's not working about that?"
"What does that cost you, in time, money or risk?"

PRIORITY
"Why is this coming up now?"
"What happens if nothing changes in the next 6 months?"

PROCESS
"Besides you, who else would be involved in a decision like this?"
"How have you bought tools like this before?"

CLOSE
"Based on what you've shared, I think [specific value] could help.
Would it make sense to set up a demo with [stakeholder] next week?"
```

## What questions should you ask on a discovery call?

Ask open questions that start with what, how and why. Below are 25 discovery call questions grouped by purpose. Pick 8 to 10 per call.

**Situation**

1. How does your team handle [process] today?
2. What tools are you using for it?
3. How many people are involved?
4. What does a typical week look like for this team?
5. What prompted you to look at this now?

**Pain and impact**

6. What is the biggest challenge with the current approach?
7. How often does that happen?
8. What does it cost you in hours, revenue or risk?
9. What have you tried so far?
10. Why did those attempts fall short?

**Goals and priority**

11. What would success look like in 6 months?
12. How would you measure it?
13. Where does this rank among your priorities this quarter?
14. What happens if you do nothing?
15. Who else cares about solving this?

**Decision process**

16. Who else will be involved in the decision?
17. What does your buying process usually look like?
18. Is there a budget set aside for this?
19. What timeline are you working toward?
20. What could stop this from moving forward?

**Fit and next steps**

21. What would you need to see to feel confident?
22. Are you evaluating other options?
23. What matters most in choosing a solution?
24. Who should join the next conversation?
25. What is the best next step from your side?

## Why are discovery calls important?

Discovery calls are important because they decide whether the rest of the sales process is worth anyone's time. Salesforce's State of Sales finding that 87% of business buyers expect reps to act as trusted advisors is earned or lost here. Done well, 1 call qualifies out bad-fit deals early, gives you the buyer's own words for later proposals, and makes the demo shorter.

They also build trust. Gong's data points the same way: in lost deals the seller's longest monologue runs longer, and buyers disengage, which is why many sales teams treat discovery quality as a leading indicator of win rate.

## How is a discovery call different from a cold call?

A cold call is an unscheduled first contact to earn a conversation, lasting 1 to 5 minutes. So what is a discovery call by comparison? A scheduled conversation with someone who already agreed to talk. Their goals differ:

| | Cold call | Discovery call |
|---|---|---|
| Who initiates | Seller, unannounced | Both sides, scheduled |
| Goal | Earn a meeting | Understand and qualify |
| Length | 1 to 5 minutes | About 30 minutes |
| Prospect's mindset | Interrupted | Expecting the call |

## Which discovery frameworks should you know?

3 frameworks cover most sales discovery. Use them as checklists, not scripts.

- **BANT:** budget, authority, need, timeline. Simple and quick for smaller deals.
- **MEDDIC:** metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, identify pain, champion. Created at PTC in 1996, and built for complex B2B deals with several stakeholders ([MEDDICC](https://www.meddicc.com/what-is-meddicc)).
- **SPIN:** situation, problem, implication, need-payoff questions, from Neil Rackham's 1988 book. Helps the buyer see the cost of their problem in their own words.

Salesforce's own guide uses a POWERFUL acronym for discovery questions, which covers similar ground. Whatever framework you choose, make sure your notes capture each part so the next person on the deal does not repeat the questions.

## What are common discovery call mistakes?

Each of these 6 mistakes comes from forgetting what is a discovery call for:

1. **Pitching too early.** Showing the product before understanding the problem.
2. **Asking yes or no questions.** They end conversations instead of opening them.
3. **Not quantifying the pain.** Without a cost, there is no urgency.
4. **Ignoring the decision process.** Learning about a procurement step in month 3.
5. **No agreed next step.** "I will follow up" is not a next step; a booked meeting is.
6. **Poor notes.** Losing the buyer's exact words, which are the best material for the proposal.

## What is the difference between a discovery call and a demo?

A discovery call is for listening and qualifying, usually in 30 minutes; a demo is for showing how your product solves the problems you uncovered, usually in 30 to 60. Built on good discovery, demos are shorter and more relevant. Why? You show only what matters to that buyer.

| | Discovery call | Demo |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Understand and qualify | Show the solution |
| Who talks most | The buyer | The seller |
| Typical length | 30 minutes | 30 to 60 minutes |
| Output | Notes, qualification, next step | Evaluation, proposal |

## What is a discovery call outside of sales?

Outside sales, a discovery call is any first call, typically 15 to 30 minutes, to understand someone's needs before offering a service. Coaches, consultants, agencies, lawyers and freelancers use free discovery calls, usually 15 to 30 minutes, to see whether they are a good fit for a client. In recruiting, a discovery call can mean an early conversation to understand a role or a candidate's goals.

Ask a coach or a consultant what is a discovery call and you hear the same 3 steps: understand the situation, the problem and the goal, then agree on a next step.

## How do you prepare for a discovery call?

Spend 10 to 15 minutes preparing. Check the prospect's website, their LinkedIn profile, any past conversations with your company, and why they booked the call. Salesforce's advice is to arrive with insights, a vision of the outcome and ideas for getting there, so write 3 hypotheses to test with your questions.

## How do you follow up after a discovery call?

Send a follow-up email within 24 hours, the same day where you can, restating what you heard, confirming the next step and including anything you promised. Keep it short and specific to their situation.

In a good recap, include the prospect's top goal, main challenge and timeline in their own words, then the date of the next meeting. ## FAQ

### What is the main purpose of a discovery call?

To understand the prospect's problem, priorities, decision process and timeline, and to decide together whether there is a fit worth pursuing. One number worth knowing: Gong found the average seller talks 60% of a call, while reps who won talked 57%, so the gap is smaller than most coaching suggests.

### How long is a discovery call?

Most run about 30 minutes. Salesforce advises under 60 minutes, 20 to 30 for a simple transactional sale, and up to an hour across several calls for a complex enterprise deal. Book 30 by default, and keep the last 5 minutes for agreeing the next step.

### Is a discovery call an interview?

Not exactly. Think two-way conversation. While the seller asks most of the questions, the buyer should also learn something useful and decide whether to continue, leaving with a clearer view of their own problem and options. Good calls feel useful to the buyer.

### Are discovery calls free?

In sales, yes: they are part of the sales process. Coaches and consultants also usually offer free discovery calls of 15 to 30 minutes. In law, a first consultation may be free or paid depending on the firm, so check before booking.

### What happens after a discovery call?

After the call, the seller sends a recap and, if there is a fit, books the next step, often a demo with additional stakeholders or a proposal review. Send that recap the same day, using the prospect's own words for their goals, challenges and timeline.

### What is another name for a discovery call?

You may also hear it called a discovery meeting, a qualification call, a needs analysis call or, for consultants and coaches, a consultation call. Whatever the name, the structure is the same: understand the situation, the problem and the goal, then agree on a next step.
