## What is legal transcription?

Legal transcription is the conversion of legal audio or video into written text, and it is usually done in 1 of 3 ways: by a human legal transcription service, by AI software or by a certified court reporter. Typical recordings include client interviews, attorney dictation, depositions, hearings and case team meetings.

The difference matters. An official record of federal court proceedings must come from the court's reporter, while a client meeting transcript is an internal working document. Knowing which 1 you need decides the tool.

## What does legal transcription look like?

Legal transcription usually looks like a script with 1 speaker label per turn, and the words are kept close to verbatim. Here is a short illustrative example of an internal client meeting transcript, the kind Lynkk produces:

> **Attorney:** When did you first receive the notice from the landlord?
> **Client:** On March 3. It was taped to the door, and I took a photo.
> **Attorney:** Please send me that photo and the lease by Friday.

## What do law firms need from transcription?

Law firms need 3 things from transcription: accuracy they can rely on, strict confidentiality and fast turnaround so work can start the same day. The right balance depends on what the transcript is for.

- **Accuracy:** client meeting notes can tolerate small errors; anything filed or quoted cannot.
- **Confidentiality:** client conversations are privileged, so the tool's security matters as much as its accuracy. The ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct (Rule 1.6) require lawyers to protect client information.
- **Speed:** a case team wants notes and tasks before the next call, not in 2 days.

When a firm needs all 3 at once, 2 tools often work better than 1: a human service for verified transcripts and a faster AI tool for daily meetings.

## When should a law firm still use a legal transcription service?

A law firm should still use a human legal transcription service, or a certified court reporter, in 3 situations: official court records, text that will be filed or quoted as evidence, and recordings where every word must be verified.

Federal law is clear on court records. Under [28 U.S.C. 753](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title28-section753&num=0&edition=prelim), federal court proceedings are recorded verbatim, and only transcripts certified by the court's reporter count as official. AI transcription does not replace that.

For everything else, such as client intake, strategy calls, internal case meetings and first-pass witness interviews, AI transcription is faster and far cheaper.

A simple rule helps teams decide. If the transcript will be filed, quoted in a brief or shown to the other side, send it to a human service or use the court's certified record. If it helps the team remember, plan and assign work, AI is the right tool, and the time saved goes back into the case.

## Will AI replace legal transcription?

AI will not replace all legal transcription: certified court records and verified evidence transcripts still need people. But AI already handles 1 large share of the work, the everyday conversations inside a firm, in seconds instead of days.

The practical model is a mix. Use AI like Lynkk for daily meetings and internal notes, and keep a human legal transcription service for the smaller set of recordings that must be verified word for word.

The skills also shift. Legal staff spend less time typing and more time reviewing: checking key quotes against the recording, tagging what matters to the case and turning notes into next steps. That review step is where accuracy is protected.

## Is it legal to record and transcribe client calls?

Yes, with the right consent, and the rule depends on where each person is. Under [18 U.S.C. 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511), federal law generally allows recording when 1 party consents.

Some states require everyone's consent. [California Penal Code 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632) requires all parties to agree before a confidential conversation is recorded. The safe habit for a law firm is to ask every participant at the start and note their answer in the transcript.

## FAQ

### Are transcripts legal documents?
It depends on the transcript. Under 28 U.S.C. 753, a federal court transcript certified by the court's reporter is treated as prima facie correct and is the official record. A transcript of a client meeting made with software is an internal working document, useful for notes and tasks but not an official record.

### What is the legal transcription format?
Legal transcription usually follows 3 conventions: a speaker label for each turn, text kept close to verbatim and a note of the date and participants. Firms often add their own rules for pauses or unclear words. Lynkk produces speaker-labeled transcripts with notes and action items for internal client and case meetings.

### How accurate is AI legal transcription?
Accuracy depends on audio quality and accents. Lynkk transcribes 15+ languages at about 97% accuracy with speaker labels. Human services publish higher claims, such as 99% or more. For anything quoted, filed or used as evidence, have a person verify the text against the recording before relying on it.

### Is AI transcription safe for confidential client meetings?
It can be, if the tool protects data properly and you get consent. Check 3 things: encryption, where data is processed and whether conversations train AI models. Lynkk encrypts data with AES-256, lets firms choose their processing region and never uses conversations to train anyone's model.
