# Sprint Retrospective Template: 6 Formats, Questions and Examples for Agile Teams

*Last updated: 24 November 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A sprint retrospective template has 4 sections: what went well, what didn't go well, what we learned, and action items with owners. The Scrum Guide timeboxes the retrospective to a maximum of 3 hours for a one-month sprint, so most 2-week teams need 45 to 90 minutes.

A **sprint retrospective** is the meeting at the end of every sprint where a Scrum team looks back at how it worked and agrees on what to change. A good template keeps that conversation from drifting into complaints and makes sure it ends with 1 to 3 concrete actions.

Below you will find a copyable sprint retrospective template, 6 popular formats, 15 questions, a sample filled-in retro, and how to run it in Jira, Confluence or Miro.

## What is a sprint retrospective?

It is a Scrum event where the team inspects the last sprint and plans improvements. According to the [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html), its purpose "is to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness," and it concludes the sprint.

The team looks at people, interactions, processes, tools and its Definition of Done. The most useful changes can go straight into the next sprint backlog.

### When is a sprint retrospective meeting held?

The retro is held at the end of each sprint, after the sprint review and before the next sprint planning. For a 2-week sprint, that usually means every other Thursday or Friday.

### How long should a sprint retrospective take?

The Scrum Guide sets a maximum of 3 hours for a one-month sprint and says shorter sprints usually need less. In practice, teams plan about 45 minutes per week of sprint.

| Sprint length | Typical retro length |
|---|---|
| 1 week | 30 to 45 minutes |
| 2 weeks | 45 to 90 minutes |
| 3 weeks | 90 to 120 minutes |
| 4 weeks | Up to 3 hours |

## What is the difference between a sprint review and a sprint retrospective?

A sprint review looks at the product (what the team built), while a sprint retrospective looks at the process (how the team worked). The review includes stakeholders. The retrospective is usually limited to the Scrum team so people can speak openly.

| | Sprint review | Sprint retrospective |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | The increment and backlog | Team process and collaboration |
| Attendees | Scrum team + stakeholders | Scrum team only |
| Output | Updated product backlog | 1 to 3 improvement actions |
| Order | First | Second, closes the sprint |

## Sprint retrospective template

Copy this sprint retrospective template into a doc, a whiteboard or your team wiki. It works for any sprint length.

> **Sprint [number] retrospective** | Date: [date] | Facilitator: [name]
>
> **Sprint goal:** [Was it met? Yes / Partly / No]
>
> **1. What went well?**
> - [Item]
>
> **2. What didn't go well?**
> - [Item]
>
> **3. What did we learn?**
> - [Item]
>
> **4. Action items for next sprint**
>
> | Action | Owner | Due |
> |---|---|---|
> | [Action] | [Name] | [Sprint X+1] |
>
> **Follow-up from last retro:** [Were last sprint's actions done?]

Start every retro by reviewing the actions from the previous one. Save the filled-in sprint retrospective template each time so you can spot patterns across sprints. If they keep slipping, cut to 1 action per sprint.

## What are the best agile retrospective template formats?

The 6 most popular agile retrospective template formats are Start Stop Continue, Mad Sad Glad, Sailboat, 4Ls, Starfish and the simple "went well / to improve" format. Rotate formats every 3 to 4 sprints to keep the conversation fresh.

### 1. Start, Stop, Continue

Three columns: what the team should start doing, stop doing and keep doing. It is the fastest format and works well for new teams.

### 2. Mad, Sad, Glad

Team members sort feelings about the sprint into 3 columns. It surfaces morale issues that process-focused formats miss.

### 3. Sailboat

Draw a boat with 4 areas: wind (what pushed us forward), anchors (what slowed us down), rocks (risks ahead) and the island (our goal). Good for teams that like visual retros.

### 4. The 4Ls

Four columns: Liked, Learned, Lacked and Longed for. It balances positives and gaps and works well after long or complex sprints.

### 5. Starfish

Five areas: keep doing, more of, less of, stop doing and start doing. It gives more nuance than Start Stop Continue.

### 6. What went well, what to improve, actions

The classic 3-column format. Use it when you have only 30 minutes.

| Format | Best for | Time needed |
|---|---|---|
| Start, Stop, Continue | New teams, short retros | 30 min |
| Mad, Sad, Glad | Morale check | 45 min |
| Sailboat | Visual, goal-focused teams | 60 min |
| 4Ls | Long or complex sprints | 60 min |

## What questions should you ask in a sprint retrospective?

Ask 4 to 6 open questions per retro, mixing what happened, why, and what to change. Here are 15 retrospective questions grouped by purpose:

**About results**
1. Did we meet the sprint goal? Why or why not?
2. What are we most proud of this sprint?
3. Which story took much longer than estimated?

**About process**
4. Where did work get stuck waiting?
5. Did our standups help or just report status?
6. Were requirements clear when we started each story?
7. What slowed down code review or testing?

**About collaboration**
8. Did anyone feel blocked and not say so?
9. How well did we work with other teams?
10. Who helped you this sprint?

**About improvement**
11. What is 1 thing we should try next sprint?
12. What should we stop doing?
13. Did last retro's actions make a difference?
14. What would make next sprint 10% easier?
15. What is still puzzling us?

## How do you run a sprint retrospective?

To run a retro, follow 5 steps: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close. This structure, popularized by Esther Derby and Diana Larsen's book *Agile Retrospectives*, keeps the meeting from becoming a venting session.

1. **Set the stage (5 min).** Restate the goal and a ground rule such as "focus on the process, not people."
2. **Gather data (10 to 15 min).** Everyone adds notes silently to the template columns.
3. **Generate insights (15 to 20 min).** Group similar notes and discuss the top 3 themes.
4. **Decide what to do (10 min).** Vote and choose 1 to 3 actions with owners.
5. **Close (5 min).** Quick feedback on the retro itself.

Atlassian's [retrospective play](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/retrospective) follows the same flow and is a good reference for first-time facilitators.

### How do you make a sprint retrospective fun?

Make a retro fun by rotating the format, the facilitator and the setting every few sprints. Small changes, such as a 2-minute icebreaker, a themed board or a "retro of retros" once a quarter, keep people engaged without adding time.

## How do you create a retrospective template in Jira, Confluence or Miro?

You can set up a sprint retrospective template in all 3 tools in under 10 minutes, and each has built-in options.

| Tool | How to set it up |
|---|---|
| Confluence | Insert the built-in Retrospective template, then save your version as a space template |
| Jira | Create a "Retro action" issue type or label, and add actions to the next sprint backlog |
| Miro or FigJam | Start from a retrospective board template and duplicate it each sprint |

## FAQ

### Who attends a sprint retrospective?

The whole Scrum team attends: the product owner, the Scrum master and the developers. Stakeholders usually do not attend, so the 3 roles can speak openly about how they work.

### Is the sprint retrospective mandatory?

Yes. In Scrum, the sprint retrospective is 1 of the 5 events, and skipping it removes the team's built-in chance to improve. Teams under pressure can shorten it, but should not drop it.

### Can you combine the sprint review and retrospective?

You can hold them back to back, but keep them as 2 separate conversations. The review includes stakeholders and focuses on the product, while the retrospective is team-only and focuses on process.

### Should a sprint retrospective be anonymous?

It can be. Anonymous notes help in the first 2 or 3 retros or when trust is low. As the team gets comfortable, open discussion usually produces better actions.

### What is a project retrospective template?

A project retrospective template is a longer version used once at the end of a project rather than every sprint. It adds 3 sections: timeline review, goals vs results, and lessons to share with other teams.
