# Meeting Preparation: How to Walk Into Every Meeting With Context From the Last One

*Last updated: October 12, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** Good meeting preparation takes about 10 minutes: reread the last notes with the same people, list open tasks and decisions, set 1 goal and 3 agenda points, and write a one-page meeting brief. Lynkk does the first part for you with a morning briefing email showing who you are meeting, your last note with them and how many tasks are still open.

## What is meeting preparation?

Meeting preparation is the 10 to 15 minutes you spend before a meeting reviewing what happened last time, deciding what you want from this one, and writing it down. The goal is simple: start ahead, not cold.

Most people prepare the agenda and skip the history. That is backwards. The most useful prep is remembering what you already agreed, what is still open, and who promised what. Without it, the first 10 minutes of a meeting go to "where were we?"

## What should a meeting brief include?

A meeting brief is a one-page summary you read before a meeting, and a good one has 6 parts: who you are meeting, the goal, what happened last time, open tasks, open questions and the agenda. It should fit on 1 screen.

| Part | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Names, roles, company | Dana (VP Ops), Leo (IT) at Acme |
| Goal | The 1 outcome you want | Agree the pilot start date |
| Last time | Decisions from the last meeting | Pilot scope set to 2 teams |
| Open tasks | Who owes what | Leo: security review, due Oct 14 |
| Open questions | What is still unresolved | Budget owner for year 2 |
| Agenda | 3 to 5 topics, in order | Security review, dates, budget |

The "last time" and "open tasks" rows are the ones people skip, and they are the ones that save the most time in the room.

## Free meeting brief template

This meeting brief template takes about 5 minutes to fill in once you have last meeting's notes open. Copy it into a doc or your calendar event.

- **Meeting:** name, date, time, link
- **People:** who is coming, and their role
- **My goal:** 1 sentence, the outcome you want by the end
- **Last meeting:** 2 to 3 decisions, with the date
- **Open tasks:** each with an owner and a due date
- **Open questions:** anything you still need answered
- **Agenda:** 3 to 5 topics, most important first
- **After:** who sends the notes, and when

Keep the brief to 1 page. If it runs longer, the meeting probably needs splitting into 2.

## What is a good checklist for meeting preparation?

A checklist for meeting preparation should have 7 steps you can run in order in about 10 minutes. Here is a simple version.

1. **Read the invite.** Check who is coming and why. If the purpose is unclear, ask before the meeting, not during it.
2. **Find the last notes with these people.** Search by person or company, not by meeting title.
3. **List the open tasks.** Note who owes what, and whether it is done.
4. **List past decisions.** Write the 2 or 3 that this meeting depends on.
5. **Set 1 goal.** What must be true when the meeting ends?
6. **Write 3 to 5 agenda points.** Share them with attendees if you are running the meeting.
7. **Plan the follow-up.** Decide who records, who takes notes and who sends the summary.

Steps 2 to 4 are the slow ones when notes live in 5 different places. They are also the steps AI can do for you.

## How do you prepare for a meeting using past meeting notes?

To prepare from past notes, search by the people or company you are meeting, open the most recent note, and copy its decisions and open tasks into your brief. That takes 2 to 3 minutes when every meeting is recorded in 1 place.

It takes much longer when notes are scattered across docs, chat and notebooks. That is the real cost of weak note-taking: not the meeting itself, but every meeting that follows it.

## How long should meeting preparation take?

Meeting preparation should take about 10 minutes for a routine meeting and 20 to 30 minutes for a high-stakes one, such as a board meeting or a renewal call. If prep takes longer than the meeting, your notes are too hard to search.

| Meeting type | Prep time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Daily standup | 2 minutes | Yesterday's blockers |
| Weekly 1:1 | 5 to 10 minutes | Open tasks and feedback |
| Client or sales call | 10 to 15 minutes | Promises and open questions |
| Board or renewal meeting | 20 to 30 minutes | Decisions, numbers, risks |

Founders with 6 or more calls a day feel this most. For more on handling a full calendar, read [how founders keep up with back-to-back meetings](https://lynkk.ai/blog/founders-meeting-notes).

## Can you record a meeting without consent?

Not everywhere, so check before you record the meetings you prepare from. In the US, federal law ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)) allows recording with 1 participant's consent, but several states require everyone to agree. In the EU, a recording of identifiable people is personal data under the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

The simple rule is to say at the start of every meeting that it is being recorded. The [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework) is a useful checklist when you review any tool that stores meeting notes. Lynkk keeps recordings private to your account and never trains on your conversations. *This is general information, not legal advice.*
