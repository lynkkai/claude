# Voice Memos Are the Most Underrated Note-Taking Tool on Your Phone

*This story was written with AI assistance and edited by the Lynkk team.*

Most people open Voice Memos twice a year. It's the app for recording a song idea, a lecture, or a voicemail you want to keep.

That's a waste, because a phone you already carry everywhere, with a decent microphone and a recording app that syncs across devices, is a very good capture tool. Paired with transcription, it becomes a note-taking system that works when typing doesn't: walking, driving (hands-free, please), or sitting across a table from someone.

## Why voice notes beat typed notes in some situations

**You capture more.** Speaking an idea out loud takes seconds. Typing it on a phone keyboard takes long enough that you trim it down, and the trimmed part is often the interesting bit.

**You capture it sooner.** The best time to record a thought is the moment you have it. Voice Memos is one tap from the lock screen with a widget or Control Center button.

**You can capture conversations.** An interview, a site visit, a conversation with a contractor. With permission, a recording is more reliable than your memory of who said what.

## Getting a transcript out of a voice memo

Recent versions of iOS can transcribe Voice Memos recordings directly in the app, on supported devices and languages. You open a recording, tap the transcript option, and the text appears alongside the waveform. You can search and copy it.

When it works, it's great. When it doesn't (the option is missing, the transcript is blank, or your language isn't supported), the cause is usually one of a handful of settings or device limits. The Lynkk team wrote a step-by-step guide to [voice memo transcription on iPhone](https://lynkk.ai/blog/voice-memo-transcription), including how to fix transcription that won't start.

If you need a transcript of an older recording, or one in a language your phone doesn't support, you can export the audio file and run it through a transcription tool. Here's a broader guide to [transcribing any audio to text](https://lynkk.ai/blog/transcribe-audio-to-text), with free options on Mac and Windows and a comparison of paid tools.

## From transcript to notes

A raw transcript isn't a note. It's a pile of words. The trick is a two-minute pass right after recording:

1. **Title it with a who and a what.** "Site visit, Ardmore Foods, warehouse layout" beats "New Recording 47."
2. **Pull out three lines at the top**: the decision, the next step and the open question.
3. **Leave the transcript below** as the source, in case you need the exact words.

If you use a notes app, paste the three lines plus the transcript into one note. In three months, searching "Ardmore" will find it instantly.

## A few habits that make recordings usable

- **Say the context at the start.** "It's Tuesday, I'm with Sam from Ardmore, we're talking about the warehouse layout." Your future self and your transcription tool will both thank you.
- **Put the phone between speakers**, not in your pocket. Distance and fabric are the two biggest enemies of transcript quality.
- **Ask permission when recording other people.** Recording laws vary by place, and asking is simply courteous. Most people say yes when you explain it's so you can listen properly instead of scribbling.
- **Keep recordings short and topical.** Five 5-minute memos are easier to use than one 25-minute ramble.

## When to graduate from Voice Memos

Voice Memos is a great start. It runs out of road when:

- you record **long meetings** every day and want summaries and action items, not just text;
- you need **speaker labels**, so you know who said what;
- you want **one archive** for phone recordings, laptop meetings and online calls;
- you work in **more than one language**, sometimes in the same sentence.

At that point you're choosing between a dedicated recorder device and an app. Devices have a better microphone and a battery that won't die mid-meeting; apps cost less and work with what you already carry. We compared [AI voice recorders, devices vs apps](https://lynkk.ai/blog/ai-voice-recorders), on 12-month cost and transcript quality.

## Try it tomorrow

Next time you leave a meeting, a call or a site visit, open Voice Memos and talk for 60 seconds about what just happened: what was decided, what you need to do and what's still unclear. Transcribe it, title it, and save the three lines.

Do that for a week and you'll have better notes than most people get from an hour of typing.
