# Keyword Stuffing Check: Lynkk Use-Case Story Pages

> Checked 2026-10-17 on the 10 story drafts in `content/use-cases/story-drafts/`. Keywords come from Ubersuggest.

## Result

**No keyword stuffing found.** Every keyword reads as a normal part of a sentence, no paragraph repeats the same keyword, and no keyword goes above about 2.2% of the page's words.

Every page tells one simple story. Each story now carries its use-case keyword and one or two feature keywords (such as AI note taker, transcribe audio, meeting summary and action items), each used once in a natural sentence. The keywords sit inside the story, not on top of it.

Google's spam policy describes keyword stuffing as repeating words or phrases so often that it sounds unnatural, or listing them out of context. None of the pages do this.

## How we checked

- Counted every target keyword in the page copy (exact phrase, plural included, not case sensitive). The title and meta description are not counted in the totals.
- Worked out keyword density: uses x words in the keyword / words on the page.
- Checked each paragraph for the same keyword used twice.
- Checked where each keyword appears: title, meta description, H1, intro, the story, headings and other sections.
- Read each story as a reader would: does every keyword sound like a person wrote it?

**Safe range we used:** about 0.5% to 2.5% for the main keyword, and 1 to 3 uses for each smaller keyword.

## Feature keywords inside the stories

Besides the use-case keywords, each story now carries one or two keywords about what Lynkk does (AI note taker, transcription, meeting summary, action items and so on). These have much bigger search volume, and they fit the story because they describe what the person actually does with Lynkk.

How we kept them safe from keyword stuffing:

- Each feature keyword is used **once** per story, inside a normal sentence that moves the story forward.
- The mix changes from page to page, so the ten pages don't share the same keyword sentences (Google can treat copy-paste wording across many pages as low-value, scaled content).
- No paragraph has more than one feature keyword.
- They sit in the story (problem, how Lynkk helps), not in lists or footers.

## Notes

- Keyword density is only a rough guide. Google does not use a fixed number. What matters is that the page reads naturally and helps the reader.

- The keyword tables at the top of each story draft show the real number of uses and where each keyword appears.
