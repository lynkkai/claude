## What are meeting notes?

Meeting notes are a written record of a meeting that captures 3 things: decisions, action items and the people who own them. Unlike formal minutes, they can be informal, as long as they are accurate and shared.

For founders, notes are memory. With 6 different kinds of meetings in a week, from investor updates to customer calls, notes are the most reliable way to know what you promised and to whom.

## What should you include in meeting notes?

Useful notes include 6 items, and each one answers a question someone will ask later. Keep them short enough to read in 1 minute.

| Item | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Who attended | Who heard this? |
| Purpose | Why did we meet? |
| Decisions | What did we agree? |
| Action items | What happens next? |
| Owners and dates | Who does it, and by when? |
| Open questions | What is still unresolved? |

If a note does not answer at least the decisions and action items, it will not help you next week.

Keep the tone factual. Write what was agreed, not how the conversation felt, and use the other person's words for anything you promised them. That makes notes safe to forward to a co-founder or an investor without editing.

## What is the best way to take meeting notes?

The best way to take meeting notes when you run back-to-back calls is to stop taking them by hand. Taking meeting notes by hand while talking splits your attention in 2, and founders need full attention in investor and customer conversations.

There are 3 common approaches:

- **Typing during the call:** complete, but you listen less and speak less.
- **Writing notes after the call:** you listen fully, but details fade, and the next call starts before you finish.
- **An AI meeting notes taker:** you listen fully, and the notes arrive on their own.

For a founder with 20 or more calls a week, only the third approach scales.

## What do founders need from back-to-back meetings?

Founders need 3 things from back-to-back meetings: context before each call, focus during it and fast follow-up after it. The hard part is not any 1 meeting; it is switching between 6 or 8 of them in a day.

Here is an illustrative founder Tuesday:

| Time | Meeting | What is needed |
|---|---|---|
| 9:00 | Investor update | Numbers promised last month |
| 10:30 | Customer call | Open requests from the last call |
| 13:00 | Hiring interview | Notes from round 1 |
| 15:00 | Team standup | Blockers and owners |
| 16:30 | Partner call | Terms discussed before |

Without notes, every call starts cold. With AI notes, each one starts with the right context.

## What should I do after a meeting?

After a meeting, do 3 things within 24 hours: send the notes, confirm each action item's owner and date, and put the next step on the calendar. Follow-up speed is where deals and hires are won or lost.

Here is a weekly founder notes template to copy:

1. **Top 3 decisions this week**, with the meeting each came from.
2. **Open promises**, what you owe investors, customers and candidates.
3. **Action items by owner**, with due dates.
4. **Risks**, anything that came up more than once.
5. **Next week's key meetings**, with the context you need for each.

Lynkk fills items 1 to 3 from your meetings automatically, so the review takes minutes. Items 4 and 5 are yours: they are the judgment calls that only a founder can make about where the company is heading.
