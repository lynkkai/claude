## Post body

*Quick disclosure before anything else: I'm one of the people building [Lynkk](https://lynkk.ai), an AI meeting assistant. So yes, I have a horse in this race. I've tried to write the checklist I'd want a friend to use, even if it points them somewhere else.*

Almost every team I talk to has tried an AI note taker by now. A surprising number of them have quietly stopped using it.

When I ask why, the answers are rarely "the transcript was bad." Transcription is mostly a solved problem. The complaints sound more like this:

- "I got a nice summary. Then I still had to copy everything into the CRM."
- "It's great on Zoom, useless in the conference room."
- "I never read the notes. I just forget the meeting happened."
- "Legal asked where the recordings go, and nobody could answer."

That's the pattern: most AI note takers are built for the hour *after* a meeting. But meetings have three parts, and the expensive mistakes usually happen in the other two: walking in without context, and letting follow-ups die.

So here's the checklist I'd use to pick one.

### 1. Does it work in the room, or only on the call?

A lot of important conversations still happen in person: client visits, interviews, workshops, the hallway chat after the board meeting. Many tools only work when they can join a video call.

**Ask:** Can it record an in-person conversation from a laptop or phone? Does it still separate speakers? Do in-person and online meetings end up in the same searchable place?

If half your meetings are in person and the tool only lives in Zoom, you'll have a memory with holes in it.

### 2. Does it help *during* the meeting, or only after?

This is the question most buyers don't think to ask. A note taker that writes things down is useful. One that can answer a question while you're still in the meeting is a different kind of tool.

**Ask:** If someone says "what did we agree on pricing last time?", can the assistant find it and tell you, live? Can it pull up a document mid-call?

This was the reason we started Lynkk: Lynkk joins the meeting and speaks when you need it, so you can ask for past context out loud and get an answer before the conversation moves on.

### 3. Do you walk in prepared, or cold?

The best meeting notes are the ones you see *before* the next meeting.

**Ask:** Before a call, does the tool show what was discussed last time, what's still open, and who promised what? Or do you have to go digging?

### 4. What happens in the 5 minutes after the call?

A summary is the minimum. The real time sink is everything that should happen *because* of the meeting.

**Ask:**
- Are action items extracted *and assigned* to someone?
- Does it update CRM fields, or just give you text to paste?
- Can it create Jira tickets from what was discussed?
- How long does all that take? (Lynkk aims for about 30 seconds after the meeting ends.)

If the answer to most of these is "you can copy and paste," the tool has saved you note-taking, not work.

### 5. Can you find anything three months later?

Notes that live in separate documents are hard to search across. When someone asks "when did the customer first mention the integration issue?", you want one place to ask.

**Ask:** Can you search across every meeting at once, by person, topic or decision? Does it connect related meetings, or treat each one as an island?

### 6. Where does your data go?

This question gets skipped until legal or a customer asks it, and then it blocks everything.

**Ask:** Which region is my audio processed and stored in? Can I choose? Which models process it, and where do they run?

With Lynkk, you pick a region and every model behind it processes and answers from that region. Whatever you choose, get a clear answer in writing before rolling a tool out to a team.

### 7. Does it fit how your team actually works?

A few practical ones:

- **Languages:** Do your meetings happen in more than one language? Test it in the languages you actually speak.
- **Offline:** Does it work on a plane or with bad Wi-Fi?
- **Integrations:** Calendar, CRM, Jira, scheduling tools. And if you use something unusual, can it connect through MCP?
- **Price:** Is there a free plan to try it on real meetings before you commit?

### How to actually test one

Don't judge an AI note taker on a demo. Give it one real week:

1. Use it on at least one online call **and** one in-person meeting.
2. Before a follow-up meeting, check whether it showed you useful context.
3. Count how many things you still had to copy into other tools by hand.
4. Ask it a question about a meeting from five days ago.

The tool that wins that week is the one you'll still be using in six months.
