Hindi speech to text has quietly become one of the most useful features on Indian phones. It is faster than typing Devanagari on a small keyboard, and it works for WhatsApp messages, emails, documents and notes.

This guide shows how to turn on Hindi voice typing on every common device, what to use for long recordings and meetings, and how to get more accurate results.

## What is Hindi speech to text?

Hindi speech to text converts spoken Hindi into written text as you speak, usually in Devanagari script. It is also called Hindi voice typing or Hindi dictation. The same technology, applied to recordings rather than live speech, is called [Hindi transcription](https://lynkk.ai/blog/hindi-transcription).

Two ways to use it:

| Mode | Best for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Live voice typing | Messages, emails, short notes | Gboard, iPhone keyboard, Google Docs |
| Recorded or meeting capture | Interviews, lectures, meetings | AI transcription and meeting tools |

## How do you convert Hindi speech to text on Android?

On Android, Hindi speech to text works through Gboard, Google's keyboard, in 4 steps:

1. Open **Gboard settings**, then **Languages**, and add **Hindi (हिन्दी)**.
2. Open any app with a text box, such as WhatsApp or Gmail.
3. Switch the keyboard to Hindi with the globe or language key.
4. Tap the **microphone** and speak in Hindi.

Gboard writes in Devanagari. If you want Roman-script Hinglish instead, add the Hinglish or Hindi (Latin) keyboard layout and dictate in that layout.

## How do you use Hindi voice typing on iPhone?

On iPhone, Hindi voice typing uses the built-in keyboard dictation in 3 steps:

1. Go to **Settings**, then **General**, then **Keyboard**, then **Keyboards**, and add **Hindi**.
2. Make sure **Enable Dictation** is turned on in the same Keyboard settings.
3. In any app, switch to the Hindi keyboard and tap the **microphone** key.

Dictation follows the language of the active keyboard. If your words come out in English, switch keyboards before you tap the mic.

## How do you get Hindi speech to text online for free on a computer?

The easiest free Hindi speech to text online is Google Docs voice typing, which lists Hindi as a supported language and works in Chrome, Edge and Safari ([Google Docs Help](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/4492226)). It works on Windows, Mac and Chromebook:

1. Open a new Google Doc in your browser.
2. Click **Tools**, then **Voice typing**.
3. Choose **हिन्दी (भारत)** from the language list above the mic.
4. Click the microphone and speak.

Google Docs voice typing is free and has no time limit, but it needs a stable internet connection and the Doc must stay open. Its spoken punctuation commands work only in English, so add Hindi punctuation by hand.

## Can you use Hindi speech to text on Windows 10 and Mac?

Yes. On Windows 10 and 11, Google Docs voice typing in Chrome or Edge is the most reliable free route for Hindi. Windows' own voice typing (Windows key + H) supports a set of languages that depends on your Windows version and installed language packs, so check whether Hindi appears in your list.

On Mac, built-in dictation supports many languages. Go to **System Settings**, then **Keyboard**, turn on **Dictation**, and add Hindi in the dictation languages if it is available on your macOS version. For more Mac options, see our guide to [dictation on Mac](https://lynkk.ai/blog/dictation-on-mac).

## How do you make Hindi voice typing more accurate?

Most Hindi speech to text errors come from 5 causes, and all 5 are fixable:

1. **Wrong keyboard language.** Switch to Hindi before tapping the mic.
2. **Noise.** Move away from fans, traffic and TV, or use earphones with a mic.
3. **Speed.** Speak at a normal pace with short pauses between sentences.
4. **Punctuation.** Spoken punctuation support varies by keyboard, so plan to add full stops and commas by hand afterward.
5. **Names and English words.** Check and fix them after dictating, since they are the most common errors.

For meetings, place the device within 1 metre of the speakers and ask people not to talk over each other during decisions.
