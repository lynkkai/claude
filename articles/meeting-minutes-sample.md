## What is a meeting minutes sample?

A meeting minutes sample is a filled-in example of minutes that shows 2 things: what to record and how to lay it out. It saves time because you copy the structure and change the details, instead of starting from a blank page.

The 2 samples in this guide cover the most common cases: a team meeting and a formal board meeting. Both follow the same 6-part format, with the board version adding motions, votes and a quorum.

## What should be included in meeting minutes?

Meeting minutes should include 8 details, and the list is the same whether the meeting is a team sync or a board meeting.

| Detail | Example |
|---|---|
| Organization and meeting type | Board of Directors, regular meeting |
| Date, time and place | May 14, 6:00 pm, video call |
| Who presided and who took minutes | Chair and Secretary |
| Attendance and quorum | 7 of 9 directors present |
| Approval of prior minutes | April minutes approved as written |
| Decisions or motions with results | Motion passed 6 to 1 |
| Action items with owners and dates | Treasurer, by May 30 |
| Adjournment time | 7:05 pm |

Robert's Rules of Order, the parliamentary guide many boards use, says minutes should record what was done at the meeting, not what each person said. *Source: Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised.*

## What is the standard meeting minutes format?

The standard meeting minutes format follows the order of the meeting in 6 parts: header, attendance, prior minutes, agenda items with decisions, action items and adjournment. Keeping that order makes minutes easy to check against the agenda.

Write in the past tense and a neutral voice. Record decisions and results clearly, such as "The team agreed to..." or "The motion carried 6 to 1", and leave out side conversations and opinions.

## What does an example of team meeting minutes look like?

Here is 1 example meeting minutes page for a weekly team meeting. It is short, scannable and focused on decisions and owners.

> **Team:** Growth team, weekly meeting
> **Date and time:** Tuesday, May 14, 10:00 to 10:30 am, video call
> **Attendees:** Maya (lead), Jordan, Priya, Lee. Absent: Sam.
> **1. Last week's actions:** Landing page test launched (done). Pricing survey still open (Priya).
> **2. Campaign results:** Email sign-ups rose 12% week over week. Decision: keep the new subject line.
> **3. Webinar plan:** Decision: run the webinar on June 5.
> **Action items:** Book speaker (Jordan, by Friday). Draft invite email (Lee, by Monday). Close pricing survey (Priya, by Thursday).
> **Next meeting:** Tuesday, May 21, 10:00 am.

This meeting minutes example fits on 1 screen, which is the goal for most internal meetings.

## What does a board meeting minutes sample look like?

A board meeting minutes sample is more formal and records at least 4 extra things: the quorum, each motion, who moved and seconded it, and the vote. Here is a sample of board meeting minutes for a fictional nonprofit.

> **Organization:** Riverside Community Food Bank, Board of Directors
> **Meeting:** Regular meeting, May 14, 6:00 pm, video call
> **Presiding:** Dana Lee, Chair. **Minutes:** Chris Moore, Secretary.
> **Attendance:** 7 of 9 directors present; quorum confirmed.
> **Call to order:** 6:02 pm.
> **Approval of minutes:** April 9 minutes approved as written.
> **Treasurer's report:** Received and filed.
> **Motion 1:** To approve the 2026 operating budget. Moved by A. Patel, seconded by R. Gomez. Carried 6 to 1.
> **Motion 2:** To lease a second delivery van. Moved by R. Gomez, seconded by J. Kim. Tabled to June.
> **Action items:** Treasurer to file budget with the bank by May 30. Chair to request van quotes by June 1.
> **Adjournment:** 7:05 pm. Next meeting: June 11, 6:00 pm.

Use this board meeting minutes sample as a starting point, and check your bylaws for any extra items they require.

## How do you write minutes after a meeting?

You write minutes after a meeting in 4 steps, and the best time is the same day, while memory is fresh.

1. **Start from the agenda** and your notes or recording.
2. **Fill in the format** above, section by section.
3. **Record decisions and actions precisely**, including owners and dates.
4. **Send the draft** to the chair or lead for review before sharing.

With a recording, AI can do steps 1 to 3 for you. The secretary's job becomes checking the draft rather than writing it, which is faster and keeps the draft close to what was actually said and decided.

## When should meeting minutes be sent out?

Send team minutes within 24 hours so owners can start on their actions. For nonprofit boards, the IRS sets a timing benchmark worth knowing.

The [Instructions for Form 990](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990) ask whether an organization contemporaneously documented the meetings of its governing body and committees. The IRS treats documentation as contemporaneous if it is done by the later of the next meeting or 60 days after the meeting, and it can include approved minutes or similar writings that explain the action, when it was taken and who decided.

## Are meeting minutes legally binding?

Meeting minutes are the 1 official written record of what a board or committee decided, and they are often the main evidence of those decisions. The decisions themselves take effect under the organization's bylaws and applicable law, which is why accurate minutes matter.

That is also why minutes should be approved at the next meeting and stored safely. If your organization records meetings to help write minutes, get consent: under [18 U.S.C. 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511), federal law generally needs at least 1 party to agree, and some states require everyone.

## FAQ

### How long should meeting minutes be?
Meeting minutes should be as short as they can be while still recording every decision, motion result and action item. For a 30-minute team meeting, 1 screen is usually enough. Board minutes run longer because each motion, mover, seconder and vote is recorded, but they still leave out discussion detail.

### Can meeting minutes be approved by email?
It depends on your bylaws and state law. Many organizations approve minutes at the next meeting, while some bylaws allow approval by email. The IRS Form 990 instructions accept documentation permitted by state law, including approved minutes or similar writings. Check your bylaws before changing how you approve minutes.

### Do meeting minutes need to be signed?
Your bylaws and procedures decide whether minutes need a signature, and 1 common practice is for the secretary to sign the minutes after the board approves them, sometimes with the chair. For team meetings, no signature is needed; sharing the minutes in 1 agreed place is enough.

### Who takes minutes at a board meeting?
The board secretary is the 1 officer who usually takes or oversees the minutes at a board meeting, depending on your bylaws. Some boards ask staff to take notes for the secretary to review. With Lynkk, the secretary reviews a draft written about 30 seconds after the meeting instead of taking notes live.
