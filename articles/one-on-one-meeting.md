## What is a one-on-one meeting?

A one-on-one meeting is a regular conversation, often 30 minutes, between a manager and 1 direct report, usually called a 1:1. It belongs to the employee as much as the manager: it is where priorities are set, problems are raised early and growth is discussed.

It is not a status update. Project status can go in a standup or a written update. The 1:1 is for the things that do not fit in a group: feedback, concerns, career goals and support.

## How often should one-on-one meetings be?

A common rhythm is every 1 or 2 weeks, and US federal guidance also favors regular check-ins. The Office of Personnel Management tells supervisors to hold [regular performance check-ins and give frequent feedback](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/performance-management/performance-management-and-accountability-playbook/monitoring-and-developing/).

| Frequency | Best for |
|---|---|
| Weekly | New hires, fast-moving projects, first-time direct reports |
| Every 2 weeks | Most experienced team members |
| Monthly | Senior people with high autonomy, plus weekly async notes |

Keep the slot even when there is "nothing to discuss". Short meetings are fine; cancelled ones send the wrong signal. If you must move a 1:1, reschedule it in the same week rather than skipping it, so the rhythm holds.

## How long should one-on-one meetings be?

A 1:1 should usually last 30 minutes, with 45 to 60 minutes for monthly meetings or career conversations. The length matters less than protecting the time and keeping it focused.

If 1:1s often run over, check whether status updates are taking the space. Move those to writing, and keep the meeting for topics that need a conversation.

## How do you structure a one-on-one meeting?

A good agenda for one-on-one meeting slots has 5 parts, and the employee's topics come first. Here is a sample one-on-one meeting agenda for a 30-minute meeting:

1. **Check-in (3 minutes):** how the person is doing.
2. **Their topics (10 minutes):** whatever they want to raise.
3. **Priorities and blockers (7 minutes):** what matters most and what is in the way.
4. **Feedback and growth (7 minutes):** in both directions.
5. **Next steps (3 minutes):** who does what, by when.

Share the agenda in a running document before the 1:1 so both people can add topics. Review last meeting's next steps at the start of each 1:1.

## What are good one-on-one meeting questions?

Good one-on-one meeting questions are open, specific and about the person, not just the project. Here are 20 questions for one-on-one meeting conversations, grouped by purpose.

### Check-in and wellbeing
1. What is on your mind this week?
2. How is your workload right now, on a scale of 1 to 10?
3. What gave you energy this week, and what drained it?
4. Is anything outside work affecting you that I should know about?

### Priorities and blockers
5. What are your top 3 priorities until we meet again?
6. What is blocking you, and how can I help?
7. Is anything taking longer than you expected?
8. Which meeting or task could you drop?

### Feedback
9. What is 1 thing I could do differently to support you?
10. What feedback do you have on how the team is working?
11. Is there anything you wish I had told you sooner?
12. What would you change about our 1:1s?

### Growth and career
13. What skill do you want to build this quarter?
14. What work would you like more of, and less of?
15. Where do you see yourself in 2 years?
16. Who in the company would you like to learn from?

### Team and company
17. How do you feel about the team's direction?
18. Is there anything the team should start or stop doing?
19. Do you understand how your work connects to company goals?
20. What question do you wish I had asked today?

Pick 3 or 4 per meeting. Asking all 20 turns a conversation into a survey. Rotate through the groups over a month so wellbeing, blockers, feedback and growth each get regular time, and note which questions led to the most useful answers.

## What should you say in a one-on-one meeting with your manager?

In a 1:1 with your manager, bring 3 things: an update on your priorities, a blocker or decision you need help with, and 1 question about growth or feedback. Prepare them in a short list before the meeting.

Be direct about problems early, while they are small, and bring a suggested fix when you can. Ask for feedback on something specific, such as "How did my presentation land?", which gets a more useful answer than "Any feedback?"

## Should you record one-on-one meetings?

Only with clear consent, and some 1:1s should not be recorded at all. Sensitive topics like health, conflict or personal matters are often better unrecorded, and either person should be able to say no.

When you do record, the law applies. Under [18 U.S.C. 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511), federal law generally needs at least 1 party to agree, and some states require everyone. ## FAQ

### What is the purpose of a one-on-one meeting?
A 1:1 gives a manager and 1 team member private time to align on priorities, remove blockers, share feedback and discuss growth. It builds trust and catches problems early. Unlike a status meeting, the employee sets much of the agenda, and the meeting ends with clear next steps for both people.

### Are one-on-one meetings effective?
They are effective in 3 conditions: they happen regularly, follow an agenda and end with next steps that someone tracks. They fail when they are cancelled often or turn into status updates. Tracking follow-ups is the part most managers drop, which is why Lynkk links each next step to the meeting.

### What should I not say in a one-on-one meeting?
Avoid 2 things: turning a 1:1 into a list of complaints with no ask, and avoid discussing colleagues' personal matters. Keep feedback specific and about work. If something is sensitive, say so, and agree whether it should be written down. Focus on what you need and what you can do next.

### How do I title a one-on-one meeting invite?
Use a clear, consistent title such as "Alex and Sam 1:1" or "Weekly 1:1: Alex / Sam", so both calendars show it at a glance. Add a link to the running agenda document in the invite. Consistent titles also help Lynkk recognize the recurring 1:1 and bring context from last time.

### Can AI take notes in a one-on-one meeting?
Yes, with both people's agreement. Lynkk records the 1:1, writes notes with next steps after, and brings those next steps back before the following meeting. Either person can decline recording for sensitive conversations, and the manager can still add notes by hand.
