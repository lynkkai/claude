# How to Record Yourself on Mac: Video, Voice and Screen, for Free

## 4 built-in ways to record yourself on a Mac, plus the setup tips that make you look and sound ready

To record yourself on Mac, open QuickTime Player, choose File, then New Movie Recording, pick your camera and microphone from the arrow next to the Record button, and click Record. Photo Booth works too and is even simpler. If you want your screen and your face in the same video, combine the Shift-Command-5 screen recorder with a floating QuickTime camera window.

All of it is free and already on your Mac, whether it is a MacBook Air, a MacBook Pro or an iMac. This guide covers 4 ways to record yourself, when to use each, and the light, sound and framing tips that make the difference between "recorded on a laptop" and "looks ready to send".

## Which way should you record yourself?

Pick by what you need to make:

- **A video message or a practice run:** QuickTime Player's movie recording.
- **A quick clip with no settings:** Photo Booth.
- **A tutorial or a demo with your face in the corner:** Shift-Command-5 plus a QuickTime camera window.
- **Your voice only, for a podcast draft or a voiceover:** QuickTime audio recording or Voice Memos.

## 1. How to record yourself on Mac with QuickTime Player

QuickTime gives you the most control of the built-in options. Here are the steps, following Apple's QuickTime Player guide:

1. **Open QuickTime Player** from your Applications folder.
2. **Choose File, then New Movie Recording.** A window opens showing your camera.
3. **Click the arrow next to the Record button** and choose your camera, your microphone and the quality.
4. **Click Record** to start, and click it again to stop.
5. **Choose File, then Save,** and pick a name and a folder.

If you have an iPhone, you may be able to pick it in the camera list. Continuity Camera lets a nearby iPhone act as your Mac's webcam on recent versions of macOS and iOS, and the picture is usually much sharper than the built-in camera.

## 2. How to record yourself with Photo Booth

Photo Booth is the fastest route when you do not care about settings:

1. **Open Photo Booth.**
2. **Click the video button** at the bottom left of the window, to switch from photos to video.
3. **Click the Record button.** Click it again to stop.

The clip appears in the strip at the bottom. To save it somewhere else, drag it to your desktop or a folder. Photo Booth also has fun effects, which are great for a birthday message and less great for a job application.

## 3. How to record your screen and yourself at the same time

This is the setup most people want for tutorials, product demos and class presentations. Your screen fills the video, and your face sits in a small window in the corner.

1. **Open QuickTime Player and choose File, then New Movie Recording.** Do not press Record in this window.
2. **Choose View, then Float on Top,** so the camera window stays above everything else.
3. **Resize the camera window** and drag it to a corner of the screen.
4. **Press Shift-Command-5** to open the screen recording toolbar.
5. **Click Options and choose your microphone.** Without this step, your screen recording has no sound.
6. **Click Record.** Stop with the Stop button in the menu bar, or press Command-Control-Esc.

The camera window is part of your screen, so it is captured in the screen recording. The result is one video with you in the corner.

A note on sound: the microphone setting records your voice. If you also need the sound your Mac is playing, such as a video in your demo, look for a system audio option in the same Options menu on newer versions of macOS. That is the usual answer to how to record system audio on Mac without extra software.

## 4. QuickTime audio recording for your voice only

For a voiceover, a podcast draft or practice for a talk, you may not need video at all. QuickTime audio recording is the quickest route:

1. **Open QuickTime Player** and choose **File, then New Audio Recording.**
2. **Click the arrow** next to the Record button to choose your microphone.
3. **Click Record,** and click again to stop.
4. **Choose File, then Save.**

Voice Memos is just as easy: open it, click the red Record button, and click Done when you are finished.

## How to look better when you record yourself

The camera matters less than people think. The setup matters more. Before you press Record, run through these:

- **Face a window.** Daylight from the front is the most flattering light you will get for free. A window behind you turns you into a silhouette.
- **Raise the laptop.** Put it on a stack of books so the camera is at eye level. Looking down into a MacBook camera is the most common reason self-recordings look awkward.
- **Look at the camera, not the screen.** Put the notes you need right under the camera so your eyes stay close to it.
- **Frame head and shoulders,** with a little space above your head.
- **Check the background.** Tidy one corner and sit in front of it.

## How to sound better when you record yourself

Bad sound ruins a video faster than bad light. A few fixes cost nothing:

- **Pick a small room with soft things in it.** Curtains, a sofa and a rug absorb echo.
- **Get closer to the microphone.** If you use the built-in mic, sit at arm's length, not across the room.
- **Turn off fans and notifications.** Do Not Disturb stops alerts from landing in your recording.
- **Do a 10-second test.** Record a sentence, play it back with headphones, and fix anything before the real take.
- **Use earbuds with a mic** if you have them. They are often clearer than a laptop mic.

## How to record yourself on Mac Air without running out of space

Video files are big, and many MacBook Air models have small drives. A few habits help:

- **Pick a lower quality** in QuickTime's options if the video is just for practice.
- **Record short takes** instead of one long one, and delete the bad ones straight away.
- **Save to an external drive or cloud folder** when you are done.
- **Close other apps** before recording, so the Mac does not stutter on longer clips.

## How to review your recordings faster

Recording yourself is most useful when you actually watch it back. Watching a 20-minute practice talk is slow, though, so try these:

- **Watch once with the sound off** to check body language and eye contact.
- **Listen once without watching** to catch filler words and rushed sections.
- **Write down 3 fixes,** then record again. Comparing 2 takes teaches you more than watching 1 take twice.

For spoken practice, a transcript makes the second step much faster, because you can scan for "um", repeated phrases and long rambles in seconds. ## Summary: how to record yourself on Mac

- **QuickTime movie recording** for the most control.
- **Photo Booth** for a quick clip.
- **Shift-Command-5 plus a floating QuickTime window** for your screen and your face together.
- **QuickTime audio recording or Voice Memos** for your voice only.
- **Light from the front, camera at eye level, a 10-second sound test** before every real take.

The best self-recording is the second one, so record, watch, fix 3 things and go again.
