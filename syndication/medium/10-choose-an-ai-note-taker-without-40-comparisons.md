# How to Choose an AI Note Taker Without Reading 40 Comparison Posts

*This story was written with AI assistance and edited by the Lynkk team. Lynkk makes an AI note taker, so read this with that in mind; the questions below apply whichever tool you pick.*

Search for "best AI note taker" and you'll find dozens of lists. Most rank the same eight or nine tools in a different order, and most are written by one of the companies on the list (including us).

The rankings don't help much, because the right tool depends on how you meet. A sales team on Zoom, a research team doing in-person interviews and a bilingual startup on Google Meet need different things.

So instead of another ranking, here are the six questions that actually decide it. Answer them first and the choice usually narrows to two or three tools.

## 1. Where do your meetings happen?

Start here, because it eliminates the most options.

- **One platform only?** Check what's built in first. Zoom AI Companion, Google Meet's Gemini notes and Copilot in Teams may be enough, if your plan includes them.
- **Several platforms?** You want one tool that covers all of them, or you'll end up with three archives.
- **In person too?** Many tools only work on video calls. If you have conference-room meetings, interviews or site visits, this is a hard filter.

If you're on Zoom today and wondering what else is out there, here's a comparison of [Zoom AI Companion alternatives](https://lynkk.ai/blog/zoom-ai-companion-alternatives), including tools that also cover Meet, Teams and in-person calls.

## 2. Bot or no bot?

Some note takers send a bot into the call as a participant. Others record from your own computer with no bot. Bots work for meetings you can't attend and are visible to everyone. Bot-free tools change nothing about the meeting but require you to be there and to disclose the recording yourself.

Neither is better in general. Decide which your team and your customers are comfortable with.

## 3. What languages do people actually speak?

This question gets skipped and then causes the most frustration. If everyone speaks one language, most tools are fine. If people switch languages mid-sentence, as in Hindi-English (Hinglish) or Spanish-English conversations, many tools produce a mess. Test with a real recording from your own team before you commit.

## 4. Where should the output go?

A summary that sits in the note taker's app is only half useful. Ask:

- Do action items go to your task tracker (Jira, Asana, Linear)?
- Can you get answers in your chat tool (Slack, Teams)?
- Does it update your CRM, or do you copy it there by hand?
- Can you search and ask questions across all past meetings, with links to the source?

Be careful here: "integrates with" can mean anything from a full two-way sync to a button that copies text. Ask what exactly happens.

## 5. What does free actually include?

Free plans vary enormously. Some limit the number of meetings, some limit minutes, some limit how long notes are kept, some limit AI summaries. One tool's "free" covers a light user for months; another's runs out in a week.

Compare the limit that matches how you work. If you have many short meetings, a per-meeting cap hurts. If you have few long ones, a per-minute cap hurts. Our team keeps a comparison of [free AI note taker plans](https://lynkk.ai/blog/best-free-ai-note-takers) that lays out what each one includes and what the upgrade costs.

## 6. What happens to your data?

Ask every vendor, in writing:

- Do you train AI models on our conversations?
- Which third-party providers process our audio and transcripts?
- Where is the data processed, and can we choose?
- Who inside our company can see a note, and is that logged?
- How do we export or delete everything?

If a vendor can't answer clearly, that's an answer.

## Putting it together

Once you've answered the six questions, the field narrows fast. A few examples:

- **Zoom-only sales team, English, wants CRM sync** → shortlist tools with real CRM integrations and test the sync.
- **Product team on Meet and in person, two languages** → in-person capture and code-switching support are must-haves; many popular tools drop out.
- **Solo consultant, few meetings, tight budget** → the best free plan for your meeting pattern matters more than features.

If you're replacing a specific tool, it helps to start from that tool's gaps. For example, here are [Otter.ai alternatives](https://lynkk.ai/blog/otter-ai-alternatives) compared on free limits and fit for sales, product and in-person meetings. And if you do want the full side-by-side, our [AI note taker comparison](https://lynkk.ai/blog/best-ai-note-takers) covers nine tools on free limits, paid plans, bot or bot-free, and platform support.

## Then test, don't read

The last step beats any comparison post: **take two finalists, use each for a week on real meetings, and compare the notes.** Look at how accurate the transcript is, whether the action items are right, and whether anyone on the team actually opened the summary.

That week will tell you more than forty lists.
