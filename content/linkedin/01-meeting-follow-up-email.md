# Meeting Follow-Up Email: 5 Examples That Actually Get Replies

I spend a lot of my week thinking about what happens after meetings, because that's where most of them quietly fall apart.

Everyone leaves the call feeling aligned. Then a day goes by, and the client remembers one version of the plan, your manager remembers another, and the action item nobody owned is simply gone. A short meeting follow-up email is the cheapest fix I know. Below are five examples I'd actually send, plus the simple structure behind all of them.

## The structure behind every good follow-up

Whatever the meeting was, a follow-up email that gets read (and answered) has four parts:

1. **Thanks, in one line.** Not a paragraph.
2. **What was decided.** Write decisions as decisions ("We'll go with option B"), not topics ("We discussed the options").
3. **Who does what, by when.** Every action item gets an owner and a date. No owner means nobody.
4. **One next step.** A meeting, a decision, or a question. If you need a reply, ask for it plainly.

Send it the same day if you can, and within 24 hours at the latest. After that the details blur and your email has to argue with everyone's memory.

## Example 1: After a client meeting

The goal here is alignment. Make it easy for the client to reply "Looks right" and move on.

> **Subject:** [Project]: recap and next steps from today
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for today. To make sure we're on the same page:
>
> **Agreed**
> - [Decision]
> - [Decision]
>
> **Next actions**
> - [Your name] will [task] by [date]
> - [Client name] will [task] by [date]
>
> Our next check-in is [date]. If I've got anything wrong, just reply and I'll fix it.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

## Example 2: After a sales discovery call

The best sales follow-ups repeat the prospect's problems back in their own words. It shows you listened, and it gives them an email they can forward to their boss without editing.

> **Subject:** Following up on our call, [Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the time today. Here's what I heard matters most to [their company] right now:
>
> - [Problem, in their words]
> - [Problem, in their words]
> - [Deadline or goal they mentioned]
>
> I've attached [resource] as promised. From my side, I'll [action] by [date].
>
> Does [day] at [time] work for a short demo with [stakeholder]? I'll build it around the points above.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

## Example 3: Recapping meeting notes for the team

Use this when you took the notes, or when half the team couldn't make it. Keep it scannable. People will come back to it when they forget what they agreed to.

> **Subject:** Notes and owners: [Meeting name], [Date]
>
> Hi team,
>
> Recap of today's meeting ([attendees]).
>
> **In short:** [One or two sentences on the outcome.]
>
> **Decisions**
> - [Decision]
> - [Decision]
>
> **Owners**
> - [Task]: [Name], by [date]
> - [Task]: [Name], by [date]
>
> **Still open:** [Question]. [Name] is finding out before [date].
>
> Corrections welcome, just reply here.
>
> [Your name]

## Example 4: After a networking coffee

On LinkedIn this is probably the most useful one, and the most often skipped. No action items, no pitch. Just be specific and follow through on whatever you promised.

> **Subject:** Great to meet you, [Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Really enjoyed our chat today, especially hearing about [specific thing they said].
>
> Here's the [article, intro or resource] I mentioned: [link].
>
> If I can ever help with [their area], let me know. Hope our paths cross again soon.
>
> [Your name]

Then connect with them here on LinkedIn the same day, with a one-line note that references the conversation.

## Example 5: After a job interview

Short, specific, and sent within a few hours. If you met several people, send each one their own note with a different detail.

> **Subject:** Thank you for today: [Role]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for the conversation about the [Role] role. I especially enjoyed hearing about [project or challenge they mentioned].
>
> It made me even more excited about the position. My experience with [skill] maps closely to [what they need], and I'd love to help with it.
>
> Happy to share anything else that would be useful. Looking forward to next steps.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your name]

## Subject lines that work

Your recap will get searched for later, so make the subject line say exactly what it is:

- Recap and next steps: [Meeting], [Date]
- [Project]: decisions and owners
- Following up on our call, [Name]
- Notes from today + action items
- Action needed by Friday: [Topic]

## Three mistakes I see constantly

- **Sending a transcript.** Nobody reads a play-by-play. Send outcomes.
- **"We'll look into it."** That sentence has no owner, so nothing will happen. Put a name on it.
- **Waiting until Thursday.** A recap sent three days later feels like an afterthought, and it reads like one.

## Make it faster

The writing isn't the slow part. Remembering who agreed to what is. Two things help:

**Take notes in the shape of the email.** Open three headings before the meeting starts: Decisions, Action items, Next step. Your recap is half written by the time you hang up.

**Let an AI meeting assistant do the first draft.** This is the problem we work on at [Lynkk](https://lynkk.ai). It records the conversation, online or in person, and about 30 seconds after the meeting ends you have structured notes and assigned action items. Your follow-up email turns into a quick edit. You still read it before you hit send, because your name is on it.
