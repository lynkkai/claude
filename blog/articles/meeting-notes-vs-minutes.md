# Meeting Notes vs Minutes in 2026: How US Teams Turn Rough Notes Into Approved Minutes

*By [Samarth Wagh](https://lynkk.ai/blog/authors/samarth-wagh), Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** Meeting notes and minutes differ in 3 ways: purpose, format and approval. **Meeting notes** are informal and written for yourself or your team. **Meeting minutes** are the official record of a meeting: who attended, what was decided, who owns each action, and any votes. Minutes are usually reviewed and approved at the next meeting. To turn meeting notes into minutes, keep decisions, owners and dates, and cut the discussion.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant that drafts notes and minutes.*

People search "meeting notes minutes" or "meeting minutes vs meeting notes" because the two words get used interchangeably at work. Casual team sync or [daily standup](https://lynkk.ai/blog/daily-standup-meeting)? The difference rarely matters. In a board meeting, an HOA meeting or a contract negotiation, it matters a lot: minutes can become evidence of what an organization agreed to.

Below: the real difference between meeting notes and minutes, what minutes must include, and a simple method to turn your notes into minutes in about 10 minutes, plus a worked example.

## What is the difference between meeting notes and minutes?

The difference between meeting notes and minutes comes down to 3 things: purpose, format and approval. Meeting notes help the person or team remember what happened, while meeting minutes are the organization's official record of what was decided, approved by the group and kept on file for reference.

| | Meeting notes | Meeting minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Personal or team memory | Official record |
| Audience | You or your team | The organization, auditors, members |
| Content | Ideas, discussion, reminders | Attendance, decisions, actions, votes |
| Format | Free form | Fixed structure |
| Approval | None | Usually approved at the next meeting |
| Retention | As long as useful | Often kept for years |

A simple test: if a lawyer, auditor or new board member might read it in 2 years, write minutes.

## Are meeting notes the same as minutes?

No. All minutes start as notes, but not all notes become minutes. Opinions, side ideas and half-finished thoughts can all go in notes. Minutes include only what the group did: who attended, motions made, decisions taken and actions assigned.

[Robert's Rules of Order](https://robertsrules.com/), the parliamentary guide many US boards and associations follow, puts it simply: minutes should record what was done at the meeting, not what was said by members during the debate.

## Why are meeting notes called minutes?

The word "minutes" has nothing to do with the 60-second unit of time. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minutes) notes that "minutes" here simply means "notes", and [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/minute) traces the noun to the Latin *minuta*, from *minutus*, meaning "small". Clocks never came into it.

So when someone asks for "the minutes", they mean the formal, polished record. Not the rough jottings.

## What should meeting minutes include?

Every set of meeting minutes should include 8 core items. Boards, associations and other formal bodies add motions and votes.

1. Organization name, meeting type, date, time and location
2. Who presided and who recorded the minutes
3. Attendees, absentees and whether a quorum was present
4. Approval of the previous meeting's minutes
5. Each agenda item with a short summary
6. Decisions, motions and vote results
7. Action items with an owner and a due date
8. Time of adjournment and the next meeting date

## What should meeting notes include?

Meeting notes are flexible. Useful ones still capture 4 things, namely key points, decisions, action items and open questions, and unlike minutes, they can include context, ideas and your own reminders.

Good notes make minutes easy. Already separating decisions and actions from discussion? Then converting them takes minutes, not an hour. Techniques are covered in our guide on how to take good meeting notes.

## How do you turn meeting notes into minutes?

Here is how to turn meeting notes into minutes in about 10 minutes (industry estimate), using 6 steps:

1. **Start from a template.** Use a fixed minutes format so nothing is missed.
2. **Add the facts.** Date, time, location, attendees, absentees and quorum.
3. **Keep decisions, cut debate.** Replace "we discussed pricing at length" with "The team agreed to raise the Pro price to $20 from January 1."
4. **Record motions and votes exactly.** Who moved, who seconded, and the count for, against and abstaining.
5. **List every action** with one owner and one due date.
6. **Send the draft for review**, then approve it at the next meeting.

Stick to the past tense and neutral wording, and skip direct quotes unless someone's precise phrasing matters. Boring is good here.

## Meeting notes vs minutes example

Same agenda item, two versions: notes first, then minutes.

**Notes:**

```
Pricing: Sam thinks we're too cheap. Priya worried about churn.
Long back and forth. Maybe test in one region first?
Agreed: raise Pro to $20 from Jan 1. Sam to update site.
Need churn data before Q1 review.
```

**Minutes:**

```
3. Pricing review
The team reviewed Pro plan pricing and churn risk.
Decision: Pro plan price to increase to $20 per month from January 1.
Action: Sam Lee to update the pricing page by December 15.
Action: Priya Shah to present churn data at the Q1 review.
```

Only 1 decision and 2 actions survive into the minutes. Debate, opinions and guesses? Those remain in the notes.

## How is a meeting summary different from minutes?

A meeting summary is a short, readable recap, usually 5 to 10 lines (industry estimate), sent to the team after a meeting, whereas minutes are longer and formal, with attendance, motions and votes. Many teams send a summary the same day. Minutes? Only for formal meetings.

| | Meeting summary | Meeting minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 5 to 10 lines | 1 to 3 pages |
| Tone | Conversational | Formal, past tense |
| Includes votes | Rarely | Yes, for formal bodies |
| Approved by the group | No | Usually |

## How long should meeting minutes be?

Most meeting minutes run 1 to 2 pages for a 1-hour meeting (industry estimate). Aim for 2 to 4 lines per agenda item plus the exact text of any motion or resolution. Past 3 pages? Chances are you're recording discussion that belongs in notes.

Brevity wins, provided 3 things are complete: each decision, each vote, and [each action with an owner and date](https://lynkk.ai/blog/action-items-examples-template).

## When do you need formal minutes instead of notes?

Whenever an organization makes decisions it may need to prove later, you need formal minutes. In the US, that usually includes 5 kinds of meetings:

- **Board of directors meetings**, for companies and nonprofits
- **HOA and condo association meetings**, where state law often requires records
- **LLC and corporate member meetings**, to document major decisions
- **Public bodies** such as city councils and school boards
- **Committee meetings** that make decisions on the board's behalf

Internal team meetings? Clear notes with decisions and actions usually suffice, and product teams often [turn those minutes into Jira issues](https://lynkk.ai/blog/meeting-minutes-to-jira). See our templates for HOA meeting minutes and LLC meeting minutes.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between meeting notes and minutes?

The deciding difference is the approval vote. Notes never need one, while minutes only become official once the group adopts them. Under [Robert's Rules](https://robertsrules.com/frequently-asked-questions/) (RONR, 12th edition), corrections raised at that vote go straight into the text, and the minutes are recorded as approved "as corrected" without listing the change.

### Are meeting minutes just notes?

No, and once approved they are much harder to change than notes. Robert's Rules (12th edition) requires a motion to Amend Something Previously Adopted to fix approved minutes. Even then, the secretary leaves the original wording alone and adds a margin note pointing to the correction. Your own notes carry no such rule.

### Why are they called minutes?

Because a "minute" is also a brief note or memorandum. Merriam-Webster still lists that sense, with the example of someone sending the president "a minute explaining the problem". The plural took on a narrower job: the official record of a meeting's proceedings. The 1/60-of-an-hour meaning shares the word but not the purpose.

### Who takes meeting minutes?

Usually a secretary, clerk or designated note-taker. In boards and associations the secretary drafts and signs them, but the members approve them, often by unanimous consent at the next meeting. Not the chair alone. Keep the 2 roles apart, since running a discussion and recording it at once usually means both suffer.

### Should minutes include who said what?

Usually not. Robert's Rules (RONR 12th ed., 48:2) calls summarizing debate in minutes improper, not just unnecessary. Name the member who made each main motion. The seconder's name, as Wikipedia's summary of minute-taking practice notes, is not strictly necessary. Quote someone directly only when the exact words could matter in a later dispute.

### How soon should minutes be sent after a meeting?

Send draft minutes within 1 to 2 business days, marked as a draft, then approve them at the next meeting. Some laws set an outer limit. California's [Civil Code section 4950](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_4950) requires HOA boards to make minutes, marked drafts or a summary available to members within 30 days of any board meeting other than an executive session.
