# How Recruiters and Hiring Teams Use Interview Transcription to Run Better Debriefs and Hire Faster

*Last updated: September 30, 2026*

## What is interview transcription?

Interview transcription is the process of turning an interview recording into written text, usually with a label for each speaker. For recruiters, it creates 1 shared record of every answer a candidate gave.

It can be done 3 ways: by hand, by a human interview transcription service, or by AI software that transcribes in minutes or seconds. Most hiring teams now use software, because interviews happen every day and notes are needed the same day.

## What do interview transcripts look like?

An interview transcript is a text version of the conversation, split into turns with a speaker label on each. Here is a short interview transcription example of the kind Lynkk produces, with 2 speakers.

> **Recruiter:** Tell me about a project where you had to change plans quickly.
> **Candidate:** Last year our launch date moved up by 3 weeks. I split the work into 2 streams and cut 1 feature we could ship later.
> **Recruiter:** How did the team react?
> **Candidate:** Some pushback at first. I shared the trade-offs in writing, and we agreed in 1 meeting.

Below the transcript, Lynkk adds a summary and action items, such as "Send the take-home task by Thursday", each linked back to the moment it was said.

## What do recruiters need from interviews?

Recruiters need 3 things from every interview: full attention on the candidate, accurate evidence for the debrief and a record the team can check later. Typing notes during the conversation works against all 3.

- **Attention:** follow-up questions come from listening, not from catching up on notes.
- **Evidence:** hiring managers want the candidate's words, not a paraphrase written an hour later.
- **Records:** US employers must keep hiring records, as covered below.

On a busy day with back-to-back interviews, the gap between what was said and what was written grows fast.

Hiring managers feel it too. When 3 or 4 interviewers each keep their own notes, the debrief turns into a debate about who remembers what. A shared transcript settles those questions in seconds and keeps the conversation on the candidate's answers.

## How do you transcribe an interview?

You can transcribe an interview in 4 steps with transcription software, and most of the work happens automatically.

1. **Get consent** from the candidate before you start recording.
2. **Record the interview:** online, the [Lynkk meeting bot](https://lynkk.ai/features/meeting-bot) joins for free (audio only) or the bot-free [Chrome extension](https://lynkk.ai/features/chrome-extension) records; in person, Lynkk captures the room.
3. **Let the software transcribe it** with a label for each speaker.
4. **Review and share** the transcript and notes with the hiring team.

Doing it by hand means replaying the audio and typing every turn, which takes far longer than the interview itself. That is why teams switch to transcription software for interviews once volume grows.

For clean transcripts, 3 habits help. Use a headset or a quiet room, avoid talking over the candidate, and pause for a second before each new question. In person, place the laptop between you and the candidate so both voices are captured clearly.

## What should an interview debrief template include?

A useful interview debrief template has 5 parts, and it works best when each part points to evidence in the transcript. Copy this template for your next hiring debrief.

1. **Role and stage:** the job title and interview round.
2. **Competencies scored:** each skill you assessed, with a score and 1 quote from the transcript.
3. **Strengths:** 2 or 3 points, each backed by the candidate's own words.
4. **Concerns:** open questions to test in the next round.
5. **Decision and next step:** advance, hold or decline, with an owner and a date.

Quoting the transcript keeps the debrief about what the candidate said, which helps interviewers compare candidates on the same evidence. Send the completed template to the hiring manager within 24 hours, while the interview is still fresh for everyone involved.

## Do candidates need to consent to interview recording?

In many US states, yes, and asking every candidate is the simplest rule. Under [18 U.S.C. 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511), federal law generally allows recording when 1 party consents, but some states require every party to agree.

Keep the records too. Under [29 CFR 1602.14](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XIV/part-1602/subpart-C/section-1602.14), employers must keep hiring records for 1 year from when they were made or from the personnel action, whichever is later. ## FAQ

### What is interview transcription in research?
In research, interview transcription turns recorded interviews into text so researchers can quote, code and analyze what participants said. The same 3 options apply: typing by hand, a human service or AI software. Lynkk transcribes research interviews with speaker labels in 17 languages, including interviews recorded offline in the field.

### Is an interview transcript a primary source?
In research, an interview transcript is usually treated as a primary source, because it records a participant's own words at 1 point in time. Its value depends on accuracy, so review AI transcripts against the audio for any quote you publish. Lynkk links each note back to the moment in the recording.

### How long should recruiters keep interview transcripts?
