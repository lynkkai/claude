## What is a Zoom note taker?

A Zoom note taker is an app that records a Zoom meeting, turns the audio into a transcript, and writes notes such as a summary and action items. Most AI Zoom note taker tools work in 1 of 3 ways: a bot that joins the call, a desktop app, or a browser extension.

The difference matters. A bot shows up as an extra participant. Some hosts block it. A desktop app or extension records from your own computer, so nobody else sees a new name in the call.

Zoom also has its own option, AI Companion, which is built into paid Zoom Workplace plans. It is covered below as tool number 2.

## Does Zoom have a built-in AI note taker?

Yes. Zoom AI Companion is Zoom's built-in AI note taker, included at $0 extra on paid Zoom Workplace plans. Free Zoom Basic accounts record locally only, and Zoom transcribes cloud recordings only, so free users usually need a separate app for notes.

There is also a timing catch. Zoom only transcribes a cloud recording if the transcript setting was on before you hit record. For the full steps, see [how to transcribe Zoom meetings](https://lynkk.ai/blog/how-to-transcribe-zoom-meetings).

## How do you turn on a Zoom note taker?

To turn on a Zoom note taker, you either enable Zoom AI Companion in your Zoom settings or add a third-party app, which takes about 2 minutes. How to turn on Zoom note taker features depends on which kind you use.

1. **Zoom AI Companion:** on a paid plan, turn on meeting summaries in your Zoom web settings (an admin may need to allow it first), then start it from the AI Companion button in the meeting.
2. **A meeting bot:** paste the Zoom link into the note taker, or connect your calendar so the bot joins on its own.
3. **A desktop app:** install it, and it offers to record when a Zoom call starts. In Lynkk, you click Record on the "Zoom call detected" card.
4. **A browser extension:** join Zoom in your browser and press Record on the extension.

Whichever you pick, tell everyone in the call. Every time.

## Can a Zoom note taker work without a bot joining?

Yes. A Zoom note taker can work with no bot in 2 ways: a desktop app that records from your computer, or a browser extension that records the Zoom web client. Granola and Lynkk both offer bot-free recording.

Bot-free recording helps when a host does not allow outside participants, or when a bot in the attendee list would feel awkward on a sales or interview call. Lynkk's Mac app also hides its call card and recording column from screen share on macOS 15.4 and later (best effort).

## How do you use a Zoom note taker after the call?

You use a Zoom note taker after the call by checking 5 things it should give you: a transcript with speaker names, a short summary, decisions, action items with owners, and a way to find the moment something was said. A transcript alone is not enough. You would still be doing the note-taking work.

Check 3 practical details too. How long can a single recording run? Can you share notes with someone who was not in the call? Can you search across past meetings, not just the last one?

In Lynkk, each timestamp in the Meeting Brief plays the recording from that moment, notes can be shared with a revocable link, and Ask Lynkk (Pro) answers questions across all your notes with citations.

## Is it legal to use a Zoom note taker?

Using a Zoom note taker is legal in most of the US with the consent of at least 1 participant under federal law ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)). Some states, such as California ([Penal Code § 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632)), require everyone on the call to agree.

Zoom shows a recording notice for its own recordings, but a desktop app or extension does not announce itself. Say at the start of the call that you are recording, and add it to the chat. The [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework) is a helpful checklist when your company reviews any AI note taker. *This is general information, not legal advice.*
