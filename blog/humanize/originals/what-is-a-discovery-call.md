# What Is a Discovery Call? A 2026 Guide for US Sales Teams

*By [Samarth Wagh](https://lynkk.ai/blog/authors/samarth-wagh), Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** What is a discovery call? It is the **first real sales conversation** with a prospect, usually about **30 minutes**, where you learn their **problem, goals, buying process and timeline** to decide whether there is a fit, before you pitch or demo. It follows a short agenda and ends with an agreed **next step**.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant used on sales calls.*

If you are new to sales, or moving from a non-sales role, "discovery call" can sound like jargon. It is simply the meeting where you listen more than you talk. A good discovery call saves both sides time: the buyer gets understood, and you find out whether a deal is real.

This guide explains the purpose of a discovery call, how long it should be, a simple agenda and script, 25 questions to ask, and how to follow up.

## What is a discovery call in sales?

In sales, a discovery call is the first meeting after a prospect shows interest, where the seller asks questions to understand the buyer's situation and qualify the opportunity. It comes before the demo or proposal ([Vidyard's sales glossary](https://www.vidyard.com/glossary/sales-glossary-discovery-call/) defines it the same way). [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales/what-is-a-discovery-call/) and most sales methodologies treat it as the foundation of the deal.

What a discovery call is not:

- **Not a demo.** You might show nothing at all.
- **Not a pitch.** You talk about 30% of the time or less.
- **Not an interrogation.** It is a conversation that should feel useful to the buyer.

## What is the purpose of a discovery call?

The purpose of a discovery call is to answer 4 questions: Does the prospect have a problem you can solve? Does it matter enough to act on now? Who is involved in the decision? Is there a realistic path to buying?

| Purpose | What you learn |
|---|---|
| Problem | The pain, its cause and its cost |
| Priority | Why now, and what happens if nothing changes |
| People | Decision makers, influencers, users |
| Process | Budget, timeline, steps to purchase |

Many teams use frameworks such as BANT (budget, authority, need, timeline) or MEDDIC to structure these questions. The framework matters less than covering all 4 areas.

## How long should a discovery call be?

Most discovery calls should be 30 minutes. Book 45 minutes for complex B2B deals with several stakeholders, and keep to 15 to 20 minutes for simple, transactional products.

A 30-minute discovery call agenda:

1. **Introductions and agenda (3 min):** confirm time and goals.
2. **Their situation (10 min):** current process, team, tools.
3. **Pain and impact (10 min):** what is not working and what it costs.
4. **Decision process (5 min):** who decides, budget, timeline.
5. **Next step (2 min):** agree on a specific follow-up meeting.

## What is a good discovery call script?

A discovery call script is a flexible outline, not lines to read. Use this as a starting point:

```
OPEN
"Thanks for making time, [Name]. I'd like to spend most of our
30 minutes learning about [company] and what prompted you to reach out.
At the end, if it makes sense, we can agree on a next step. Sound good?"

SITUATION
"Walk me through how your team handles [process] today."

PAIN
"What's not working about that?"
"What does that cost you, in time, money or risk?"

PRIORITY
"Why is this coming up now?"
"What happens if nothing changes in the next 6 months?"

PROCESS
"Besides you, who else would be involved in a decision like this?"
"How have you bought tools like this before?"

CLOSE
"Based on what you've shared, I think [specific value] could help.
Would it make sense to set up a demo with [stakeholder] next week?"
```

## What questions should you ask on a discovery call?

Ask open questions that start with what, how and why. Here are 25 discovery call questions grouped by purpose; pick 8 to 10 per call.

**Situation**

1. How does your team handle [process] today?
2. What tools are you using for it?
3. How many people are involved?
4. What does a typical week look like for this team?
5. What prompted you to look at this now?

**Pain and impact**

6. What is the biggest challenge with the current approach?
7. How often does that happen?
8. What does it cost you in hours, revenue or risk?
9. What have you tried so far?
10. Why did those attempts fall short?

**Goals and priority**

11. What would success look like in 6 months?
12. How would you measure it?
13. Where does this rank among your priorities this quarter?
14. What happens if you do nothing?
15. Who else cares about solving this?

**Decision process**

16. Who else will be involved in the decision?
17. What does your buying process usually look like?
18. Is there a budget set aside for this?
19. What timeline are you working toward?
20. What could stop this from moving forward?

**Fit and next steps**

21. What would you need to see to feel confident?
22. Are you evaluating other options?
23. What matters most in choosing a solution?
24. Who should join the next conversation?
25. What is the best next step from your side?

## Why are discovery calls important?

Discovery calls are important because they decide whether the rest of the sales process is worth anyone's time. A good discovery call qualifies out bad-fit deals early, gives you the buyer's own words for later proposals, and makes the demo shorter and more relevant.

They also build trust. A buyer who feels understood on the first call is far more likely to take the second one. That is why many sales teams treat discovery quality as a leading indicator of win rate.

## How is a discovery call different from a cold call?

A cold call is an unscheduled first contact to earn a conversation. A discovery call is a scheduled conversation with someone who already agreed to talk. The goals differ:

| | Cold call | Discovery call |
|---|---|---|
| Who initiates | Seller, unannounced | Both sides, scheduled |
| Goal | Earn a meeting | Understand and qualify |
| Length | 1 to 5 minutes | About 30 minutes |
| Prospect's mindset | Interrupted | Expecting the call |

## Which discovery frameworks should you know?

Three frameworks cover most sales discovery. Use them as checklists, not scripts.

- **BANT:** budget, authority, need, timeline. Simple and quick for smaller deals.
- **MEDDIC:** metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, identify pain, champion. Built for complex B2B deals with several stakeholders.
- **SPIN:** situation, problem, implication, need-payoff questions. Helps the buyer see the cost of their problem in their own words.

Salesforce's own guide uses a POWERFUL acronym for discovery questions, which covers similar ground. Whatever framework you choose, make sure your notes capture each part so the next person on the deal does not repeat the questions.

## What are common discovery call mistakes?

These 6 mistakes cost the most deals:

1. **Pitching too early.** Showing the product before understanding the problem.
2. **Asking yes or no questions.** They end conversations instead of opening them.
3. **Not quantifying the pain.** Without a cost, there is no urgency.
4. **Ignoring the decision process.** Learning about a procurement step in month 3.
5. **No agreed next step.** "I will follow up" is not a next step; a booked meeting is.
6. **Poor notes.** Losing the buyer's exact words, which are the best material for the proposal.

## What is the difference between a discovery call and a demo?

A discovery call is for listening and qualifying; a demo is for showing how your product solves the problems you uncovered. Demos built on a good discovery call are shorter and more relevant, because you show only what matters to that buyer.

| | Discovery call | Demo |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Understand and qualify | Show the solution |
| Who talks most | The buyer | The seller |
| Typical length | 30 minutes | 30 to 60 minutes |
| Output | Notes, qualification, next step | Evaluation, proposal |

## What is a discovery call outside of sales?

Outside sales, a discovery call is any first call to understand someone's needs before offering a service. Coaches, consultants, agencies, lawyers and freelancers use free discovery calls, usually 15 to 30 minutes, to see whether they are a good fit for a client. In recruiting, a discovery call can mean an early conversation to understand a role or a candidate's goals.

The structure is the same: understand the situation, the problem and the goal, then agree on a next step.

## How do you prepare for a discovery call?

Spend 10 to 15 minutes preparing. Check the prospect's website, their LinkedIn profile, any past conversations with your company, and why they booked the call. Write 3 hypotheses about their likely problems to test with your questions.

## How do you follow up after a discovery call?

Send a follow-up email the same day that restates what you heard, confirms the next step and includes anything you promised. Keep it short and specific to their situation.

## FAQ

### What is the main purpose of a discovery call?

To understand the prospect's problem, priorities, decision process and timeline, and to decide together whether there is a fit worth pursuing. Most sellers aim to talk 30% of the time or less, using open questions about the situation, the pain, the priority and the decision process.

### How long is a discovery call?

Most discovery calls last about 30 minutes. Complex B2B deals may need 45 minutes, and simple products 15 to 20 minutes. Book 30 minutes by default and end on time. Agree on a specific next step before the call ends, rather than promising to follow up later.

### Is a discovery call an interview?

Not exactly. It is a two-way conversation. The seller asks most of the questions, but the buyer should also learn something useful and decide whether to continue. A good call feels useful to the buyer, because they leave with a clearer view of their own problem and options.

### Are discovery calls free?

In sales, yes: they are part of the sales process. Coaches and consultants also usually offer free discovery calls of 15 to 30 minutes. In law, a first consultation may be free or paid depending on the firm, so check before booking.

### What happens after a discovery call?

The seller sends a recap and, if there is a fit, books the next step, often a demo with additional stakeholders or a proposal review. Send the recap the same day, using the prospect's own words for their goals, challenges and timeline.

### What is another name for a discovery call?

It is also called a discovery meeting, qualification call, needs analysis call or, for consultants and coaches, a consultation call. Whatever the name, the structure is the same: understand the situation, the problem and the goal, then agree on a next step.
