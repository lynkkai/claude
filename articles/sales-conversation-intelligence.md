## What is conversation intelligence?

Conversation intelligence is software that captures conversations, turns speech into text and pulls out what matters: decisions, objections, next steps and owners. In sales, it replaces 2 manual jobs: taking notes during calls and writing up next steps afterward.

So, what is conversation intelligence in plain terms? It is a memory for every customer conversation that your whole team can search. Instead of relying on 1 rep's notes, the team works from what the customer actually said.

## What is conversation intelligence software?

Conversation intelligence software is the tool that does this work, usually in 4 steps: record, transcribe, analyze and act. The last step is the one that saves reps time, because it moves insights into a task list, a ticket or a follow-up email.

| Step | What the software does |
|---|---|
| Record | Joins or captures the call, online or in person |
| Transcribe | Turns speech into text with speaker labels |
| Analyze | Finds decisions, objections, owners and next steps |
| Act | Creates tasks, assigns owners and drafts follow-ups |

Basic sales call recording software stops at the first 2 steps. A full conversation intelligence platform handles all 4.

## What do sales teams need from their calls?

Sales teams need 3 things from every call: context before it, focus during it and accurate follow-up after it. Missing any 1 of them slows deals down or loses them.

- **Before:** what was promised last time, who the stakeholders are and what is still open.
- **During:** answers to "what did we agree on pricing?" without scrolling through old notes.
- **After:** next steps with owners and dates, and a follow-up sent the same day.

Most reps handle these by hand. That means notes typed while trying to listen, and next steps written hours later from memory. Managers then coach from partial notes, and handoffs to customer success lose details the buyer shared early in the deal.

## What should a sales call follow-up checklist include?

A good sales call follow-up checklist has 6 items, and conversation intelligence can fill most of them automatically. Use this after every discovery or demo call.

1. **Summary in 3 lines:** the customer's problem, their goal and the outcome of the call.
2. **Decision makers:** names and roles mentioned.
3. **Next steps:** each with 1 owner and a date.
4. **Objections:** what worried the customer, in their words.
5. **Deal details:** stage, timing and next meeting, ready to copy into your sales tools.
6. **Follow-up message:** sent the same day, with the agreed next steps.

Lynkk writes items 1, 2 and 3 for you and links each to the moment in the call. Your rep checks them and sends the follow-up.

Here is an example of the kind of output a rep reviews after a 30-minute discovery call:

> **Summary:** Operations lead wants to cut onboarding time for 40 new hires a quarter. Budget review is in 2 weeks.
> **Next steps:** Send security overview (owner: rep, due Friday). Book demo with IT lead (owner: rep, due next Tuesday).
> **Objection:** Worried about setup time for a small IT team. Rep offered a guided setup call.
> **Next meeting:** Demo with the IT lead, next Tuesday.

## Do you need consent to record sales calls?

Yes, in many cases, and the rule depends on where each person on the call is located. Under [18 U.S.C. 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511), federal law generally allows recording when 1 party consents.

States can be stricter. [California Penal Code 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632) requires the consent of all parties to a confidential conversation, which matters when your buyer is in California.

Announce the recording at the start of every call. Lynkk's meeting bot joins as a visible participant, and the [Lynkk security page](https://lynkk.ai/security) covers encryption and GDPR. This is not legal advice.
