# Free Meeting Notes Template, Plus 9 Templates for Standups, 1:1s, Sales Calls and More

*Last updated: October 1, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A good meeting notes template has 7 parts: title and attendees, a 2 line summary, key takeaways, topics, action items with an owner and due date, decisions with the reason behind them, and open questions. Copy the free meeting notes template below into Word, Google Docs or Notion, or let Lynkk fill in the same structure automatically from a recording of the meeting.

## What should a meeting notes template include?

A meeting notes template should include 7 sections, in this order, so anyone who missed the meeting can catch up in under 2 minutes. Each one answers a question people ask after every meeting.

| Section | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| Title, date, attendees | Which meeting was this, and who was there? |
| Summary | What happened, in 2 lines? |
| Key takeaways | What should I remember? |
| Topics | What did we cover? |
| Action items | Who does what, by when? |
| Decisions | What did we agree, and why? |
| Open questions | What is still unresolved? |

Most free templates stop at "notes" and "action items". The 2 sections people regret leaving out are **decisions with the reason** and **open questions**, because those are what get argued about a week later.

## Free meeting notes template you can copy

This free meeting notes template follows the 7 sections above. Copy the blank version, then look at the sample to see how much detail each section needs.

### The blank template

Copy these 7 headings into any doc. Delete a section only if it is genuinely empty, not because nobody wrote it down.

- **Meeting:** [name] · **Date:** [date] · **Attendees:** [names]
- **Summary:** [2 lines: what the meeting was for and what came out of it]
- **Key takeaways:** [3 to 5 bullets]
- **Topics:** [list of what was discussed]
- **Action items:** [owner] to [task] by [date], priority [high, medium or low]
- **Decisions:** [decision], because [reason]
- **Open questions:** [question], owner to follow up: [name]

### A sample meeting notes template, filled in

Here is a sample meeting notes template from a 30 minute product sync, so you can see the level of detail that works.

- **Meeting:** Onboarding sync · **Date:** Thursday, October 1 · **Attendees:** Priya, Rahul, Sam
- **Summary:** Reviewed onboarding drop-off. Agreed to cut onboarding from 5 steps to 3 for the November release.
- **Key takeaways:** 40% of new users stop at step 3; the billing step causes most support tickets.
- **Topics:** drop-off data, billing step, release timing.
- **Action items:** Priya to share a 3 step mockup by Tuesday (high). Rahul to estimate engineering effort by Thursday (medium).
- **Decisions:** Move billing after the first project is created, because most drop-off happens at billing.
- **Open questions:** Do we need legal review for the new billing flow? Sam to ask by Friday.

## How do you structure meeting notes so people read them?

Structure meeting notes so the most useful lines come first: the summary at the top, then action items, then everything else. People skim the first 3 lines and stop, so put what they must do where they will see it.

Three habits make notes easier to use:

1. **Write action items as sentences with a verb, an owner and a date.** "Priya to share the mockup by Tuesday" beats "mockup".
2. **Record the reason for each decision.** The reason is what stops the same debate next month.
3. **Send the notes within 1 hour.** After a day, people remember the meeting differently.

## Which meeting notes template is best for each type of meeting?

The best meeting notes template depends on which of 9 common meeting types you run: a standup needs blockers, a sales call needs next steps, and a board meeting needs motions and votes. Use the base template above, then add the 2 or 3 lines that matter for each type.

### 1. General meeting

The general template is the 7 section base above with 1 addition: a line for **next meeting date**. It works for most team syncs, project check-ins and cross-team reviews.

### 2. Daily standup

A standup template needs 3 questions per person and 1 list of blockers, and nothing else. Keep it short enough to fill in during a 15 minute standup.

- **Per person:** done yesterday, doing today, blocked by
- **Blockers:** [blocker], owner to unblock: [name]
- **Action items:** owner, task, due today or tomorrow

See how teams handle this in [standup notes with action items, blockers and decisions](https://lynkk.ai/use-cases/team-standups).

### 3. Product review

A product review template adds 3 fields to the base: what was shown, the feedback by person, and the go or no-go call. It also works as a project management meeting notes template for milestone reviews.

- **Shown:** [feature, build or mockup]
- **Feedback:** [name]: [feedback]
- **Call:** ship, change or stop, because [reason]

### 4. Engineering design review

An engineering design template records 4 things: the proposal, the options considered, the risks, and the decision with its reason. That last line saves hours when someone asks "why did we build it this way?"

- **Proposal:** [link or 1 line]
- **Options considered:** [option A, option B]
- **Risks:** [risk], mitigation: [plan]
- **Decision:** [option], because [reason]

### 5. Sales call

A sales call template needs 4 lines the base does not have: the customer's goal, their objections, the budget and timeline, and agreed next steps with dates. More on this in [AI notes for sales calls](https://lynkk.ai/use-cases/sales-calls).

- **Goal:** what the customer wants to achieve
- **Objections:** [objection], our answer: [answer]
- **Budget and timeline:** [what they said]
- **Next steps:** [who] to [do what] by [date]

### 6. Customer interview

A customer interview template focuses on 3 things: the problem in the customer's own words, how they solve it today, and direct quotes. Quotes with timestamps are what product teams use most later. See [user interview transcription with speaker labels](https://lynkk.ai/use-cases/user-interviews).

### 7. One-on-one (1:1)

A 1:1 template has 4 parts: wins since last time, challenges, feedback in both directions, and commitments from each person. Carry open commitments into the next 1:1 so nothing quietly disappears.

### 8. Board meeting

A board meeting template adds 3 formal fields: whether a quorum was present, each motion with who moved and seconded it, and the vote result. Boards of US tax-exempt organizations should keep these records carefully: the IRS asks on Form 990 whether the governing body's meetings were documented at the time ([IRS Instructions for Form 990](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990)).

### 9. Brainstorm

A brainstorm template captures 3 lists: every idea raised, the shortlist, and who will explore each shortlisted idea next. Write ideas down without judging them first; sort them at the end.

## Are meeting notes the same as meeting minutes?

No, they differ in 2 ways: meeting notes are informal and written for the team, while meeting minutes are a formal record, often required for boards and committees. Notes focus on actions and decisions; minutes also record motions, votes and attendance in a set format.

For most internal meetings, notes are enough. Use formal minutes for board meetings, shareholder meetings and anything with legal or governance weight.
