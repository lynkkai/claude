# Meeting Agenda Template: 10 Free Agenda Forms and Examples for Every Kind of Meeting

*Last updated: October 13, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A good meeting agenda template has 6 parts: the goal, attendees, time-boxed topics, an owner for each topic, the outcome you want from each one, and a slot for follow-up. Copy the template below, adapt 1 of the 10 examples for your meeting, and send it 24 hours ahead so people arrive prepared.

## What is a meeting agenda template?

A meeting agenda template is a reusable outline that lists what a meeting will cover, in what order, for how long, and who leads each part. Most teams use 1 template and adjust it per meeting, so every agenda has the same shape and nobody has to start from a blank page.

People search for meeting agenda forms, layouts and templates, but they all solve the same problem. Without an agenda, the loudest topic takes the time. With one, the meeting has a finish line.

The best templates do more than list topics. Each item says what decision or output it should produce, which is what turns a talk into a meeting.

## What should a meeting agenda include?

A useful agenda includes 6 parts, and it should fit on 1 screen. Skip any part that does not apply, but never skip the goal.

- **Goal:** the 1 outcome the meeting must produce. "Agree the Q4 launch date" is a goal. "Discuss Q4" is not.
- **Attendees:** who is coming and what each person is there for.
- **Topics with times:** each item time-boxed, such as "10 min".
- **Owner per topic:** the person who leads that item and comes prepared.
- **Outcome per topic:** decide, inform, or get input.
- **Follow-up:** who sends notes and action items, and by when.

Add a "last time" line at the top for recurring meetings. Two or three open tasks from the previous session save 10 minutes of "where were we?"

## Free meeting agenda template you can copy

This meeting agenda template works in any doc, calendar invite or meeting agenda form. Fill in the header, then 1 row per topic.

**Meeting:** [name] · **Date and time:** [date, start to end] · **Link or room:** [link]

**Goal:** [the 1 outcome you want by the end]

**Attendees:** [name, role] · [name, role] · [name, role]

**Last time:** [2 or 3 decisions or open tasks from the previous meeting]

| Time | Topic | Owner | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 min | Check-in and last time's open tasks | Organizer | Status update |
| 15 min | [Main topic] | [Name] | Decide |
| 10 min | [Second topic] | [Name] | Get input |
| 5 min | [Third topic] | [Name] | Inform |
| 5 min | Recap: decisions and action items | Organizer | Confirm owners and dates |

**Follow-up:** [who sends notes, and by when]

The last row matters most. Five minutes to read back decisions and tasks is what makes the next meeting shorter.

## How to write a meeting agenda in 6 steps

If you searched "how to write meeting agenda", here is the short version: start from the goal, not the topics, and give every item an owner and a time. The full routine takes about 10 minutes.

1. **Write the goal first.** One sentence. If you cannot write it, you may not need the meeting.
2. **List the topics that serve the goal.** Cut anything that does not.
3. **Put the hardest decision first,** while people are fresh.
4. **Time-box each topic** and add up the total. Trim until it fits.
5. **Name an owner per topic** and tell them in advance.
6. **Send it 24 hours before,** with any reading attached.

Leave 5 minutes of slack in a 30-minute meeting. Agendas packed to the minute always run over.

## What are good meeting agenda examples?

Here are 10 meeting agenda examples you can adapt. Each one shows typical timings for a 30 to 60 minute meeting; change the minutes to fit your calendar.

### 1. Weekly team meeting agenda (30 minutes)

A team meeting agenda should spend most of its 30 minutes on blockers and decisions, not status updates people could read.

- **5 min:** Wins and last week's open tasks
- **10 min:** Blockers, 1 per person
- **10 min:** One decision the team needs to make
- **5 min:** Recap of owners and dates

### 2. One-on-one meeting agenda (30 minutes)

A 1:1 belongs to the report, not the manager, so the report fills in the first 2 items.

- **10 min:** What is on your mind
- **10 min:** Progress on goals and anything blocked
- **5 min:** Feedback, both ways
- **5 min:** Next steps

### 3. Project kickoff meeting agenda (60 minutes)

A kickoff agenda sets 4 things in 60 minutes: scope, roles, timeline and how the team will communicate.

- **10 min:** Goals and success measures
- **15 min:** Scope, including what is out of scope
- **15 min:** Roles and owners
- **10 min:** Timeline and milestones
- **10 min:** Communication plan and next steps

### 4. Client meeting agenda (45 minutes)

A client agenda opens with their 2 or 3 priorities, then your updates, then decisions.

- **5 min:** Their priorities since the last call
- **15 min:** Progress against what you promised
- **15 min:** Open questions and decisions
- **10 min:** Next steps, with dates on both sides

### 5. Sales call agenda (30 minutes)

A discovery or demo call works best with 3 parts: their problem, your fit, and an agreed next step.

- **10 min:** Their goals and current process
- **15 min:** How your product fits, using their examples
- **5 min:** Next step and who else should join

### 6. Sprint planning meeting agenda (60 minutes)

Sprint planning needs 3 inputs before the meeting: a ranked backlog, the team's capacity and last sprint's carry-over.

- **10 min:** Sprint goal
- **35 min:** Pick and size backlog items
- **10 min:** Capacity check
- **5 min:** Confirm the commitment

### 7. Retrospective meeting agenda (45 minutes)

A retro agenda has 4 parts, and the last one, picking 1 or 2 changes, is the one teams skip.

- **10 min:** What went well
- **10 min:** What did not
- **15 min:** Root causes for the top 2 issues
- **10 min:** 1 or 2 changes, each with an owner

### 8. Daily standup agenda (15 minutes)

A standup should take 15 minutes or less, with 3 questions per person and problem-solving moved to after the call.

- **10 min:** Yesterday, today, blockers (1 minute each)
- **5 min:** Blockers that need a follow-up meeting

For more on running standups, see [standup notes with action items, blockers and decisions](https://lynkk.ai/use-cases/team-standups).

### 9. All-hands meeting agenda (45 minutes)

An all-hands agenda balances 3 things: company news, recognition and open questions from the team.

- **10 min:** Company update and key numbers
- **10 min:** 1 or 2 team spotlights
- **15 min:** Questions collected in advance
- **10 min:** Live questions

### 10. Brainstorming meeting agenda (45 minutes)

A brainstorm agenda separates 2 phases that kill each other when mixed: generating ideas and judging them.

- **5 min:** The problem, in 1 sentence
- **15 min:** Ideas, no criticism
- **15 min:** Group and vote
- **10 min:** Pick the top 2 and assign owners

## How do you make a meeting agenda template in Word or Google Docs?

To make a meeting agenda template in Word or Google Docs, build it once with real headings and a 4-column table, then save it as a template. It takes about 5 minutes.

1. **Add a heading** for the meeting name, then a line for date, time and link.
2. **Insert a table** with 4 columns: Time, Topic, Owner and Outcome.
3. **Use the built-in heading styles,** not bold text, so screen readers can navigate the document. [Section508.gov](https://www.section508.gov/) has guidance on accessible documents.
4. **Save it as a template.** In Word, save as a template file. In Google Docs, keep a master copy and use "Make a copy" for each meeting.

A meeting agenda template in Word is easy to attach to an invite. A Google Docs version is easier to edit together before the meeting starts.

## How long should a meeting agenda be?

A meeting agenda should usually have 3 to 5 topics and fit on 1 screen. A 30-minute meeting can handle about 3 real topics; a 60-minute meeting about 5.

If your agenda has 8 or more items, split it into 2 meetings, or move status updates into a written note people read beforehand. Long agendas are a sign the meeting is trying to do too many jobs.

## Should you send the agenda before the meeting?

Yes. Send the agenda at least 24 hours before the meeting, so attendees can prepare, add a topic or decline if they are not needed. For a board or client meeting, send it 3 to 7 days ahead with any reading attached.

Ask attendees to add topics by a set time, then lock the agenda. Last-minute additions are how a 30-minute meeting becomes 60.

## FAQ

### What is the best format for a meeting agenda?
The best format is a short header (meeting name, date, goal, attendees) followed by a 4-column table: time, topic, owner and outcome. It fits on 1 screen, shows who leads each item, and makes clear whether each topic needs a decision, input or just an update. End with a 5-minute recap row.

### How many items should be on a meeting agenda?
Most agendas should have 3 to 5 items. A 30-minute meeting fits about 3 real topics and a 60-minute meeting about 5. If you have 8 or more, split the meeting in 2 or move status updates into a written note people read beforehand, so the meeting time goes to decisions.

### Who should write the meeting agenda?
The meeting organizer writes the agenda, but attendees should be able to add topics by a set time, such as 24 hours before. For a 1:1, the report usually adds most items. For recurring meetings, keep 1 template and update it each week instead of starting over.

### What is the difference between an agenda and meeting minutes?
An agenda is the plan you send 24 hours before the meeting: topics, times and owners. Minutes are the record written after it: attendance, decisions and action items. The easiest way to write good minutes is to follow the agenda's order and add the decision and tasks under each topic.

### Can I use a meeting agenda template in Word or Google Docs?
Yes. Build the template once with a heading, a date line and a 4-column table, then save it as a Word template or keep a master Google Doc and make a copy for each meeting. Use the built-in heading styles so the agenda is easy to navigate with a screen reader.
