# Spotter: product video script

For https://lynkk.ai/features/spotter

Target length: 70 seconds (hero cut). A 30-second social cutdown is at the bottom.
Format: 16:9 hero, 1:1 and 9:16 crops from the same footage.
Tone: calm, confident, no hype. Spotter is help you call for, not a bot talking over you.

Everything shown must be real app footage from the early build. Any shortcut on screen
has to match the real default binding.

---

## Shot list

### 1. Cold open: the keyboard (0:00 to 0:05)

**Visual:** Extreme close-up, macro, shallow depth of field. A MacBook keyboard lit
from the side, screen glow spilling across the keys. We are tight on the bottom-left
cluster so the ⌘ key fills most of the frame. A thumb rests on it. Nothing moves.

**Audio:** Room tone only. Faint, muffled voice from a call somewhere off screen,
like you are hearing it through the laptop speaker.

**On-screen text:** none.

**Voiceover:** none. Let it sit.

---

### 2. The press (0:05 to 0:08)

**Visual:** The thumb presses ⌘ and the other hand hits Return. Single clean key
travel, shot at 120fps and played slightly slow so you feel the click. On the press,
pull focus from the keys up to the screen behind.

**Audio:** One crisp key click. A single soft tone, the Spotter chime, lands on the
keypress.

**On-screen text:** `⌘↩` appears small, bottom center, held for one second.

**Voiceover:** "One key."

---

### 3. Spotter appears on the call (0:08 to 0:18)

**Visual:** Rack focus completes onto the laptop screen. A live call is running,
gallery view, a real face mid-sentence. The Spotter bar slides down from the top of
the screen, over the call, and an answer writes itself into it line by line.

The question on the call is the kind nobody has ready: a pricing objection, or a
"how does this handle our data?" The answer in the bar is specific and short.

Keep the camera on the physical laptop for the first beat, then cut to a clean screen
recording for the answer so the text is readable.

**On-screen text:** none. The bar is the text.

**Voiceover:** "Spotter reads the front window and hears the call. Ask for help with
the moment you are in, and the answer is on your screen before the pause gets
awkward."

---

### 4. Three keys (0:18 to 0:33)

**Visual:** Three quick screen-recording beats, cut tight, each with the shortcut
stamped in the lower third as it is pressed. Match cut back to the macro keyboard for
each press, then straight to the result.

1. `⌘↩` Get help with this moment. Also: type a question, press Return.
2. `⌘⇧\` Start or stop listening. Show the mic label "Me" and the system audio label
   "Them" lighting up as both sides are heard.
3. `⌘\` Show or hide the bar. The bar snaps away, the call is untouched underneath.

**Voiceover:** "Three keys do the work. Ask. Listen. Hide. Your microphone is you,
your Mac's sound is them, and nothing is heard until you press Listen."

---

### 5. Modes (0:33 to 0:44)

**Visual:** The mode picker opens. Cursor moves down the list: General, Meeting,
Sales, Support, Lecture, Interview prep, Mock interview. Land on Sales, then cut to a
custom mode with the user's own instructions and notes pasted in.

Then one short payoff beat: the same question from shot 3, answered in Sales mode and
answered in Support mode, side by side, visibly different.

**On-screen text:** "Seven modes, and your own."

**Voiceover:** "Tell it what kind of help you want. Seven modes, or write your own
instructions and drop in your notes."

---

### 6. Visible, and private (0:44 to 0:58)

**Visual:** Pull back to a two-up: the presenter's screen on the left, the attendee's
view of the screen share on the right. Spotter is in both. No hiding, no trick.

Then a short beat on the session: the ask happens, the bar clears, nothing is left in
the list of notes. Unless you choose to keep it, and we show that one click too.

**On-screen text:** "Out in the open. Nothing kept."

**Voiceover:** "Spotter shows up in every screen share and recording, on purpose.
It reads your screen only when you ask. Your asks are not saved unless you keep the
session as a note."

---

### 7. One call, then the next (0:58 to 1:05)

**Visual:** The call ends. Within seconds, the Lynkk notes view fills in: structured
notes, action items, the open question from the call still flagged. Then the next
meeting opens on the calendar and the context panel is already there.

**Voiceover:** "And it is one call in one place. What came up today is what Spotter
has ready for you tomorrow."

---

### 8. End card (1:05 to 1:10)

**Visual:** Black. Lynkk logo. Below it, the ⌘↩ glyph, then the URL.

**On-screen text:**
```
Spotter
Live AI help on your calls.

Early access on Lynkk Pro, Mac app.
Apple silicon, macOS 14.2 or later.

lynkk.ai/features/spotter
```

**Voiceover:** "Spotter. In early access now, in the Lynkk Mac app."

Word count: 132. At a calm read that is roughly 55 seconds of speech inside a
70-second cut, which leaves the silence the open and the keypress need.

## Production notes

- The cold open only works silent. Resist the urge to put music under the first five
  seconds. The first sound in the film should be the key click.
- Shoot the macro keyboard and the screen recording separately, then match cut. Trying
  to get both sharp in one take will cost a day and look worse.
- Use a real call with a real second person. A fake call reads as fake within a second,
  and the whole claim here is that Spotter is in the room with you.
- Every answer shown on screen needs to be a real Spotter output. If an answer is too
  long to read in the cut, change the question, not the answer.
- Label the footage as the early build wherever a frame could be mistaken for the
  shipped product.
- Captions on by default for every crop.
- Shortcuts to keep accurate on screen: ⌘↩ ask, ⌘⇧\ listen, ⌘\ show or hide,
  ⌘R new chat, ⌘← and ⌘→ move the bar, ⌘⇧↑ and ⌘⇧↓ scroll the answer. These are the
  defaults and can be changed in Settings, so if the demo machine has custom bindings,
  reset it before shooting.
