# The meeting ended. The work didn't.

*A 15-minute follow-up system for the hour after every call, and the three places action items go to die.*

---

Every meeting ends the same way.

"Great, so next steps: I'll send over the doc, you'll check with your team, and let's find time next week." Everyone nods. Everyone means it. The call ends at 10:58 and the next one starts at 11:00.

By Friday, the doc wasn't sent, nobody checked with the team, and "next week" became a calendar ping that nobody answered.

Nothing went wrong *in* the meeting. Everything went wrong in the 30 minutes after it, which no one had.

## The follow-up gap is where meetings lose their value

A meeting is an investment: an hour of salary times the number of people in the room. The return only shows up if something changes because of it.

That return is collapsing. [Atlassian's survey of 5,000 knowledge workers](https://fortune.com/2024/03/21/meetings-productivity-ineffective-atlassian-report) found meetings were ineffective 72% of the time, and 78% of people said the sheer number of meetings made it hard to get their actual work done.

That's the trap: more meetings means less time to act on meetings, which means more meetings to re-discuss what didn't happen.

In sales, it shows up directly in the numbers. [Salesforce's State of Sales research](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/sales-ai-statistics-2024/) found reps spend 70% of their time on non-selling work like admin and meeting prep. Much of that is the follow-up: logging the call, updating fields, writing the recap, chasing the next step.

## Where action items go to die

Action items rarely fail because people are lazy. They fail in three specific places.

### 1. They were never written down as tasks

"We should look into SSO" is a topic, not a task. It has no owner and no date. If the notes say "discussed SSO," that item is already dead.

### 2. They were written down in the wrong place

The action item lives in a meeting doc. The person's work lives in Jira, the CRM, or their inbox. Anything that requires someone to go *find* it will be forgotten. A task has to land where the owner already works.

### 3. Nobody confirmed them while everyone was still there

Two people leave the same call with two different versions of who's doing what. Nobody finds out until the deadline passes.

> An action item without an owner, a date, and a home is just a nice memory of a meeting.

## The 15-minute follow-up routine

If you do nothing else from this post, block 15 minutes after any meeting that matters. Literally put it on the calendar, attached to the meeting. It starts one step before the call ends.

### Before you hang up: play it back

In the last two minutes: "Let me confirm: you're doing X by Tuesday, I'm doing Y by Thursday. Right?" This kills failure point 3 for free.

### Minutes 0-5: Turn topics into tasks

Go through your notes and rewrite every next step in this shape:

**[Verb] [specific thing] / owner: [name] / due: [date]**

"Look into SSO" becomes "Send SSO requirements doc to their IT lead / owner: Priya / due: Wed." If you can't fill in all three parts, it isn't a task yet. Either fix it or drop it.

### Minutes 5-10: Put each task where its owner works

Tickets go into Jira. Deal changes go into the CRM. Your own tasks go into whatever you actually check. Don't link to the meeting notes and hope. Copy the task to its home.

### Minutes 10-15: Send the follow-up email

Use the template below. Send it within the hour, while the meeting is still fresh for everyone.

## The follow-up email template

```
Subject: [Company] / [topic]: recap and next steps

Hi all, thanks for the time today. Quick recap so we're aligned:

Decided:
- [decision 1]
- [decision 2]

Next steps:
- [Task] / [owner] / [date]
- [Task] / [owner] / [date]
- [Task] / [owner] / [date]

Open questions:
- [anything unresolved, and who will answer it]

Next meeting: [date] to [the one goal for that call].

If I missed or misheard anything, reply here and I'll update.
```

Two notes on why it's built this way. Separating **Decided** from **Next steps** stops anyone from relitigating settled points next week. And the last line gives people an easy, low-cost way to correct you, which is far better than finding out at the next meeting.

## The honest problem with this system

It works. It also costs 15 minutes per meeting.

If you have six meetings a day, that's 90 minutes of follow-up, every day, on top of the meetings themselves. Most people try this for a week, then start skipping it for the "less important" calls, then for all of them.

That's not a willpower problem. It's a signal that this is exactly the kind of work that shouldn't be done by hand.
