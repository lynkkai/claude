# How to Write a Meeting Brief in 5 Minutes (With a Template You Can Copy)

## A one-page meeting brief template, 3 examples, and a short checklist for meeting preparation

A meeting brief is a one-page note you read, or send, before a meeting. It says who is coming, what you want to achieve, what was decided last time, which tasks are still open, and what is on the agenda. A good one takes about 5 minutes to write and saves the first 10 minutes of the meeting.

This guide gives you the six parts every meeting brief needs, a template you can paste into any doc, three short examples for different meetings, and a checklist you can run in the minutes before you join.

## Why a meeting brief beats an agenda alone

An agenda tells people what you will talk about. A meeting brief tells them where things stand. That difference matters most in recurring meetings, where the real question is never "what is on the list?" but "what happened since last time?"

Without a brief, the opening of most meetings goes the same way. Someone asks what was decided last week. Someone else remembers it differently. A task turns out to have stalled, and nobody knew. A brief puts all of that on one page before anyone joins.

## The 6 parts of a meeting brief

Keep each part short. If the brief runs past one page, the meeting probably needs splitting.

- **Who:** names, roles and, for outside meetings, company. One line each.
- **Goal:** the one outcome you want by the end. "Agree the launch date" is a goal. "Discuss the launch" is not.
- **Last time:** two or three decisions from the previous meeting, with the date.
- **Open tasks:** what is still owed, by whom, and when it was due.
- **Open questions:** anything unresolved that this meeting should answer.
- **Agenda:** three to five topics, most important first.

The "last time" and "open tasks" parts are the ones people skip, and they are the ones that save the most time.

## A meeting brief template you can copy

Paste this meeting brief template into Google Docs, Word, Notion or your calendar invite, and fill in the brackets.

> **Meeting brief: [meeting name]**
>
> Date and time: [date, time, link or room]
>
> **Who:** [name, role] · [name, role] · [name, role]
>
> **Goal:** [the one outcome you want by the end]
>
> **Last time ([date]):**
> • [Decision 1]
> • [Decision 2]
>
> **Open tasks:**
> • [Owner]: [task], due [date], [done / in progress / late]
> • [Owner]: [task], due [date], [done / in progress / late]
>
> **Open questions:**
> • [Question this meeting should answer]
>
> **Agenda:**
> 1. [Most important topic]
> 2. [Next topic]
> 3. [Next topic]
>
> **After the meeting:** [who sends notes, and by when]

If you run the meeting, send the brief to attendees the day before. If you are attending, keep it for yourself.

## 3 meeting brief examples

Here is how the brief changes for three common meetings. Only the parts that differ are shown.

**1. A weekly team meeting**

> **Goal:** Unblock the release and confirm owners for next week.
> **Last time:** Release moved to Thursday; QA owns the regression pass.
> **Open tasks:** Lee: fix the login bug, due Monday, in progress.
> **Open questions:** Do we need a second reviewer for the payments change?

**2. A client or sales meeting**

> **Who:** Dana (VP Operations), Leo (IT lead) at the client.
> **Goal:** Agree the pilot start date.
> **Last time:** Pilot scope set to 2 teams. We promised the security answers.
> **Open tasks:** Us: send security questionnaire answers, due Friday, done.
> **Open questions:** Who owns the budget for year 2?

**3. A leadership update**

> **Goal:** Get a decision on hiring two engineers this quarter.
> **Last time:** Headcount request deferred pending Q3 numbers.
> **Open tasks:** Finance: share Q3 actuals, due October 10, done.
> **Agenda:** 1. Q3 numbers. 2. Hiring request. 3. Risks if we wait.

Notice that each brief leads with the goal and the history. The agenda comes last because, once the history is clear, the agenda is usually obvious.

## Checklist for meeting preparation (10 minutes)

Use this checklist for meeting preparation in the 10 minutes before you join. Pre meeting preparation does not need to be long; it needs to be specific.

1. **Read the invite.** Check who is coming and why.
2. **Find the last notes with these people.** Search by person or company, not by meeting title.
3. **List the open tasks.** Note what is done and what is late.
4. **Pull two or three past decisions** this meeting depends on.
5. **Write one goal.** What must be true at the end?
6. **Write three to five agenda points,** most important first.
7. **Decide the follow-up:** who takes notes, who sends them, by when.

Steps 2 to 4 are the slow ones. They are slow because notes from past meetings are scattered across documents, chat threads and notebooks. Fix the note-taking, and the brief almost writes itself.

## Who should write the meeting brief?

The person running the meeting writes the brief and shares it. That is usually the organizer, the account owner for a client call, or the manager for a team meeting.

Attendees benefit from a private version too. Even if the organizer sends nothing, a two-minute brief for yourself (who, your goal, what you still owe) means you walk in knowing what you need to say and what you need to ask. For recurring meetings, rotate the job so one person does not carry it every week.

## Common meeting brief mistakes

- **Writing a summary of everything.** A brief is not minutes. Keep only what this meeting needs.
- **No goal.** If you cannot write one sentence for the goal, ask whether the meeting is needed.
- **Old information.** A brief with last month's task status causes more confusion than none.
- **Too long.** One page, or one screen. Cut anything a reader would skim past.

## How to write meeting briefs faster

The fastest brief is the one where the history is already written down. Three habits help:

- **Keep all meeting notes in one searchable place,** so step 2 of the checklist takes seconds.
- **End every meeting by reading the tasks aloud,** so the next brief's "open tasks" list is accurate.
- **Send notes within 24 hours,** so they are fresh when the next brief needs them.

## Summary

- A meeting brief is one page: who, goal, last time, open tasks, open questions, agenda.
- The history parts matter more than the agenda.
- Use the template, adjust it per meeting type, and send it the day before if you run the meeting.
