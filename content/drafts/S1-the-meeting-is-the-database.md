# The Meeting Is the Database

*By [name], [role] at Lynkk*

---

Every tool in the AI meeting category produces the same object: a summary of one call.

You finish a conversation, and thirty seconds later there is a tidy artefact with bullet points and
action items. It is genuinely useful, and it is the wrong unit of work.

I have come to think this is why the whole category plateaus at roughly the same place. The summary
is a local answer to a question nobody actually has. Nobody wakes up wanting a recap of Tuesday's
call. What they want to know is whether this deal is real, what this customer has been promised,
and what has changed since the last time anyone spoke to them.

Those are not questions about a meeting. They are questions about a relationship, and the answer
lives across fifteen conversations, not in one.

## Notes are a filing system for a problem that is not filing

The mental model behind meeting notes is the minute book. Something was discussed, so it must be
recorded, so there is a record. That model comes from governance, where the point genuinely is the
record: the board resolved X on date Y, and the document exists so it can be referred back to.

Sales conversations are not like that. Nobody refers back to a single sales call. They refer back to
a customer. And the shape of the useful answer is almost never "what happened in this meeting", it
is "how did we get here".

How has their budget moved? When did IT first come up? Which of the four people on their side has
actually said anything in the last month? What exactly did we promise, and when?

Every one of those requires reading across calls, in order, and noticing change. A folder of
beautifully summarised individual meetings is close to useless for all of them. You have built an
archive and the question needed a sequence.

## The three things that only exist between meetings

Once you look at it this way, you notice that the most valuable information in a customer
relationship is structurally invisible to a per-meeting tool.

**Change.** A customer who said $30,000 in March and $50,000 in October has told you something very
important, and neither of those two notes contains it. The information is in the delta, and the
delta has no home in a system that stores one note per call.

**Silence.** The economic buyer who attended the first two calls and has not spoken since is the
single most predictive fact about a late-stage deal. It is, definitionally, not in any note,
because nothing was said. You can only see it by looking across the sequence and noticing an
absence.

**The commitment ledger.** Every "I'll send you that" ever uttered, tracked against whether it was
sent. Each individual promise sits in one note. The thing you need is the list, which exists in no
note at all.

Three of the most decision-relevant properties of a relationship, and all three live in the gaps
between the documents.

## What this implies, practically

If the relationship rather than the meeting is the unit, a few things follow, and they are not
cosmetic.

**Conversations have to be queryable, not readable.** The output format matters less than whether
you can ask a question across all of it and get an answer with the source attached. Reading is what
you do when you cannot ask.

**The citation is the whole product.** An AI that tells you a customer's budget went up is a
liability. An AI that tells you the budget went up and plays you the eleven seconds where they said
it is a tool. Sales decisions get made on this, and a confident paraphrase is how optimism enters a
forecast. Anything that cannot show you the sentence should not be trusted with the conclusion.

**The before matters as much as the after.** If the point is the sequence, the most valuable moment
to deploy any of this is not after a call, it is in the ninety seconds before the next one, when
somebody is about to walk into a conversation without remembering what is still open.

**Capture has to be total, or the sequence has holes.** This is the unglamorous one. A memory with
gaps where the in-person meeting, the corridor conversation and the call somebody took from a car
should be is not a memory, it is a sample. The incentive to build for the easy case, the scheduled
video call with a link, is strong, and it produces exactly the holes that make the sequence
unreliable.

## The uncomfortable part

I work on this, so I should be straight about where the idea cuts against us as much as for us.

If the relationship is the unit, then the switching cost of these tools is enormous, and that is
not obviously good for customers. A per-meeting notes tool is easy to leave: you lose a folder of
summaries. A system that holds four years of account history is much harder to leave, and vendors
know it. The right response is to make export genuinely complete and genuinely easy, and to say so
plainly, rather than to enjoy the lock-in quietly. Anyone evaluating this category should ask what
export looks like before they ask what the summary looks like.

The second uncomfortable thing: an organisation that remembers every commitment is an organisation
where every commitment can be checked. That is mostly good and occasionally oppressive, and which
one it is depends entirely on whether the system is pointed at helping the rep or at auditing them.
That is a management decision, not a product one, and no vendor can make it for you.

## Where this lands

The category is going to keep getting better at summarising calls, and that will keep not being the
thing. The summary was always a proxy. What people wanted was to not have to hold a customer
relationship in their head, and to be able to ask it questions.

The meeting is not the document. The meeting is a write to the database. The product is the query.
