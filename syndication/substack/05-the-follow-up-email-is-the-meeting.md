# The Follow-Up Email Is the Meeting

*Forty-five minutes of conversation, and the only part anyone will remember is the five lines you send afterwards.*

Welcome back to **After the Call**. This week's issue is about the most underrated document in business: the email you send after a meeting.

## Memory is a terrible record

Here's what happens after almost every meeting. Everyone leaves with a slightly different version of what was agreed. The differences are small: a date that was "end of the month" for one person and "the 28th" for another, a deliverable that was "a draft" or "the final version." Over a week, small differences become missed deadlines and awkward conversations.

The follow-up email fixes that. Not because it's polite (though it is), but because it's the first time the meeting's outcome exists in writing. **Until someone writes it down, the meeting didn't decide anything. It just talked.**

## The five-line follow-up

You don't need a long email. You need five things:

1. **What we decided.** One or two sentences.
2. **What I'll do, and by when.**
3. **What you'll do, and by when.**
4. **The next touchpoint.** A date, a meeting, a milestone.
5. **One open question**, if there is one.

That's it. Here's what it looks like:

> Hi Priya,
>
> Thanks for today. To confirm: we'll start with a 15-person pilot once your IT team completes the security review.
>
> I'll send the security overview and the pilot plan by Friday. Could you introduce us to your IT lead this week?
>
> Let's check in on the 3rd to confirm the pilot start date.
>
> One open question: who on your side will own training for the pilot group?

Short enough to write in three minutes. Specific enough that nobody can misremember it.

For different situations (a client meeting, a networking coffee, a sales call, the "they never replied" follow-up), we put together [12 follow-up email examples](https://lynkk.ai/blog/follow-up-email-after-meeting) with subject lines and timing advice.

## Internal meetings need it too

The follow-up isn't just for clients. Internal meetings drift even more, because everyone assumes someone else will send the recap.

For team meetings, the format shifts slightly toward a summary: decisions, action items with owners and dates, and open questions, posted where the team will see it. A consistent format matters more than a perfect one; people learn where to look. Here's a free [meeting summary template](https://lynkk.ai/blog/meeting-summary-template) with real examples for team, client and project meetings.

## Send it within the hour

Timing matters more than polish. A follow-up sent within an hour:

- catches mistakes while everyone still remembers the meeting;
- signals that you were listening;
- lands before the other person's next meeting buries this one.

A follow-up sent three days later is a history lesson.

If you struggle to find the time, there are two tricks. First, write the email *during* the last five minutes of the meeting, and read it out loud: "Here's what I'm going to send, anything missing?" Second, let a notetaker draft it. Many tools, ours included, can produce the [meeting notes](https://lynkk.ai/features/meeting-notes) (decisions, action items, owners) a few minutes after the call ends, so the follow-up becomes a two-minute edit instead of a ten-minute write.

## The hidden benefit: your future self

There's one more reason to send the follow-up, and it's selfish. Six months from now, when someone asks "didn't we agree to X?", the answer will be in your sent folder, dated, in writing, with the other person's reply below it.

That's worth five lines.

## This week's takeaway

The meeting isn't over until the follow-up is sent. Five lines, within the hour, every time.
