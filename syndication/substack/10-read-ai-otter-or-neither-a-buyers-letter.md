# Read AI, Otter, Copilot or None of the Above? A Letter to Someone Choosing a Note Taker

*You don't need the best AI note taker. You need the one your team will still be using in three months.*

Welcome back to **After the Call**. This week's issue is written as a letter, because it answers a question that comes up constantly: *"We're choosing a note taker. Which one should we pick?"*

The usual disclosure, louder than usual: we make one of these tools. So we'll try hard to give you a way of deciding rather than a sales pitch.

---

Dear person with twelve browser tabs of comparison posts open,

First, close most of them. They mostly compare the same features in different orders, and most were written by a vendor (including ours). They won't tell you what you actually need to know: which tool fits the way *your* team meets.

Here's how we'd decide.

## Start with what you already pay for

Before adding anything, check what your meeting platform already includes.

- **On Microsoft Teams?** Depending on your licenses, you may already have transcription, intelligent recap with Teams Premium, or Copilot. They're often the simplest option, and the most limited outside Teams. We wrote a breakdown of [AI note takers for Microsoft Teams](https://lynkk.ai/blog/ai-note-taker-microsoft-teams), including what Copilot and Teams Premium cost for a 10-person team.
- **On Zoom?** Zoom AI Companion is included with many paid plans.
- **On Google Meet?** Gemini's note-taking may be included with your Workspace plan.

If your team meets on one platform and the built-in option is included, try it properly for two weeks before buying anything. It may be enough.

## If it isn't enough, ask why

The reasons built-in tools fall short are usually specific:

- you meet across two or three platforms;
- you also meet in person;
- you speak more than one language in the same meeting;
- you want action items in your task tracker, or questions answered across months of meetings;
- you need features the built-in plan doesn't include.

Write down your reason. It's your filter.

## The popular names, briefly

You've probably narrowed it to a few well-known tools. Two that come up constantly are Otter and Read AI. They take different approaches: broadly, Otter grew out of transcription and is known for live transcripts, while Read AI leans into meeting analytics and reports. Which matters more depends on whether you care most about the transcript or the insights. We put them [head to head, Read AI vs Otter AI](https://lynkk.ai/blog/read-ai-vs-otter-ai), on free limits, price per user and integrations for US teams.

If you're already using Otter and something isn't working, start from what's missing. Here's a comparison of [Otter.ai alternatives](https://lynkk.ai/blog/otter-ai-alternatives) by use case: sales, product and in-person meetings.

## Use the free plans as a trial

Nearly every tool has a free plan, and they're wildly different. Some cap meetings, some cap minutes, some cap how long notes are kept, some cap AI summaries. Match the cap to your habits: lots of short meetings, or a few long ones? Here's a side-by-side of [free AI note taker plans](https://lynkk.ai/blog/best-free-ai-note-takers) and what each upgrade costs.

Then do the one thing almost nobody does: **pick two tools and run both on the same real meetings for a week.** Compare the notes. Which got the names right? Which caught the action items? Which one did your colleagues actually open?

## The question that decides it

After the trial, ask your team one question: *"Which one would you be annoyed to lose?"*

That's your answer. Not the feature list. Not the price difference of a few dollars. The tool people would miss is the one they're actually using, and a note taker nobody uses is the most expensive one of all.
