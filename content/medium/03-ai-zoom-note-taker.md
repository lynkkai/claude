# How an AI Zoom Note Taker Works: Bot, Desktop App or Browser Extension?

## Pick the right kind of Zoom note taker app before you pick a brand

An AI Zoom note taker records your Zoom call, turns the audio into a transcript and writes notes such as a summary and action items. Almost every one works in one of three ways: a bot that joins the call, a desktop app on your computer, or a browser extension. Zoom also has its own built-in option.

Which kind you choose matters more than most feature lists suggest. It decides who sees that you are recording, whether a host can block it, and what happens when you join from a browser. This guide walks through each type, what to check before you trust one with work calls, and how to turn one on.

## Type 1: the meeting bot

A meeting bot is an extra participant that the note-taking service sends into your call. You paste the Zoom link, or connect your calendar so it joins on its own, and it records from inside the meeting.

**Why people like it:**

- It works no matter which computer or phone you join from.
- It can join meetings you are not in yet, or ones you miss.
- Everyone can see it in the attendee list, which makes recording visible.

**What to watch:**

- A host can leave it in the waiting room or remove it.
- Some companies block outside participants, which stops bots completely.
- On sensitive calls, a bot with a product name in the attendee list can make people guarded.

## Type 2: the desktop app

A desktop app runs on your own computer. It notices when a Zoom call starts and offers to record it. Nothing new appears in the meeting, because the recording happens on your machine.

**Why people like it:**

- No extra participant, so hosts cannot block it.
- It often works for other call apps too, not just Zoom.
- Recording does not depend on the host's settings or plan.

**What to watch:**

- It only records calls you join from that computer.
- Because nobody sees a bot, telling people you are recording is entirely your job.
- Check which operating systems it supports before you rely on it.

## Type 3: the browser extension

A browser extension records a Zoom call that you join through the Zoom web client in your browser. It captures the tab's audio and your microphone.

**Why people like it:**

- No install beyond the extension, and no bot in the call.
- Useful on locked-down work laptops where you cannot install desktop apps.

**What to watch:**

- It only works when you join Zoom in the browser, not the Zoom desktop app.
- As with a desktop app, you need to announce the recording yourself.

## What about Zoom's own AI note taker?

Zoom has a built-in assistant called AI Companion. According to Zoom's pages checked on September 30, 2026, it is included at no extra cost on paid Zoom Workplace plans, and licensed users can also invite it to Microsoft Teams and Google Meet meetings.

Two details catch people out:

- **Free Zoom accounts record locally only.** Zoom's transcripts come from cloud recordings, which need a paid plan.
- **Settings must be on before you hit record.** Zoom only transcribes a cloud recording if the transcript setting was already enabled.

At work, your account admin can also switch meeting summaries off. So "Zoom has AI notes" does not always mean you can use them.

## How to turn on a Zoom note taker

How to turn on a Zoom note taker depends on the type:

1. **Zoom AI Companion:** on a paid plan, turn on meeting summaries in your Zoom web settings (an admin may need to allow it first), then start it from the AI Companion button during the meeting.
2. **A meeting bot:** paste the meeting link into the service, or connect your calendar and turn on auto-join. Admit the bot if the meeting has a waiting room.
3. **A desktop app:** install it, sign in, and click Record when it detects the Zoom call.
4. **A browser extension:** add it to your browser, join Zoom in a browser tab, and press Record.

Whichever you use, test it on a short internal call first. A 10-minute test shows you where the notes land and how long they take to appear.

## 7 questions to ask before you trust one with work calls

Feature lists all look alike. These questions separate a tool you can use at work from one you cannot.

1. **Does it offer a bot, no bot, or both?** Both gives you options when a host blocks bots.
2. **What do the notes include?** A transcript alone still leaves you doing the work. Look for decisions, action items with owners, and timestamps you can click.
3. **Is there a cap per recording?** Long workshops and board meetings can run past limits.
4. **Is there a zoom note taker free plan, and what does it limit?** Free plans usually cap minutes, meetings or how long notes are kept.
5. **Does it train AI models on your conversations?** Ask directly, and get the answer in writing.
6. **Who can see your notes?** Check sharing links, team spaces and admin access.
7. **Which languages does it transcribe?** This matters a lot for global teams.

## Recording consent still applies

An AI Zoom note taker is still a recording device. In the US, federal law allows recording when at least one participant consents ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)), but several states require everyone on the call to agree. The [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework) is a useful checklist if your company is reviewing a recording tool.

The practical rule: say at the start of the call that it is being recorded, and add a line in the chat. This is general information, not legal advice.

## How to get notes from Zoom faster

Whatever tool you use, three habits make the notes more useful:

- **Say decisions out loud.** "So we agreed to launch on the 20th" is easy for any note taker to catch.
- **Name owners when you assign tasks.** "Sam, can you send that by Friday?" beats "someone should send that".
- **Skim the notes within an hour,** while you can still fix anything the AI got wrong.

## Summary: choosing an AI Zoom note taker

- **Bot:** visible, works from any device, can be blocked by hosts.
- **Desktop app:** invisible in the call, works when hosts block bots, only on your computer.
- **Browser extension:** no install, no bot, only for Zoom in the browser.
- **Zoom AI Companion:** built in on paid plans, but admins can switch it off.
- **Always:** check what the notes include, whether your data trains models, and tell people you are recording.
