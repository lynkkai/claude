# Follow-Up Email After a Meeting: 12 Examples for 2026

*By Samarth Wagh, Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A good example of a follow up email after a meeting does 4 things: **thanks** people, **restates decisions**, lists **action items with owners and dates**, and confirms the **next step**. Send it **within 24 hours**, ideally the same day. Copy one of the 12 examples below and fill in the brackets.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant that drafts follow-ups automatically.*

The follow-up email is where a meeting turns into results. Without it, people leave with different memories of what was agreed. With it, everyone has the same list of decisions, owners and dates in their inbox.

Below you will find a simple structure, 12 examples for common situations, subject lines that get opened, and guidance on timing and polite reminders.

## What should you write in a follow-up email after a meeting?

Write 5 parts, in this order: a short thank-you, the purpose or context in one line, decisions, action items with owners and dates, and the next step. Keep it under 150 words when you can.

```
Subject: [Meeting name] follow-up: next steps

Hi [Name],

Thanks for your time today. A quick recap of what we agreed:

Decisions
- [Decision]

Next steps
- [Owner]: [Task] by [date]
- [Owner]: [Task] by [date]

[Next meeting or next contact]

Best,
[Your name]
```

## How soon should you send a follow-up email after a meeting?

Send a follow-up email within 24 hours of the meeting, and the same day for sales and client meetings. The sooner you send it, the more accurately it reflects what was said, and the more likely people act on it.


## What is a good example of a follow up email after a meeting?

### 1. Follow-up email after a meeting with a client

```
Subject: Recap and next steps from today's review

Hi [Client name],

Thank you for today's project review. Here is what we agreed:

- Phase 2 scope is approved as discussed.
- Go-live moves to [date].

Next steps
- [Your name]: send the revised timeline by [date].
- [Client name]: share test accounts by [date].

I will send a calendar invite for our check-in on [date]. Let me know if I missed anything.

Best,
[Your name]
```

### 2. Follow-up email after a sales or discovery call

```
Subject: Great speaking today, [Name]: summary and next step

Hi [Name],

Thanks for walking me through [company]'s goals today. What I heard:

- Priority: [their top goal]
- Challenge: [their main pain point]
- Timeline: [their timeline]

As promised, here is [resource or case study link].

Next step: a 30-minute demo with [stakeholder] on [date]. Does [time] work?

Best,
[Your name]
```

For what to cover on that call, see [what is a discovery call](https://lynkk.ai/blog/what-is-a-discovery-call).

### 3. Follow-up email after a business meeting

```
Subject: Following up on our meeting about [topic]

Hi [Name],

It was good to meet today to discuss [topic]. To confirm, we agreed to [decision].

I will [your action] by [date], and you will [their action] by [date].

Looking forward to working together.

Best,
[Your name]
```

Next step: a trial for your team starting [date]. I will send setup details tomorrow.

Best,

```

### 8. Follow-up email after a meeting with your manager

```
Subject: 1:1 follow-up

Hi [Manager],

Thanks for today. To confirm:
- I will [task] by [date].
- You will [task, e.g. approve budget] by [date].

I will update you on progress at our next 1:1 on [date].

[Your name]
```

### 9. Follow-up email after a meeting with an employee

```
Subject: Summary of our conversation on [date]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for meeting today. To summarize what we discussed:
- [Topic and outcome]
- [Agreed support or resources]

Next steps
- You: [action] by [date]
- Me: [action] by [date]

We will review progress on [date]. Let me know if anything here does not match your understanding.

[Your name]
```

For sensitive HR conversations, follow your company's HR process and wording.

### 10. Follow-up email after a meeting with no response

```
Subject: Re: [original subject]

Hi [Name],

Just checking whether you had a chance to review the next steps from our [date] meeting. The one item I need from you is [specific ask] by [date].

Happy to jump on a quick call if that is easier.

Best,
[Your name]
```

### 11. Follow-up email after a vendor or partner meeting

```
Subject: Next steps from our [vendor/partner] meeting

Hi [Name],

Thanks for meeting with us. To confirm:
- You will send [proposal / pricing / contract] by [date].
- We will review internally and respond by [date].

Questions we still need answered: [question].

Best,
[Your name]
```

### 12. Follow-up email after a board or committee meeting

Subject: [Board/committee] meeting [date]: actions and next meeting

Dear members,

Thank you for attending. Draft minutes will follow by [date] for review.

Actions agreed
- [Name]: [action] by [date]

Next meeting: [date, time, location].

Regards,

```

## How do you follow up when someone does not reply?

Wait 2 to 3 business days, then reply in the same thread with 1 specific ask and a date. Most professionals stop after 2 or 3 polite reminders; after that, a short call or a message on another channel works better than more email.

A good reminder sequence:

1. **Day 3:** reply in the thread, restate the one thing you need (see example 10 above).
2. **Day 7:** offer an easier option, such as a 10-minute call or a yes/no question.
3. **Day 14:** close the loop politely: "I will assume we are pausing this for now; reply anytime."

Keep every reminder short. Each one should be easier to answer than the last.

## What mistakes should you avoid in a follow-up email?

The 5 most common follow-up mistakes are easy to avoid:

- **Sending it late.** After 48 hours, details blur and the email feels like an afterthought.
- **Recapping the whole meeting.** Send decisions and actions; link full notes.
- **Actions without owners or dates.** "We will look into it" is not a next step.
- **Burying the ask.** Put what you need from the reader in the first 3 lines.
- **No clear next meeting.** End with a date, a calendar link or a specific next contact.

A quick test: an example of a follow up email after a meeting is good if the reader knows their task within 10 seconds of opening it.

## What subject lines work for follow-up emails?

The best follow-up subject lines name the meeting and the content, so people know why to open them. Use 1 of these 6 patterns:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Recap and next steps | "Recap and next steps from today's review" |
| Meeting name plus date | "Team sync Oct 21: decisions and actions" |
| Count of actions | "Q4 planning: 2 decisions, 4 actions" |
| Personal thank-you | "Great speaking today, Maya" |
| Reply to original thread | "Re: Phase 2 scope" |
| Specific ask | "Need your sign-off on the timeline by Friday" |

Avoid vague subjects like "Following up" on its own; they get skipped.

## FAQ

### How do you write a follow-up email after a meeting?

Thank the attendees, restate decisions, list action items with owners and dates, and confirm the next step. Keep it short and send it within 24 hours.

### How long after a meeting should you send a follow-up email?

Within 24 hours, and the same day for client and sales meetings.

### What is a good subject line for a follow-up email after a meeting?

Name the meeting and the content, such as "Recap and next steps from today's review" or "Team sync Oct 21: decisions and actions".

### How do you follow up politely if there is no response?

Wait 2 to 3 business days, reply in the same thread, restate the one thing you need and offer a quick call.

### Should a follow-up email include action items?

Yes. Action items with an owner and a due date are the most important part, because they turn the meeting into work.
