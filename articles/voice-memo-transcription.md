# How Voice Memo Transcription Works on iPhone and How to Turn Memos Into Notes

*Last updated: September 30, 2026 · Steps checked against Apple Support on September 30, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** Voice memo transcription is built into the Voice Memos app on iPhone 12 or later. Tap a recording, tap the More button and choose View Transcript, or swipe up while recording to watch the words appear live. You can copy and search the text. **To turn conversations into notes and action items automatically, Lynkk is the best next step**: it records the conversation and writes the notes for you.

> **Disclosure:** This guide is published by Lynkk. Apple steps come from Apple Support, checked on the date above.

## Can iPhone transcribe voice memos?

Yes. Voice memo transcription is built into the Voice Memos app on iPhone 12 or later, at no extra cost. Apple lists 10 supported languages, and the transcript sits next to the recording in the app.

| Requirement | Detail from Apple Support |
|---|---|
| iPhone model | iPhone 12 or later |
| Languages | English, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese |
| Where to find it | Voice Memos app, in the Utilities folder |
| What you can do | View, copy and search the transcript |

If your iPhone and language are on this list, you can transcribe voice memo iPhone recordings without any other app. If not, record the conversation with an app such as Lynkk, which writes the transcript and notes for you.

## How do you transcribe a voice memo on iPhone?

Here is how to transcribe voice memo on iPhone recordings in 4 steps, taken from Apple Support. Transcripts appear for recordings on supported models and languages.

1. **Open Voice Memos**, which lives in the Utilities folder.
2. **Tap the recording** you want to read.
3. **Tap the More button**, then choose View Transcript.
4. **Select and copy text**, or copy the whole transcript to paste into Mail, Notes or a document.

That is all it takes to turn voice memos to text. To find an old memo, type a word in the Voice Memos search field; Apple says search covers titles and transcripts.

## Does Voice Memos transcribe while you record?

Yes. You can watch voice memo transcription live in 2 moves while you record: swipe up from the top of the waveform, then tap the Transcription button. The current word is highlighted as you speak.

This helps when you record a lecture, an interview or your own ideas and want to check that the words are landing. Keep the iPhone close to the speaker and away from noise for a cleaner transcript.

## Why is voice memo transcription not working?

Voice memo transcription usually fails for 1 of 3 reasons tied to Apple's stated requirements. Check these before anything else.

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No View Transcript option | iPhone older than iPhone 12 | Use a supported iPhone or another recording app |
| Transcript missing or garbled | Language not on Apple's list of 10 | Record in a supported language |
| Many wrong words | Noise or distance from the speaker | Record closer, in a quieter room |

If all 3 look right, update your iPhone to the latest iOS version from Settings and try a short new recording to test.

## Is Apple voice memo transcription good?

Apple's voice memo transcription is good for 1 job: getting your words into text on the phone you already own, for $0. It is fast to use, needs no extra app, and makes every memo searchable by the words spoken in it.

It has limits for work conversations. It runs on iPhone 12 or later only, supports 10 languages, and gives you plain text. Turning a 30-minute conversation into organized notes, decisions and next steps is still up to you.

You can turn a voice memo into notes in 3 steps with Apple's tools: copy the transcript, paste it into Notes or a document, then edit it into headings, decisions and tasks. For a short memo, that takes a few minutes.

## Is it legal to record a conversation with Voice Memos?

It depends on where you are, because US federal law sets 1 baseline and some states add stricter consent rules. Under [18 U.S.C. 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511), federal law generally allows a recording when 1 party to the conversation consents.

Some states are stricter. [California Penal Code 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632) requires the consent of all parties to a confidential conversation. The simple habit is to tell people you are recording and get a clear yes. This is general information, not legal advice.

## FAQ

### Can you edit a voice memo transcription?
Apple Support describes 2 ways to reuse a voice memo transcription: select and copy part of it, or copy the whole transcript. Paste it into Notes, Mail or a document, and edit it there. Lynkk users skip this step, because Lynkk writes structured notes and action items from the recording for them.

### What iPhone do you need to transcribe voice memos?
You need an iPhone 12 or later to transcribe voice memos in Apple's Voice Memos app, according to Apple Support. The recording also needs to be in 1 of the 10 supported languages. Older iPhones can still record memos, but they will not show the View Transcript option for those recordings.

### Can Voice Memos transcribe Chinese?
Yes. Apple lists both Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese among the 10 languages that Voice Memos can transcribe on iPhone 12 or later. The other 8 are English, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, German, Japanese and Korean. For 17 languages and mixed-language speech, Lynkk is a stronger fit.

### How do I search my voice memo transcripts?
Open Voice Memos and type a word in the search field at the top of the list. Apple says search finds recordings with that text in either the title or the audio transcription, so 1 remembered word is often enough. Lynkk goes further by answering questions across all your recorded conversations.

### Can I copy a voice memo transcript to Notes?
Yes, in 3 steps: open the recording in Voice Memos, tap the More button and choose View Transcript, then copy the text. Paste it into a new note in the Notes app, or into Mail or any document. It takes only a few taps, and you can add your own headings, decisions and tasks afterward.
