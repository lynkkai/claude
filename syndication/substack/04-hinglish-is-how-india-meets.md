# Hinglish Is How India Actually Meets. Most Meeting Tools Still Pretend It Isn't.

*Half your sentence in Hindi, half in English, and a transcript that makes sense of neither.*

Namaste, and welcome back to **After the Call**. This week's issue is for everyone who has ever read an AI meeting summary and wondered which meeting it was describing.

## The sentence every Indian team says

> "Client ne bola ki budget thoda tight hai, so let's propose the smaller pilot first."

If you work in an Indian company, you've heard (and said) a hundred sentences like that this week. Hindi grammar, English nouns, a switch in the middle. Linguists call it *code-switching*. Everyone else calls it Hinglish, or just "how we talk."

Now run that sentence through a meeting tool that asks you to choose a language up front. Choose English, and "client ne bola ki budget thoda tight hai" becomes a string of near-miss English words. Choose Hindi, and "propose the smaller pilot" comes out as an awkward transliteration. Either way, the one line that mattered in the meeting is garbled.

## Why it matters more than accuracy scores suggest

A transcript with a few wrong words isn't a disaster. A transcript where the *important* words are wrong is.

In Hinglish meetings, the important words are very often the English ones: product names, numbers, deadlines, client names, technical terms. "Deployment," "pilot," "28 tareekh tak," "sign off." If a tool mangles exactly those, the summary built on top of it will be confidently wrong, and the action items will name the wrong thing.

## Three things to check before you trust a tool

**1. Does it handle mixed speech in one recording?**
Not "does it support Hindi" and "does it support English" separately. Ask whether it handles both *in the same sentence*. Then test it.

**2. What script does the transcript come out in?**
Devanagari, Roman script or a mix? There's no wrong answer, but it should match how your team reads and searches. Most people search their notes in Roman script, so a pure-Devanagari transcript can be hard to search even if it's accurate.

**3. Test with your own audio.**
Record a real 10-minute team meeting (with everyone's okay), run it through each tool you're considering, and read the summaries side by side. Look at names, numbers and deadlines first.

## Why this matters beyond India

India is the clearest example, but code-switching is everywhere: Spanish and English in the US, French and Arabic in North Africa, Tagalog and English in the Philippines, Cantonese and English in Hong Kong. Multilingual teams are normal now. Meeting tools are slowly catching up.

## This week's takeaway

If your team speaks two languages at once, don't trust a demo in one language. Test with a real meeting, check the names and numbers first, and choose the tool that gets the important words right.
