# How to Add a Meeting Bot to Zoom, Google Meet or Teams, Step by Step

*Last updated: October 12, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** To add a meeting bot, paste the Zoom, Google Meet or Teams link into your note taker, or turn on calendar auto-join, then admit the bot from the waiting room or lobby if the host requires it. The Lynkk Zoom bot is free and records audio, and the Lynkk Mac app can record the same call with no bot at all.

## What is a meeting bot?

A meeting bot, often called a Zoom bot when it joins Zoom, is 1 extra participant that a note-taking app sends into your call to record it. It shows up in the attendee list like a person, listens for the whole meeting, and hands the recording to the app for a transcript and notes.

Most note-taking bots work on 3 platforms: Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams. The bot only needs the meeting link. Not your password.

A note-taking bot is different from the spam bots that flood open meetings. A legitimate Zoom bot joins only the calls you send it to, under a clear name, and the host can remove it at any time.

This guide is about adding a ready-made note-taking bot. Building your own Zoom bot is a developer project on Zoom's developer platform, and it is not covered here.

## How do you send a meeting bot to a meeting?

There are 2 ways to send a meeting bot to a meeting: paste the link for a single call, or connect your calendar so the bot joins every meeting with a link. Here is how to send a meeting bot to a meeting with the [Lynkk meeting bot](https://lynkk.ai/features/meeting-bot).

### Paste a link (one meeting)

1. **Copy the meeting link** from the Zoom, Google Meet or Teams invite.
2. **Paste it into Lynkk** and send the bot.
3. **Admit the bot** if the host sees a waiting room or lobby request.
4. **Watch the live transcript panel** during the meeting if you want to follow along.

### Turn on calendar auto-join (every meeting)

Connect your calendar and turn on auto-join, and the bot joins each meeting that has a Zoom, Google Meet or Teams link. This is the easiest option if you have 5 or more calls a day. You never paste a link again.

## How do you add a Zoom bot to a Zoom meeting?

To add a Zoom bot to a Zoom meeting, send the bot the Zoom link a minute or 2 before the start, then admit it if the meeting uses a waiting room. Once admitted, the Zoom bot to join meeting flow is done and recording starts.

A few Zoom details to know:

- **Waiting room:** if it is on, the host or a co-host must click Admit for the bot.
- **Host settings:** some hosts or company admins only allow signed-in users from their own domain, which can block any outside participant, including bots.
- **Join from the start:** a bot sent after the meeting begins only records from the moment it joins.

When people search "join zoom call bot", they are usually stuck on one of these 3 points. If the bot cannot get in, see the troubleshooting table below.

## How does a Google Meet bot join a call?

A Google Meet bot joins like a guest: it opens the Meet link and asks to join, and someone in the meeting clicks Admit. That takes about 10 seconds once the request appears.

Many people look for a "google meet bot joiner" because Meet blocks or hides requests in some setups. Check 2 things:

1. **Host Management or access settings:** in some Google Workspace meetings, only invited people or people in the organization can join without asking.
2. **Someone must be present to admit:** if nobody is in the call yet, the Google Meet bot waits for the first person to let it in.

If your meetings rarely allow outside guests, record from your own side instead with the [Lynkk Chrome extension](https://lynkk.ai/features/chrome-extension), which needs no bot. For Google's own transcript feature, see [how to get a Google Meet transcript](https://lynkk.ai/blog/google-meet-transcript).

## Can you add a meeting bot to Microsoft Teams?

Yes. You add a meeting bot to Teams the same way, by sending it the Teams meeting link, and it usually waits in the lobby for 1 person to admit it. Company policies matter more in Teams than in the other 2 platforms.

Teams admins can limit who bypasses the lobby. They can also block anonymous guests entirely. If your company blocks outside guests, a bot cannot get in, and a desktop app that records from your own computer is the better route. Our guide to the [best Teams AI note takers](https://lynkk.ai/blog/ai-note-taker-microsoft-teams) covers the Teams-specific options.

## Why can't my meeting bot join the call?

A meeting bot usually cannot join for 1 of 5 reasons, and most take under a minute to fix. Work down this table.

| Problem | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stuck in the waiting room | Nobody admitted it | Host or co-host clicks Admit |
| Request never appears | Meeting blocks outside guests | Ask the host, or record with no bot |
| Bot joined late | Link was sent after the start | Use calendar auto-join |
| Wrong meeting | Old or edited link | Copy the link from the latest invite |
| Removed by the host | Host did not expect a bot | Tell the host before the call |

If the meeting simply does not allow bots, the next section shows how to record without one. No bot, no admit button.

Yes. You can record Zoom, Google Meet and Teams with no bot in 2 ways: a desktop app on your computer or a browser extension. Nothing new appears in the attendee list.

- **[Lynkk Mac app](https://lynkk.ai/download):** a card says "Zoom call detected" (or Google Meet, Teams, Slack huddle, WhatsApp, FaceTime or Webex) with Record and Not now. It records both sides on 2 channels, so your side is labeled "You". It runs on Apple silicon Macs with macOS 14.2 or later.

A no-bot option is handy for sales calls and interviews. There, a Zoom bot in the attendee list can feel awkward.

## How do you stop a bot from joining your Zoom meeting?

You can stop an unwanted Zoom bot with 4 host settings, and the waiting room alone blocks most of them. Many university IT teams now publish this advice, because AI bots often join calls automatically from an attendee's calendar.

1. **Turn on the waiting room** so nobody, bot or person, gets in without being admitted.
2. **Allow only signed-in users** from your organization, if your Zoom account supports it.
3. **Remove the bot** from the participant list if it gets in, and lock the meeting.
4. **Ask attendees** to turn off calendar auto-join in their own note taker for your meetings.

The same rules apply to your own bot. If a host says no, respect it and take notes another way.

## Is it legal to use a meeting bot?

Using a meeting bot is legal in most of the US when at least 1 participant consents, under federal law ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)). Some states, including California ([Penal Code § 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632)), require every person on the call to agree.

A visible bot helps here. Everyone can see it in the attendee list. Still, say at the start that the meeting is being recorded. The [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework) is a useful checklist when your company reviews a recording tool. *This is general information, not legal advice.*

## FAQ

### Is a Zoom bot free?
The Lynkk Zoom bot is free and records audio on 3 platforms, Zoom, Google Meet and Teams, with a live transcript during the call and a Meeting Brief afterward. Video recording from the bot is a Pro feature. Other note takers set their own limits, so check them before you rely on a free plan.

### Does a meeting bot need my Zoom password?
No. A meeting bot needs only 1 thing, the meeting link, not your Zoom, Google or Microsoft password. It joins as its own participant, and the host can admit or remove it like any guest. If you connect your calendar for auto-join, the bot reads meeting links from your events, not your account login.

### Can other people see the meeting bot?
Yes. A meeting bot appears in the attendee list as 1 more participant under its own name, so everyone in the call can see it. That makes recording visible, which helps with consent. If a visible bot would feel awkward, record with no bot from the Lynkk Mac app or the Chrome extension, and announce it yourself.

### Why does my Google Meet bot keep waiting to join?
A Google Meet bot waits for 1 of 2 reasons: nobody has clicked Admit yet, or the meeting only lets invited people or people from the host's organization join. Ask someone in the call to admit it within the first minute, or record from your own browser tab with a Chrome extension instead of a bot.

### Can a meeting bot join if I am not the host?
Usually yes, as long as 1 of 2 people, the host or a co-host, admits it and the meeting allows outside guests. Some company settings block all external participants, which stops any bot. In those meetings, tell the host before the call, or record from your own computer with a desktop app that needs no bot.
