# Sprint Retrospective Template for 2026: 40 Questions

*By Samarth Wagh, Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** Here are sprint retrospective samples you can use today: a **copy-ready template**, **5 formats** (Start Stop Continue, What went well and What to improve, 4Ls, Mad Sad Glad, Sailboat), **40 questions** grouped by theme, and **sample answers**. Whatever format you choose, end with **1 to 3 action items**, each with an owner, and put them in your backlog.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant that turns retro action items into Jira tickets.*

Sprint retrospectives fail in a predictable way: the same complaints come up every 2 weeks and nothing changes. The fix is rarely a cleverer format. It is fewer, better questions and action items that someone actually owns.

These sprint retrospective samples give you everything to run a good retro this week: a template, formats, 40 questions, example answers and a way to make sure actions get done.

## What is a sprint retrospective?

A sprint retrospective is the Scrum event at the end of each sprint where the team inspects how it worked, covering people, process, tools and the definition of done, and agrees on improvements. The [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) timeboxes it to a maximum of 3 hours for a 1-month sprint; shorter sprints usually need less.

It is different from the sprint review. The review looks at the product increment with stakeholders. The retrospective looks at how the team works, usually with the team only.

## What does a sprint retrospective template look like?

This is the simplest of our sprint retrospective samples. Copy the template into Confluence, Notion, a Google Doc or a Miro board.

```
SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE
Team: [ ]   Sprint: [Sprint 42, Oct 6 to Oct 17]
Facilitator: [ ]   Date: [ ]

1. CHECK-IN (5 min)
   One word for how this sprint felt: [ ]

2. REVIEW LAST RETRO'S ACTIONS (5 min)
   | Action | Owner | Done? |

3. WHAT WENT WELL (10 min)
   - [ ]

4. WHAT DIDN'T GO WELL (10 min)
   - [ ]

5. WHAT WE'LL CHANGE (15 min)
   Vote on the top 2 issues, then agree actions.

6. ACTION ITEMS (5 min)
   | Action | Owner | Due | Ticket |
   | [ ]    | [ ]   | [ ] | [ ]    |

7. CLOSE (5 min)
   Rate this retro 1 to 5: [ ]
```

That is 55 minutes, which suits a 2-week sprint.

## Which sprint retrospective formats work best?

These 5 formats cover most teams. Rotate them every few sprints so the retro stays fresh.

| Format | Columns | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Start, Stop, Continue | Start doing, Stop doing, Keep doing | Teams that want concrete changes |
| Went well, Improve, Actions | What went well, What to improve, Actions | New teams, default choice |
| 4Ls | Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for | Reflecting on learning |
| Mad, Sad, Glad | Frustrations, disappointments, wins | Surfacing team mood |
| Sailboat | Wind (helps), anchors (slows), rocks (risks), island (goal) | Visual teams, longer projects |

If the team is tired of formats, skip them and ask 3 well-chosen questions from the list below.

## What are 40 good sprint retrospective questions?

Pick 3 to 5 questions per retro, not all 40. Like all sprint retrospective samples, they work best adapted to your team. They are grouped into 5 themes of 8.

**Delivery and goals**

1. Did we meet the sprint goal? Why or why not?
2. What did we deliver that we are proud of?
3. Where did our estimates miss, and by how much?
4. What blocked us the longest?
5. Did scope change mid-sprint? Why?
6. What did we start but not finish?
7. Was the sprint goal clear to everyone?
8. What would we cut if we ran this sprint again?

**Process and meetings**

9. Which meeting was most useful this sprint?
10. Which meeting could have been an async update?
11. Did our standups help us unblock each other?
12. Was the backlog ready when planning started?
13. How well did we follow our definition of done?
14. Where did work wait the longest?
15. Did we have too much work in progress?
16. What one process change would save the most time?

**Collaboration and communication**

17. Who helped you this sprint?
18. Where did handoffs break down?
19. Did anyone feel out of the loop on a decision?
20. How well did we work with design, QA or product?
21. Were disagreements resolved quickly?
22. Did we ask for help early enough?
23. What should we communicate more to stakeholders?
24. What did we learn from someone else on the team?

**Quality and tools**

25. Where did bugs slip through?
26. What technical debt slowed us down?
27. Which tool helped most this sprint?
28. Which tool or workflow frustrated us?
29. Were our tests and CI reliable?
30. What should we automate next?
31. Did we document decisions where people can find them?
32. What would make code review faster?

**Team health and improvement**

33. How sustainable was our pace?
34. What gave you energy this sprint?
35. What drained your energy?
36. What is one thing we should try next sprint?
37. Did we complete last retro's action items?
38. What are we avoiding talking about?
39. What would make the next sprint 10% better?
40. What do we want to celebrate?

## What are sprint retrospective samples for "what went well"?

Good "what went well" answers are specific and name a cause, so the team can repeat it. Here are 8 sprint retrospective samples for what went well:

- "We hit the sprint goal 2 days early because the backlog was refined before planning."
- "Pairing on the payment bug cut the fix time from days to an afternoon."
- "Daily standups stayed under 15 minutes."
- "QA joined planning, so we caught 3 unclear stories before coding."
- "We shipped the onboarding flow with zero rollbacks."
- "Design handoff included edge cases this time."
- "We said no to 2 mid-sprint requests and protected the goal."
- "The new CI cache made builds noticeably faster."

Compare these with weak answers like "communication was good". Specific answers turn into habits; vague ones disappear.

## What should you say in a sprint retrospective?

Say what happened, its effect and a suggestion, in that order. A simple structure is: "When X happened, it caused Y. Next time, could we try Z?" It keeps feedback about the work, not the person.

Sample answers for what did not go well:

- "When stories entered the sprint without acceptance criteria, we spent 2 days clarifying. Could we add a ready check in refinement?"
- "When the release slipped, stakeholders heard it from support first. Could the PM post a weekly status?"
- "When 3 people were pulled into incidents, our sprint goal became unrealistic. Could we keep an on-call buffer?"

## How do you run a sprint retrospective step by step?

Run a sprint retrospective in 6 steps:

1. **Set the stage.** Remind everyone the goal is improvement, not blame.
2. **Review last retro's actions.** Done, not done, or dropped.
3. **Gather data.** Use your chosen format or 3 questions, silently first.
4. **Generate insights.** Group similar points and vote on the top 2.
5. **Decide actions.** 1 to 3 actions, each with 1 owner and a due date.
6. **Close.** Quick rating of the retro so you can improve it too.

Step 2 is the one most teams skip, and it is the reason the same issues return.

## How do you make sure retrospective actions get done?

Put retrospective actions in the same backlog as feature work, with an owner and a due date, and review them at the start of the next retro. Actions that live only in a retro board are usually forgotten by day 3.

## FAQ

### What is a sprint retrospective?

A sprint retrospective is a Scrum event at the end of each sprint where the team reviews how it worked and agrees on improvements for the next sprint.

### How long should a sprint retrospective be?

The Scrum Guide timeboxes it to a maximum of 3 hours for a 1-month sprint. Most 2-week sprints use 45 to 90 minutes.

### What are the 3 questions of a sprint retrospective?

The classic 3 questions are: What went well? What did not go well? What will we do differently next sprint?

### How often should sprint retrospectives happen?

Once per sprint, at the end, after the sprint review and before the next sprint planning.

### Who attends a sprint retrospective?

The Scrum team: developers, the product owner and the Scrum Master. Stakeholders usually attend the sprint review instead.

### What is the difference between a sprint review and a retrospective?

The sprint review inspects the product increment with stakeholders. The retrospective inspects how the team worked and plans improvements.
