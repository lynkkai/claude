# Patterns that make prose sound machine-written

Use this as a scan list during the `written-prose` pass. Each pattern is a
suspect, not a rule: keep it when it is genuinely the best way to say the thing.

## 1. Vocabulary

Words that are fine alone but signal AI when they cluster:

- **Inflated verbs:** delve, leverage, harness, unlock, unleash, elevate,
  empower, streamline, navigate (for non-physical things), foster, underscore,
  showcase, revolutionize, supercharge
- **Inflated adjectives:** crucial, pivotal, vital, robust, seamless, dynamic,
  comprehensive, cutting-edge, game-changing, transformative, innovative,
  ever-evolving, meticulous
- **Abstract nouns:** landscape, realm, tapestry, journey, ecosystem, synergy,
  testament, paradigm, cornerstone, beacon
- **Fix:** use the plain word ("use", "important", "area") or delete it.

## 2. Stock phrases and throat-clearing

- "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of"
- "It's worth noting that", "It's important to note", "It goes without saying"
- "Let's dive in", "Let's explore", "In this article, we will"
- "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "In conclusion", "To sum up"
- "Whether you're a X or a Y", "Look no further"
- **Fix:** delete. The sentence almost always works without it.

## 3. Formula structures

- **"Not X, but Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y"** used repeatedly. Once
  can be a strong contrast. Three times reads as a template.
- **Rule of three by habit:** lists of exactly three adjectives or clauses in
  sentence after sentence ("fast, simple, and reliable").
- **Colon reveals:** "Here's the thing:", "The result?", "The answer:", "The
  catch:" used as drama.
- **Rhetorical-question openers** at the top of sections.
- **Tidy aphorism endings:** a short, quotable line closing every paragraph
  ("That's the power of clarity."). Keep one if it earns its place.
- **Mirror closings:** a conclusion that recites every section.
- **Fix:** keep the strongest instance, convert the others to plain statements.

## 4. Rhythm and formatting

- Every sentence about the same length, or every paragraph the same shape.
- Too many dramatic one-line paragraphs or fragments ("Simple. Effective.").
- Bullet lists where every item starts with a bolded label, used everywhere.
- Heavy bold, emoji in headings, Title Case Headings when the house style is
  sentence case.
- Em dashes and en dashes. Some house styles (including this repo's) ban them
  entirely; replace with a comma, colon, parentheses or two sentences.
- **Fix:** vary deliberately, but match the house format.

## 5. Inflation and vagueness

- **Significance inflation:** "plays a crucial role", "marks a pivotal moment",
  "a key part of".
- **Vague attribution:** "experts say", "studies show", "many believe", with no
  source. Either cite or soften to the author's own advice.
- **Universal claims** with no evidence: "everyone", "always", "nobody", "most
  teams". Soften ("often", "can") or cite.
- **Hedge stacking:** "may potentially help to some extent".
- **Fix:** be specific, cite, or scale the claim down to what's supported.

## 6. Tone

- Over-enthusiasm: "exciting", "amazing", exclamation marks.
- False intimacy or sycophancy: "Great question", "You've got this!"
- Promotional tone in non-promotional sections.
- **Fix:** write like a knowledgeable colleague explaining something calmly.
