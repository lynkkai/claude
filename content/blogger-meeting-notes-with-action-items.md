# How to Write Meeting Notes With Action Items (Free Template + Example)

**Quick answer:** good meeting notes with action items have five parts: the meeting basics (date,
attendees, purpose), the key decisions, a table of action items where every task has **one owner
and one due date**, open questions, and the next meeting date. Send them within 24 hours. A free
template you can copy is below, followed by a filled-in example.

Most meeting notes fail for one simple reason: they describe the conversation instead of the
outcome. Three days later, nobody remembers who promised what, and the same topic comes back at
the next meeting. This guide shows you how to take meeting minutes that actually move work forward.

## What are action items in a meeting?

An **action item** is a specific task that someone agreed to do as a result of a meeting. It has
a clear owner, a deadline, and a definition of done. "Look into pricing" is a topic. "Sara sends
three pricing options to the team by Friday" is an action item.

In meeting minutes, action items are usually listed in their own section or table so they're easy
to find and track. They are the most important part of the notes, because they are the only part
that creates work after the meeting ends.

## Meeting notes vs meeting minutes: what's the difference?

| | Meeting notes | Meeting minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Help the team remember and act | Official record of what was decided |
| Style | Informal, short, bullet points | Formal, structured, sometimes approved |
| Typical use | Team syncs, project meetings, client calls | Board meetings, committees, associations |
| Action items | Yes, the main focus | Yes, alongside motions and votes |

For most teams, "meeting notes with action items" is what you want. If you're writing minutes for
a board or committee, use the same template and add motions, votes and an approval line.

## Free meeting notes template with action items

Copy this into Google Docs, Word, Notion or an email. Delete any section you don't need.

```
MEETING NOTES

Meeting:        [Name of meeting]
Date / time:    [Date, start and end time]
Facilitator:    [Name]
Note taker:     [Name]
Attendees:      [Names]
Absent:         [Names]
Purpose:        [One sentence: why did we meet?]

1. KEY DECISIONS
   - [Decision 1]
   - [Decision 2]

2. ACTION ITEMS
   | # | Action (start with a verb) | Owner | Due date | Status |
   |---|----------------------------|-------|----------|--------|
   | 1 |                            |       |          | Open   |
   | 2 |                            |       |          | Open   |
   | 3 |                            |       |          | Open   |

3. DISCUSSION SUMMARY
   - [Topic]: [2-3 lines on what was said and why it matters]

4. OPEN QUESTIONS / PARKING LOT
   - [Question] (raised by [Name])

5. NEXT MEETING
   Date: [Date]
   Review: last meeting's action items first
```

Notice that every action item starts with a verb, has one name next to it, and has a real date.
That's what makes it trackable.

## How to take meeting minutes in 6 steps

### 1. Prepare the template before the meeting
Fill in the meeting name, date, attendees and purpose in advance. If there's an agenda, add each
agenda item as a heading. You'll spend the meeting listening instead of typing headers.

### 2. Listen for decisions, not dialogue
You don't need to write down who said what. Listen for phrases like "let's go with", "agreed",
"so we'll" and "final answer". Those signal a decision. Write the decision in one sentence.

### 3. Capture action items the moment they're spoken
When someone says "I'll do that" or "can you send...", write it down immediately in the action
items table. If the owner or date isn't clear, ask right then: *"Who's taking that, and by when?"*
This one question is the single biggest improvement you can make to your meeting notes.

### 4. Park off-topic questions
Unresolved questions go in the parking lot with the name of the person who raised it. This keeps
the meeting moving and makes sure the question isn't lost.

### 5. Clean up and send within 24 hours
Spend five minutes fixing typos, merging duplicate tasks and checking dates. Then send the notes
to all attendees and anyone who missed the meeting. Notes sent a week later are rarely read.

### 6. Start the next meeting with the action items
Open the next meeting by going through the previous action items: done, in progress, or blocked.
This creates accountability without anyone having to chase people.

## How to write action items that actually get done: 6 rules

1. **Start with a verb.** "Send", "Book", "Draft", "Review", "Decide". Not "Pricing" or "Website".
2. **One owner per task.** "Marketing team" is not an owner. If two people share it, pick one lead.
3. **A real due date.** "Friday 17 October" beats "next week". "ASAP" is not a date.
4. **Small enough to finish.** If it will take more than a week, break it into smaller steps.
5. **Clear definition of done.** "Draft the email" and "Send the email" are different tasks.
6. **Visible to everyone.** Put action items in a shared doc or task tool, not in one person's notebook.

## FAQ about meeting notes and action items

**Should meeting minutes include action items?**
Yes. Action items are the most useful part of meeting minutes because they record who will do what
after the meeting. Even formal board minutes usually list actions alongside motions and votes.

**What is the difference between meeting minutes and action items?**
Meeting minutes are the full record of a meeting: attendees, decisions and discussion. Action items
are one section inside the minutes: the list of tasks with owners and due dates.

**How do you write action items in meeting minutes?**
Write each one as a short sentence that starts with a verb, then add one owner and one due date.
Put them in a table so they're easy to scan and track.

**What is the best format for meeting minutes with action items?**
Meeting basics at the top, then key decisions, then an action items table (task, owner, due date,
status), then discussion summary, open questions, and next meeting date. Decisions and actions go
first because that's what readers look for.

**Can meeting minutes be changed after they're sent?**
Yes. Informal notes can be corrected at any time. Formal minutes are normally corrected by
approving amendments at the next meeting.

**How soon should meeting notes be sent?**
Within 24 hours, while everyone still remembers the conversation and before the first deadlines
arrive.
