# Meeting Notes vs Minutes: How to Write Minutes in 2026

*By [Samarth Wagh](https://lynkk.ai/blog/authors/samarth-wagh), Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** Meeting notes and minutes differ in 3 ways: purpose, format and approval. **Meeting notes** are informal and written for yourself or your team. **Meeting minutes** are the official record of a meeting: who attended, what was decided, who owns each action, and any votes. Minutes are usually reviewed and approved at the next meeting. To turn meeting notes into minutes, keep decisions, owners and dates, and cut the discussion.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant that drafts notes and minutes.*

People search "meeting notes minutes" because the two words get used interchangeably at work. In a casual team sync, the difference rarely matters. In a board meeting, an HOA meeting or a contract negotiation, it matters a lot: minutes can become evidence of what an organization agreed to.

This guide explains the real difference between meeting notes and minutes, what minutes must include, and a simple method to turn your notes into minutes in about 10 minutes.

## What is the difference between meeting notes and minutes?

The difference is purpose and formality. Meeting notes help the person or team remember what happened. Meeting minutes are the organization's official record of what was decided, approved by the group and kept for reference.

| | Meeting notes | Meeting minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Personal or team memory | Official record |
| Audience | You or your team | The organization, auditors, members |
| Content | Ideas, discussion, reminders | Attendance, decisions, actions, votes |
| Format | Free form | Fixed structure |
| Approval | None | Usually approved at the next meeting |
| Retention | As long as useful | Often kept for years |

A simple test: if a lawyer, auditor or new board member might read it in 2 years, write minutes.

## Are meeting notes the same as minutes?

No. All minutes start as notes, but not all notes become minutes. Notes can include opinions, side ideas and half-finished thoughts. Minutes include only what the group did: who attended, motions made, decisions taken and actions assigned.

[Robert's Rules of Order](https://robertsrules.com/), the parliamentary guide many US boards and associations follow, puts it simply: minutes should record what was done at the meeting, not what was said.

## Why are meeting notes called minutes?

The word "minutes" is not about time ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minutes) covers the history). It comes from the Latin *minuta*, meaning "small", and refers to the small, rough notes a clerk took during a meeting before writing a full copy. Over time, the official record itself became known as "the minutes".

So when someone asks for "the minutes", they mean the formal, cleaned-up record, not the rough notes.

## What should meeting minutes include?

Meeting minutes should include 8 core items. Formal bodies such as boards and associations add motions and votes.

1. Organization name, meeting type, date, time and location
2. Who presided and who recorded the minutes
3. Attendees, absentees and whether a quorum was present
4. Approval of the previous meeting's minutes
5. Each agenda item with a short summary
6. Decisions, motions and vote results
7. Action items with an owner and a due date
8. Time of adjournment and the next meeting date

## What should meeting notes include?

Meeting notes are flexible, but useful notes still capture 4 things: key points, decisions, action items and open questions. Unlike minutes, notes can include context, ideas and your own reminders.

Good notes make minutes easy. If your notes already separate decisions and actions from discussion, converting them takes minutes, not an hour. Our guide on [how to take good meeting notes](https://lynkk.ai/blog/how-to-take-good-meeting-notes) covers methods.

## How do you turn meeting notes into minutes?

You can turn meeting notes into minutes in about 10 minutes with 6 steps:

1. **Start from a template.** Use a fixed minutes format so nothing is missed.
2. **Add the facts.** Date, time, location, attendees, absentees and quorum.
3. **Keep decisions, cut debate.** Replace "we discussed pricing at length" with "The team agreed to raise the Pro price to $20 from January 1."
4. **Record motions and votes exactly.** Who moved, who seconded, and the count for, against and abstaining.
5. **List every action** with one owner and one due date.
6. **Send the draft for review**, then approve it at the next meeting.

Write in the past tense, use neutral language, and avoid quoting people unless the exact words matter.

## Meeting notes vs minutes example

Here is the same agenda item written as notes and as minutes.

**Notes:**

```
Pricing: Sam thinks we're too cheap. Priya worried about churn.
Long back and forth. Maybe test in one region first?
Agreed: raise Pro to $20 from Jan 1. Sam to update site.
Need churn data before Q1 review.
```

**Minutes:**

```
3. Pricing review
The team reviewed Pro plan pricing and churn risk.
Decision: Pro plan price to increase to $20 per month from January 1.
Action: Sam Lee to update the pricing page by December 15.
Action: Priya Shah to present churn data at the Q1 review.
```

The minutes keep 1 decision and 2 actions. The debate, opinions and guesses stay in the notes.

## How is a meeting summary different from minutes?

A meeting summary is a short, readable recap, usually 5 to 10 lines, sent to the team after a meeting. Minutes are longer and formal, with attendance, motions and votes. Many teams send a summary the same day and keep minutes only for formal meetings.

| | Meeting summary | Meeting minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 5 to 10 lines | 1 to 3 pages |
| Tone | Conversational | Formal, past tense |
| Includes votes | Rarely | Yes, for formal bodies |
| Approved by the group | No | Usually |

## How long should meeting minutes be?

Most meeting minutes run 1 to 2 pages for a 1-hour meeting. Aim for 2 to 4 lines per agenda item plus the exact text of any motion or resolution. If your minutes are longer than 3 pages, you are probably recording discussion that belongs in notes.

Shorter is better as long as 3 things are complete: every decision, every vote, and every action with an owner and date.

## When do you need formal minutes instead of notes?

You need formal minutes whenever an organization makes decisions it may need to prove later. In the US, that usually includes 5 kinds of meetings:

- **Board of directors meetings**, for companies and nonprofits
- **HOA and condo association meetings**, where state law often requires records
- **LLC and corporate member meetings**, to document major decisions
- **Public bodies** such as city councils and school boards
- **Committee meetings** that make decisions on the board's behalf

## FAQ

### What is the difference between meeting notes and minutes?

Meeting notes are informal records for personal or team use. Meeting minutes are the official, approved record of attendance, decisions, actions and votes. Notes help people remember. Minutes are what an organization relies on later, so they follow a fixed format and are kept for years.

### Are meeting minutes just notes?

No. Minutes are a structured, approved record of what a group decided. Notes are informal and can include opinions and side discussions that do not belong in minutes. A useful test: if a lawyer, auditor or new board member might read it in 2 years, write minutes rather than notes.

### Why are they called minutes?

"Minutes" comes from the Latin minuta, meaning small, and referred to the rough notes taken during a meeting. It has nothing to do with time. The name stuck when clerks rewrote their rough notes into a full record, which became known as the minutes of the meeting.

### Who takes meeting minutes?

Usually a secretary, clerk or designated note-taker. In boards and associations, the secretary is typically responsible, and the chair approves the final minutes. The note-taker should not also chair the meeting. Running a discussion and recording it at the same time usually means both are done badly.

### Should minutes include who said what?

Usually not. Minutes record decisions, motions and votes. Include a speaker's name only for motions, seconds, votes or when the exact statement matters. Write in the past tense and neutral language. Avoid quoting people unless the exact words matter for a motion or a later dispute.

### How soon should minutes be sent after a meeting?

Send draft minutes within 1 to 2 business days while details are fresh, then approve them at the next meeting. Late minutes are less accurate, because details fade fast. Mark them as a draft until they are approved, and keep the approved version in a shared place.
