# Title and subtitle options

Each option is a different headline shape, per the skill's title guidance. All of
them promise only what the article delivers.

## A. Direct how-to (current choice)

- **Title:** How to Take Meeting Notes That Actually Turn Into Action
- **Subtitle:** Stop recording what people said. Capture decisions, owners and
  deadlines, and hand the rest to software.
- **Why:** Contains the target search phrase ("take meeting notes"), which matters
  because Medium posts rank in Google. Clear payoff at a glance.

## B. Consequential claim

- **Title:** Stop Writing Down What People Said in Meetings
- **Subtitle:** Write down decisions, owners and deadlines instead. A reusable
  format, plus a rule for which parts to automate.
- **Why:** Stronger in the Medium feed because it challenges a habit. Backed by the
  Robert's Rules point in the article. Weaker for search.

## C. Recognizable mistake

- **Title:** Your Meeting Notes Fail the Two-Minute Test
- **Subtitle:** Could someone who missed the meeting read them and know what to do
  next? A four-part format that fixes that.
- **Why:** Curiosity hook built on the article's central test. Least search value,
  and the reader has to click to learn what the test is.

## D. Question

- **Title:** What Should You Actually Write Down in a Meeting?
- **Subtitle:** Four things worth capturing, five to drop, and which parts AI can
  handle for you.
- **Why:** Matches how people phrase the question. The subtitle uses the article's
  real counts (four sections to keep, five items to drop).

## Recommendation

Keep **A** for search traffic. If the piece is mainly for Medium's feed or a
publication, **B** is the stronger headline.
