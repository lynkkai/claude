## What is an AI note taker?

An AI note taker is an app that captures meeting audio, converts it to text with speech recognition, and uses a language model to produce a summary, key decisions and action items. Most tools deliver notes within 1-2 minutes of a call ending, which removes the 15-30 minutes people often spend writing up a meeting by hand.

There are three ways an AI meeting notes taker captures audio:

- **Meeting bot:** a participant named after the tool joins your Zoom, Teams or Google Meet call and records it (Otter, Fireflies, Read AI).
- **Bot-free desktop or phone app:** the app records your device's audio, so nobody sees a bot in the participant list (Granola, Jamie, Fathom's bot-free mode).
- **Built-in assistant:** the meeting platform itself summarizes the call (Zoom AI Companion, Microsoft Copilot in Teams, Gemini in Google Meet).

## 1. Fathom: best free AI note taker

Fathom's free plan includes unlimited recordings, transcripts and AI summaries, which is the most generous free tier in this list. Paid plans start at $15 per user per month, and Fathom offers a 90-day money-back guarantee on paid plans.

**Best for:** individuals and small teams who want a capable AI note taker for Zoom, Teams and Google Meet without paying.

**What we liked:**
- Unlimited recordings on the free plan, with advanced summaries for your first 5 calls each month.
- A choice of bot or bot-free capture.
- Clips and playlists make it easy to share one moment from a long call.

**What could be better:**
- Advanced summary templates are limited on the free plan after 5 calls a month.
- New Team and Business plans no longer include free seats, so every team member needs a paid seat.

**Pricing (checked September 30, 2026):** Free; paid plans from $15/user/month, with published tiers from $19 to $34 per user per month. [Source: fathom.ai/pricing]

## Which AI note taker is best for meetings?

The best AI note taker for meetings depends on where you meet and whether a bot is acceptable. In this list, 3 of the 9 tools can record without a bot joining the call (Fathom, Granola and Jamie), and 2 are built into meeting platforms many companies already pay for.

- **Mostly Zoom, want zero setup:** Zoom AI Companion.
- **Mostly Teams inside Microsoft 365:** Microsoft 365 Copilot, or a cheaper third-party note taker if you only need notes. See our guide to [AI note takers for Microsoft Teams](/blog/ai-note-taker-microsoft-teams).
- **Mixed Zoom, Teams and Google Meet:** Fathom (free), Otter or Fireflies.
- **No bot allowed:** Granola, Jamie or Fathom's bot-free mode.
- **In-person meetings and interviews:** Otter's mobile app, Granola on your phone, or Lynkk.
- **Help before and during the meeting, not just after:** Lynkk.

## Can an AI note taker work without joining the meeting?

Yes. A bot-free AI note taker records the audio on your own computer or phone, so no extra participant appears on the call. Granola and Jamie are bot-free by design, and Fathom offers a bot-free capture option; this matters for client calls where an unexpected bot can make people uncomfortable.

Bot-free tools still record the other people in the meeting, so the consent rules below still apply.

## Can an AI note taker record in-person meetings?

Yes, at least 3 tools in this list record face-to-face conversations: Otter through its mobile app, Granola on iPhone and Android, and Lynkk, which is built to capture in-person meetings as well as online calls. An AI note taker for in-person meetings uses your phone or laptop microphone, so place the device near the center of the table for better accuracy.

If you are weighing an app against a dedicated recorder such as Plaud or Pocket, see our guide to [AI voice recorders](/blog/ai-voice-recorders).

## Are AI note takers safe to use at work?

AI note takers are generally safe when the vendor encrypts data, lets you control retention and does not train models on your meetings without permission. Check four things before rolling one out: where data is stored, who can access recordings, whether the vendor offers SSO, and whether it signs agreements your industry needs (for example a HIPAA business associate agreement).

Recording laws also matter in the US. Some states require every participant to consent before a conversation is recorded, so announce the note taker at the start of each call. [CONFIRM: link to a reputable legal source on state recording consent laws]

## FAQ

### What is the best AI note taker?
There is no single best AI note taker for everyone. Fathom is the best free choice, Otter is strong for live transcripts, Fireflies suits teams that want integrations, Granola and Jamie lead for bot-free notes, and Lynkk fits people who want help before, during and after meetings.

### What is the best AI meeting notes taker for Zoom?
For Zoom-only teams, Zoom AI Companion is included with paid Zoom plans at no extra cost. If you also use Teams or Google Meet, Fathom, Otter and Fireflies all support Zoom plus other platforms. See our guide to [Zoom meeting recorders](/blog/zoom-meeting-recorders).

### Does Microsoft Teams have an AI note taker?
Yes. Microsoft 365 Copilot can recap Teams meetings, but it is a $30 per user per month add-on on top of a qualifying Microsoft 365 license. Third-party AI note takers are often cheaper if you only need meeting notes.

### Does Google Meet have an AI note taker?
Yes. Gemini in Google Meet can take notes on eligible Google Workspace plans. [CONFIRM: current Workspace plan eligibility] Read more in our guide to [Google Meet transcripts and AI notes](/blog/google-meet-ai-note-taker).

### Are AI note takers accurate?
Accuracy depends mostly on audio quality, accents and cross-talk. Clear audio with one speaker at a time produces the best transcripts; in noisy rooms, expect to correct names and technical terms.

### Do I need permission to use an AI note taker?
You should tell participants you are recording. Several US states require all parties to consent to a recording, so the safest habit is to announce the AI note taker at the start of every meeting.
