# How to Record a Teams Meeting, Where the Recording Goes and How to Download It

*Last updated: October 13, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** Here is how to record a Teams meeting: select **More** (the 3 dots), then **Record and transcribe**, then **Start recording**. Recordings of regular meetings are saved to the organizer's OneDrive, channel meetings go to SharePoint, and by default most Teams recordings expire after 120 days.

## How to record a Teams meeting in 3 clicks

You can record a Teams meeting in 3 clicks once the meeting has started, as long as your account and your organization's policy allow recording. People often search "how to record Microsoft Team meeting", with Team singular, but the steps are the same. Here is how to record a Teams meeting in the Teams desktop app or Teams on the web:

1. **Join the meeting.** Recording starts from inside the call, not from the calendar.
2. **Select More** (the 3 dots) in the meeting controls at the top.
3. **Select Record and transcribe, then Start recording.** Everyone in the meeting sees a banner that recording has started.
4. **Stop it the same way:** More, then Record and transcribe, then Stop recording. Recording also stops when everyone leaves.

Teams usually starts a transcript with the recording. That gives you a searchable text version as well as the video, which helps when you need to find one sentence in a 60-minute call.

Most people who record team meetings every week skip the clicks entirely. If you want the recording to start on its own, the organizer can turn on automatic recording in the meeting options before the meeting begins.

## Who can record a Teams meeting?

In most organizations, 2 groups can record: the meeting organizer and people from the same organization. Guests and people from other organizations usually cannot start a Teams recording, and anonymous attendees never can.

Before you worry about how to record a Teams meeting, check 2 things outside your control:

- **Your license.** Recording needs a work or school Microsoft 365 plan that includes Teams meeting recording.
- **Your admin's meeting policy.** IT can switch recording off for everyone or for certain groups.

If you are a guest in a client's meeting, ask the organizer to record and share the file, or use one of the options further down that records from your own side.

## Why is the Teams Record button greyed out?

A greyed-out or missing Record button almost always means 1 of 4 things:

1. **You joined as a guest** or from another organization.
2. **Your admin has turned recording off** in the meeting policy.
3. **Your license does not include recording.**
4. **The organizer limited who can record** in the meeting options.

Knowing how to record a Teams meeting will not fix any of these from inside the meeting. Ask the organizer to record, ask IT to check the policy, or record your own audio another way, with everyone's agreement.

## Where are Teams recordings saved?

Where a Teams recording is saved depends on 1 thing: the type of meeting. For regular meetings, the recording goes to OneDrive. For channel meetings, it goes to SharePoint.

- **Regular (non-channel) meetings:** the file is saved in a **Recordings** folder in the meeting organizer's OneDrive. A link also appears in the meeting chat.
- **Channel meetings:** the file is saved to the channel's SharePoint site, in a Recordings folder inside the channel's files.

The fastest way to find any recording is the meeting chat. Open the meeting in your Teams calendar or chat list and select the recording link. For a deeper look at where files land and how the transcript works, see our guide to [AI note takers for Microsoft Teams](https://lynkk.ai/blog/ai-note-taker-microsoft-teams).

## How do you download a Teams recording?

You can download a Teams recording in 2 steps: open it from the meeting chat or from OneDrive, then select **Download** at the top of the player. Only some people see that button.

By default, the person who owns the recording (usually the organizer) and people with edit rights can download it. Other attendees can watch the recording in the browser but cannot download it. If you need to download Teams recording files you do not own, ask the owner to download it or to give you edit access.

Here is how to download Teams recording files from OneDrive, step by step:

1. **Open OneDrive** in your browser and go to the **Recordings** folder.
2. **Select the file,** then select **Download**.
3. **Save the MP4** to your computer.

Transcripts download separately. Open the meeting's **Recap** tab, go to the transcript, and choose to download it as a .docx or .vtt file.

## How long do Teams recordings last?

Most Teams recordings expire after 120 days by default, according to Microsoft Learn's page on the Teams recording expiration policy. Your admin can set a different default, or turn expiry off.

The recording owner, and anyone with edit rights, can change the expiry date in the file's details, or remove it. Teams warns the owner before a file expires. After it expires, the owner can usually restore it from the recycle bin for a limited time.

If a recording matters, such as a client decision, a board discussion or an interview, download it or change its expiry date the same day.

## Can you record a Teams meeting without being the organizer?

Yes, if you are 1 of the internal attendees and the policy allows it. Any internal attendee with recording rights can start a Teams recording, even if someone else set up the meeting.

If you are outside the organization, you usually cannot use the built-in Record button. So how to record a Teams meeting as a guest? Your options are to ask the organizer, or to use a tool that records from your own device, such as an app on your computer or a browser extension. Tell everyone first, because nobody will see a recording banner from Teams.

## Is it legal to record a Teams meeting?

Usually, yes, if people know. Teams shows a recording banner to everyone in the call, which helps, but it does not replace consent rules where you live.

In the US, federal law ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)) allows recording with the consent of 1 participant, but several states require everyone to agree. In the EU, a recording of identifiable people is personal data under the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), so you need a lawful basis and should tell people why you are recording.

The safe habit everywhere: say "I'm recording this for notes" at the start, and stop if someone objects. *This is general information, not legal advice.*

## FAQ

### How do I record a Teams meeting?
Join the meeting, select More (the 3 dots), then Record and transcribe, then Start recording. Everyone sees a banner that recording has started. To stop, open the same menu and select Stop recording. The recording also stops when everyone leaves the meeting.

### Where is my Teams recording saved?
Recordings of regular meetings are saved in 1 place: a Recordings folder in the organizer's OneDrive. A link appears in the meeting chat. Channel meeting recordings are saved to the channel's SharePoint site. The quickest way to find either is to open the meeting chat and select the recording.

### Why can't I download a Teams recording?
By default, only 2 groups see the Download button: the recording owner and people with edit rights. Other attendees can watch in the browser but cannot save a copy. Ask the owner, usually the organizer, to download it for you or to give you edit access to the file.

### Do Teams recordings get deleted?
Yes. Most Teams recordings expire after 120 days by default, though your admin can change that. The owner can change or remove the expiry date in the file details. Download any recording you need to keep, or extend its expiry the day of the meeting.

### Can guests record a Teams meeting?
Usually not. 3 groups normally cannot use the Teams Record button: guests, people from other organizations and anonymous attendees. Ask the organizer to record and share it, or, with everyone's agreement, record your own side with a tool on your computer or a browser extension.
