## What is a standup meeting?

A standup meeting is a short daily meeting, often 15 minutes, where a team checks progress toward a shared goal and surfaces anything blocking the work. The name comes from the habit of standing, which keeps the meeting short.

In practice, it is not a status report to a manager. It is a quick coordination point for the people doing the work, so they can adjust the plan for the day together.

## What is the daily standup meeting in Scrum?

The daily standup meeting in Scrum is the Daily Scrum, the standup meeting Scrum teams hold every working day: a 15-minute event for the Developers of the Scrum Team, held at the same time and place each day. Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the plan.

The 2020 Scrum Guide lets the Developers choose the structure, as long as the meeting focuses on progress and produces a plan for the next day of work. You can still use the 3 classic questions, covered below. *Source: The 2020 Scrum Guide by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland.*

## What is the purpose of a daily standup meeting?

A daily standup meeting has 3 purposes: to check progress toward the goal, to adjust the plan for the next 24 hours and to surface blockers early. Problems that would wait a week for the next review get raised the same day, while there is still time in the sprint to fix them.

Deeper problem-solving happens after the standup, with only the people involved. That keeps the check-in short and useful for everyone else.

## How long should a standup meeting last?

A standup meeting should last 15 minutes or less, which is the time box the Scrum Guide sets for the Daily Scrum. Teams outside Scrum use the same limit because it forces focus.

If your standups often run to 30 minutes, the usual cause is discussion that belongs in a follow-up. Note the topic, name the people who need to talk, and move on.

Three habits keep the time box: start on time even if someone is late, keep a visible timer, and let each speaker hand over to the next. Remote teams should share the board on screen and mute when not speaking, which also gives a cleaner recording.

## What should you say in a standup meeting?

A common format is 3 classic scrum standup meeting questions, with about 1 minute per person:

1. **What did I finish since the last standup?**
2. **What will I work on until the next one?**
3. **Is anything blocking me?**

Keep answers tied to the sprint goal, not a list of every task, and save details for after the meeting. Say blockers plainly, with what you need and from whom. A blocker named in the standup can be fixed the same day; a blocker kept quiet can cost the sprint.

## How do you run a standup meeting?

You run a good standup with a fixed 4-part agenda, the same time every day and a strict 15-minute limit. Here is a standup meeting agenda that works for most engineering teams:

1. **Start on time** with the sprint goal visible, for example on the board.
2. **Walk the board or go person by person**, using the 3 questions.
3. **Capture blockers and owners** as they come up.
4. **List follow-ups** for after the standup, with who needs to join.

The weak point is step 3. When nobody writes blockers down, they are forgotten by lunch, and the same blocker comes up again the next morning. This is where AI notes help most.

## Can standups be weekly?

Yes, standups can happen 1 day a week, but then they stop being a daily check-in and work more like a weekly sync. Scrum's Daily Scrum is held every working day, because the goal is to adapt the plan each day.

Teams that meet weekly often add an async check-in on the other 4 days, where people post their update in writing. If you do, keep 1 place where blockers and owners are tracked, so nothing is lost between the weekly meetings.

## FAQ

### Why is it called a standup meeting?
It gets its name because teams traditionally hold it standing up, which keeps it short. Standing makes long discussions uncomfortable, so people get to the point in about 1 minute each. Many remote teams keep the name even though everyone is sitting at a desk on a video call.

### How many people should be in a standup meeting?
A standup works best with a small team. The 2020 Scrum Guide says a Scrum Team is typically 10 or fewer people, and the Daily Scrum is for its Developers. If your standup has 15 or more people, split it by team and share the notes across groups instead.

### Are standups necessary?
Standups are worth it when a team works toward a shared goal and needs to adjust daily. They cost 15 minutes and can save days of blocked work. If updates are routine, try async written check-ins on some days, and keep 1 place, such as Lynkk, where blockers and owners are tracked.

### Should you record standups?
Recording helps teammates who miss the standup and makes blockers easy to track. ### What is the difference between a standup and a status meeting?
A standup is 15 minutes for the team to coordinate its own work, while a status meeting reports progress to someone else, often a manager. Standups focus on blockers and the next 24 hours. Status meetings tend to run longer and look backward, which is why teams keep the 2 separate.
