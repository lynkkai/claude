# How to Record Audio on Mac: 5 Free Ways for Voice, Screen and Internal Audio

*Last updated: October 13, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** Here is how to record audio on Mac for free: open QuickTime Player and choose **File > New Audio Recording**, or open **Voice Memos** and click the red Record button. To record audio and screen together, press **Shift-Command-5** and choose a microphone under Options. For both sides of a call, use a call recorder.

## How to record audio on Mac: which of the 5 ways should you use?

There are 5 free ways, and the best answer to how to record audio on Mac depends on what you want to capture. Pick by the job, not the app:

- **Your voice only:** QuickTime Player or Voice Memos.
- **Your screen with your voice:** the Shift-Command-5 toolbar.
- **Your webcam and voice:** QuickTime's New Movie Recording.
- **Both sides of a call:** a call recorder app.
- **Sound playing on the Mac itself (internal audio):** see the internal audio section below.

All 5 methods are free. The first 3 are built into macOS, so you can start in under a minute, without downloading anything, creating an account or changing a single setting. Easy.

## How do you record audio on Mac with QuickTime Player?

QuickTime Player is the quickest built-in answer to how to record audio on Mac. It takes 4 steps, according to Apple's QuickTime Player User Guide:

1. **Open QuickTime Player** from your Applications folder.
2. **Choose File > New Audio Recording.**
3. **Click the arrow next to the Record button** to pick a microphone and the recording quality.
4. **Click Record,** then click it again to stop. Option-click the button to pause instead.

Choose **File > Save** to name the file and pick a folder. QuickTime saves audio as an .m4a file, which plays on almost any device.

Tip: watch the level meter before you start, because a recording with the wrong input selected looks fine until you play it back an hour later. If it barely moves when you speak, choose a different microphone or move closer.

## Can you record audio on Mac with Voice Memos?

Yes, in 3 clicks. Voice Memos is the other built-in answer to how to record audio on Mac, and the easiest one for quick notes, lectures or interviews:

1. **Open Voice Memos** from Applications or Spotlight.
2. **Click the red Record button.**
3. **Click Done** when you finish.

Recordings are named automatically and listed in the sidebar. If you use the same Apple Account on your iPhone, your memos can sync between devices. To turn memos into text, see our guide to [voice memo transcription](https://lynkk.ai/blog/voice-memo-transcription).

## How do you record audio and screen on Mac?

Here is how to record audio and screen on Mac in 1 shortcut: press **Shift-Command-5**. Apple's guide to recording your screen on Mac describes 5 steps:

1. **Press Shift-Command-5** to open the screenshot toolbar.
2. **Choose Record Entire Screen or Record Selected Portion.**
3. **Click Options and choose a microphone.** This is the step people miss when they ask why their screen recording has no sound.
4. **Click Record.**
5. **Stop** with the Stop button in the menu bar, or press **Command-Control-Esc**.

The recording appears on your desktop as a video file with sound. QuickTime Player can do the same thing: choose **File > New Screen Recording** and it opens the same toolbar.

If you searched "screen record audio Mac" because your recording was silent, check step 3 first. The microphone setting is off until you pick one. Every time.

## How do you record yourself on Mac with the webcam?

If you want video of yourself, not just your voice, use QuickTime's movie recording. It takes 3 steps:

1. **Open QuickTime Player** and choose **File > New Movie Recording.**
2. **Click the arrow next to Record** and choose your camera and microphone.
3. **Click Record,** then click again to stop, and save the file.

This is the simplest answer to how to record myself on Mac for a video message, a pitch practice or a class assignment. Photo Booth also records webcam video, with fewer settings.

## How do you record internal audio on Mac?

If you are searching how to record internal audio on Mac, you mean 1 thing: the sound the Mac itself plays, such as a video, a song or the other people on a call. It is the hardest of the 5 jobs, because for years macOS let you pick only a microphone, not the system sound.

You have 3 options:

1. **Check your screen recording options.** On newer versions of macOS, the Shift-Command-5 options may include system audio. Look under Options before you install anything.
2. **Use a virtual audio driver.** These route the Mac's output into a recording app. They work, but setup takes time and a wrong setting can mute your speakers.
3. **Use a call recorder** if the sound you want is a call. This is the cleanest way to record audio from a Mac call, because it captures both sides without any audio routing.

macOS asks for permission the first time an app records sound. If nothing is captured, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and allow the app under **Microphone**, and under **Screen & System Audio Recording** (called Screen Recording in older versions).

## Is it legal to record a call on your Mac?

It depends on where you and the other people are. In the US, federal law ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)) allows recording with 1 participant's consent, but several states require everyone to agree. In the EU, a recording of identifiable people is personal data under the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

A recording made on your own Mac is invisible to everyone else, so the duty to tell them is all yours. Say "I'm recording this for notes" at the start. *This is general information, not legal advice.*

## How do you turn a Mac recording into text?

Once you know how to record audio on Mac, the next step is text. You have 2 routes: transcribe the file afterwards, or record with a tool that transcribes as it goes. A transcript makes a 60-minute recording searchable and easier to share, including with people who are deaf or hard of hearing; [Section508.gov](https://www.section508.gov/) explains why text versions of audio matter for accessibility.

For saved files, our guide on how to [transcribe audio to text](https://lynkk.ai/blog/transcribe-audio-to-text) covers the options. For calls, the Lynkk Mac app gives you the transcript in 17 languages, with speaker labels, as part of each Meeting Brief.

## FAQ

### What is the easiest way to record audio on a Mac?
The simplest answer to how to record audio on Mac is QuickTime Player: choose File, then New Audio Recording, and click Record. It takes 4 steps and saves an .m4a file. Voice Memos is just as easy: open it, click the red Record button, and click Done. Both are free and built into macOS.

### How do I record my screen with audio on a Mac?
Press Shift-Command-5, choose Record Entire Screen or Record Selected Portion, then click Options and pick a microphone before you click Record. Stop with the menu bar button or Command-Control-Esc. If the recording has no sound, the microphone was not selected in Options.

### Can a Mac record internal audio?
Yes, in 3 ways. Newer versions of macOS may show a system audio option in the Shift-Command-5 Options menu. Otherwise, a virtual audio driver can route sound into a recording app. For calls, a call recorder such as the Lynkk Mac app captures both sides without any audio routing.

### Why is my Mac recording silent?
Usually 1 of 2 things: no microphone was selected, or the app lacks permission. In the Shift-Command-5 toolbar, choose a microphone under Options. Then open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and allow the app under Microphone and under Screen & System Audio Recording.

### How do I record a Zoom or Teams call on a Mac?
Use a call recorder. The free Lynkk Mac app detects calls in 7 apps, including Zoom and Microsoft Teams, and shows a Record button. No bot joins, both sides are recorded on 2 channels, and each recording becomes notes with action items. Tell everyone on the call first.
