# Board Meeting Agenda Format: Free Template, 3 Sample Board Meeting Agendas and Robert's Rules Order

*Last updated: October 13, 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A standard board meeting agenda has 10 parts, in this order: call to order, roll call and quorum, approval of the agenda, approval of the last minutes, consent agenda, reports, unfinished business, new business, executive session if needed, and adjournment. Send it with the board packet 5 to 7 days ahead.

## What is a board meeting agenda?

A board meeting agenda is the written order of business for a meeting of a board of directors or trustees, usually in 10 standard parts. It lists every item the board will discuss or vote on, in sequence, usually with a time and a presenter for each one.

It does 3 jobs at once. It tells directors what to prepare, it keeps the chair on schedule, and it becomes the skeleton of the minutes. A clear board meeting agenda is also a record: it shows that the board saw an item before it voted.

Most boards follow the same order every time, based on *Robert's Rules of Order*. That consistency is the point. Directors know where to find the finances, and nothing important gets squeezed into the last 5 minutes.

## What is the standard board meeting agenda format?

The standard board meeting agenda format has 10 parts. Robert's Rules sets out the core "order of business" (minutes, reports, unfinished business, new business), and most boards wrap it with an opening and a close:

1. **Call to order.** The chair opens the meeting and notes the time.
2. **Roll call and quorum.** The secretary records who is present and confirms there are enough directors to vote. Your bylaws set the quorum.
3. **Approval of the agenda.** The board adopts the agenda, with any changes.
4. **Approval of the previous minutes.** Corrections first, then a vote.
5. **Consent agenda.** Routine items approved together in 1 vote.
6. **Reports.** Chair, CEO or executive director, treasurer and committees.
7. **Unfinished business.** Items carried over from earlier meetings, sometimes called old business.
8. **New business.** Decisions and discussions coming to the board for the first time.
9. **Executive session.** A closed session, if needed, for sensitive topics.
10. **Adjournment.** The chair closes the meeting and notes the time.

Some boards add announcements before adjournment, or public comment near the start. Keep the format of board meeting agenda items the same from meeting to meeting, so directors and minute-takers always know where they are.

## What is a consent agenda?

A consent agenda groups routine, non-controversial items so the board approves them all with 1 motion and 1 vote. Typical items include the previous minutes, committee reports that need no action, routine contracts and staff appointments.

It saves 15 to 30 minutes in most meetings, time that moves to strategy and new business. The rule that makes it work: any director can ask to pull an item off the consent agenda for discussion, and that item then gets its own vote.

Send consent items in the board packet, with enough detail that directors can read them beforehand. A consent agenda only works if people actually read it.

## What happens in an executive session?

An executive session is a closed part of a board meeting, usually at the end, where directors meet without staff, guests or the public. Boards use it for about 4 kinds of topics: CEO or executive director performance and pay, legal matters, personnel issues and sensitive negotiations.

Put "Executive session (if needed)" on every board meeting agenda, even when you expect not to use it. That way, holding one never signals that something is wrong.

Two practical points. Minutes of an executive session are usually brief and kept separately, often recording only decisions. And public bodies, such as school boards and city councils, are bound by open meeting laws that limit when they can close a session, so check the rules for your board.

## Free board meeting agenda template you can copy

This board meeting agenda template follows the 10-part format for a 90-minute meeting. Adjust the times to fit your board.

**[Organization name] Board of Directors meeting**

**Date and time:** [date, start to end] · **Location or link:** [room or link]

**Chair:** [name] · **Secretary:** [name]

| Time | Item | Presenter | Action |

| 0:10 | 5. Consent agenda | Chair | Vote |
| 0:15 | 6. Reports: chair, CEO, treasurer, committees | Each officer | Information |

**Attachments:** previous minutes, financial statements, committee reports, any resolutions to be voted on.

Mark each item as "Vote", "Discuss" or "Information". It tells directors which items need a decision, which is the single most useful thing a board meeting agenda can do.

## What does a sample board meeting agenda look like?

Here are 3 sample board meeting agendas, each adapted from the template above. Use the one closest to your board, then change the items, not the order.

### 1. Sample nonprofit board meeting agenda (90 minutes)

This sample nonprofit board meeting agenda covers the items most nonprofit boards handle every quarter.

1. Call to order (2 min)
2. Roll call and quorum (3 min)
3. Approval of the agenda and previous minutes (5 min)
4. Consent agenda: committee reports, volunteer policy update (5 min)
5. Executive director's report: programs, staffing, fundraising (15 min)
6. Treasurer's report: financial statements and budget to actual (15 min)
7. Committee reports needing action: governance, finance, development (15 min)
8. Unfinished business: annual fundraising plan (10 min)
9. New business: approve the annual conflict of interest disclosures (10 min)
10. Executive session (if needed) and adjournment (10 min)

Nonprofits in the US should keep good minutes of each meeting. The IRS asks on Form 990 whether the organization documented its governing body's meetings at the time, and its [governance guidance for charities](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/governance_practices.pdf) covers minutes, conflict of interest policies and board oversight. More resources are on the [IRS charities and nonprofits page](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits).

### 2. Startup board meeting agenda example (2 hours)

A startup board meeting agenda example puts the numbers early and spends most of the time on 1 or 2 strategic questions.

1. Call to order and quorum (5 min)
2. Approval of the previous minutes (5 min)
3. CEO update: highlights, lowlights, key metrics (20 min)
4. Financials: cash, burn rate and runway (20 min)
5. Deep dive: 1 strategic topic, such as pricing, a market or a hire (40 min)
6. Consent agenda: option grants, ratifications of earlier actions (5 min)
7. Open questions from directors (10 min)
8. Executive session without management (15 min)

Send the board deck at least 3 days ahead, so the meeting is discussion, not a presentation. Founders who run tight board meetings usually run tight notes too; see our guide to [meeting notes for founders](https://lynkk.ai/blog/founders-meeting-notes).

### 3. Association or club board meeting agenda (60 minutes)

Associations, clubs and homeowner boards often follow Robert's Rules closely, with motions on every item.

1. Call to order and roll call (5 min)
2. Reading and approval of the minutes (5 min)
3. Reports of officers: president, treasurer, secretary (15 min)
4. Reports of committees (10 min)
5. Unfinished business (10 min)
6. New business (10 min)
7. Announcements and adjournment (5 min)

## How far in advance should you send a board meeting agenda?

Send the board meeting agenda with the board packet 5 to 7 days before the meeting. That gives directors time to read financials, reports and resolutions, and to ask questions before the meeting instead of during it.

Your bylaws may set a minimum notice period for meetings, so check them. Public bodies may also have legal notice rules. Whatever the timing, lock the agenda once it is sent; new items should go through the chair, not appear on the day.

## What are the most common board meeting agenda mistakes?

Most weak agendas fail in 1 of 5 ways, and each one is easy to fix before the packet goes out:

- **Too many reports.** If 45 of 90 minutes are reports read aloud, move them to the packet and take questions only.
- **No action labels.** Directors cannot tell which items need a vote, so they arrive unprepared to decide.
- **New business last, with no time left.** Put the most important decision before the reports if it needs the board's full attention.
- **No executive session slot.** Adding one only when there is a problem makes every closed session look like a crisis.
- **A different order every meeting.** A consistent board meeting agenda format saves minute-takers and new directors real effort.

Review the agenda against this list for 2 minutes before you send it.

## How do you write board meeting minutes from the agenda?

Write the minutes in the same order as the board meeting agenda. For each item, record 3 things: a short summary, any motion with who moved and seconded it, and the result of the vote. Then list action items with an owner and a due date.

Minutes are a record of what the board decided, not a transcript of what everyone said. Keep discussion short and decisions exact.

## Do you need consent to record a board meeting?

Usually, yes. Tell directors at the start that the meeting is recorded, and why. In the US, federal law ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)) allows recording with 1 participant's consent, but several states require everyone to agree. In the EU, a recording of identifiable people is personal data under the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

Many boards adopt a short policy: who may record, how long recordings are kept, and whether they are deleted once the minutes are approved. *This is general information, not legal advice.*

## FAQ

### What is the correct order of a board meeting agenda?
The usual order has 10 parts: call to order, roll call and quorum, approval of the agenda, approval of the previous minutes, consent agenda, reports, unfinished business, new business, executive session if needed, and adjournment. It follows Robert's Rules' order of business, with an opening and a close around it.

### What should be included in a board meeting agenda?
Include the meeting date, time and location, the 10 standard items in order, a time and presenter for each item, and whether each one needs a vote, discussion or is for information only. Attach the previous minutes, financial statements, committee reports and any resolutions in the board packet.

### How long should a board meeting be?
Most board meetings run 60 to 120 minutes. Nonprofit and association boards often fit in 90 minutes with a consent agenda. Startup boards usually need about 2 hours, with most of it on 1 or 2 strategic topics. If meetings regularly run longer, move routine items into the consent agenda.

### Who prepares the board meeting agenda?
The board chair usually sets the agenda, working with 2 people: the CEO or executive director and the board secretary. Directors can ask the chair to add items before the agenda is sent. Once it goes out with the board packet, 5 to 7 days ahead, new items should go through the chair.

### What is the difference between old business and new business?
They are 2 separate agenda items. Old business, or unfinished business, covers items the board started at an earlier meeting but did not finish, such as a postponed vote. New business covers items coming to the board for the first time. Keeping them separate on the agenda stops carried-over items from being forgotten.

## Conclusion

A good board meeting agenda follows the same 10-part format every time: call to order, quorum, agenda, minutes, consent agenda, reports, unfinished business, new business, executive session and adjournment.
