# How to Record a Teams Meeting (and Find, Download and Transcribe It)

Recording a Microsoft Teams meeting takes two clicks once you know where the button is. The harder part comes after: finding the file, getting a copy you can keep, and turning an hour of video into something your team will actually use.

> Microsoft updates the Teams interface often. The steps below match the current Teams desktop app. If a label looks slightly different on your screen, look for the same option under **More** (the three dots).

## How to record a Teams meeting on desktop

1. **Join the meeting** from the Teams desktop app or Teams on the web.
2. In the meeting controls at the top of the window, select **More** (the **...** icon).
3. Select **Record and transcribe**, then **Start recording**.
4. Everyone in the meeting sees a banner telling them the meeting is being recorded. It is good practice to say it out loud too.
5. To stop, go back to **More** > **Record and transcribe** > **Stop recording**. Recording also stops automatically when everyone leaves the meeting.

That is it. When the meeting ends, the recording appears in the meeting chat once Teams has finished processing it, which usually takes a few minutes for a typical meeting.

### Record every meeting in a series automatically

If you organize a recurring meeting and want every session recorded:

1. Open the meeting in your Teams calendar.
2. Select **Meeting options**.
3. Turn on **Record and transcribe automatically** (your admin needs to allow this option).
4. Save.

### Record a Teams call (not a meeting)

One to one and group calls work the same way. During the call, select **More** > **Record and transcribe** > **Start recording**.

## How to record on Teams mobile

On the Teams app for iPhone, iPad or Android:

1. Join the meeting.
2. Tap **More** (**...**) in the meeting controls.
3. Tap **Start recording**.
4. To stop, tap **More** > **Stop recording**.

The recording is saved to the same place as a desktop recording, so you can open it later on any device.

## Why the Record button is missing or greyed out

This is the most common Teams recording question, and the answer is almost always one of these:

| Reason | What it means | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| **You are a guest or external attendee** | Only the organizer and people from the organizer's organization can start a recording. | Ask the organizer to record, or use a tool that records from your side (see below). |
| **Your license does not include recording** | Recording needs a work or school account with an eligible Microsoft 365 or Office 365 license, such as Business Basic, Business Standard, Business Premium, or an E or A plan. The full experience is not available on personal Teams accounts. | Ask your IT team which license you have. |
| **Your admin turned recording off** | Teams admins control recording and transcription through meeting policies. | Ask IT to enable recording for your account. |
| **The organizer limited who can record** | The **Who can record and transcribe** meeting option can restrict recording to organizers and co-organizers. | Ask the organizer to make you a co-organizer or change the option. |
| **Someone is already recording** | Only one recording runs at a time. | Check for the recording banner. |

If you are on the other side of a customer's or partner's Teams meeting, you often hit the first row. That is one of the main reasons teams use a separate meeting recorder (more on that below).

## Where Teams recordings are saved

Teams no longer uses the old Microsoft Stream (Classic) for new recordings. Recordings are saved to OneDrive or SharePoint:

- **Regular (non-channel) meetings and calls:** saved to the meeting organizer's **OneDrive**, in a folder called **Recordings**.
- **Channel meetings:** saved to the **SharePoint** site for that team, in the channel's **Recordings** folder.

You can also reach any recording from:

- The **meeting chat**, where a link appears after the meeting.
- The meeting in your **Teams calendar**, under the **Recap** tab.

### Teams recordings expire

By default, new Teams meeting recordings expire after **120 days** and are moved to the recycle bin. Your admin can change the default, and the recording owner can change the expiry date for an individual recording from the file's details. After expiry, the owner can usually still recover the file from the recycle bin for a limited time.

If a recording matters (a board meeting, a customer commitment, an interview), download a copy or move the notes somewhere permanent. **Downloaded copies do not expire.**

## How to download a Teams recording

### If you own the recording (you organized or recorded the meeting)

1. Open the **meeting chat** and select the recording, or open it from the **Recap** tab.
2. The recording opens in the web player.
3. Select **Download** at the top of the player.
4. The file saves to your computer as an **MP4**.

Or go straight to the file:

1. Open **OneDrive** (or the channel's **SharePoint** site for channel meetings).
2. Open the **Recordings** folder.
3. Select the recording, then select **Download**.

### If you were only an attendee

By default, attendees can **watch** a recording but **cannot download** it. Downloading is limited to the owners (typically the organizer and co-organizers). If you need a copy:

- Ask the organizer to download it and share the file, or
- Ask the organizer to give you edit permission on the file in OneDrive or SharePoint.

### Download the transcript too

If transcription was on, the organizer can download the transcript from the **Recap** tab as a **.docx** or **.vtt** file. That is often more useful than the video itself, because you can search it and paste quotes from it.

## Are Teams calls recorded automatically?

**Not by default.** A Teams meeting or call is only recorded when:

- Someone starts a recording manually, or
- The organizer turned on **Record and transcribe automatically** for that meeting, or
- Your organization uses **compliance recording**, which some regulated industries (for example, financial services) set up for specific roles.

In normal meetings, every participant sees a notification when recording starts, and it is listed in the meeting chat afterwards. Some organizations also require participants to explicitly agree to being recorded before they can unmute or turn on video.

Whatever tool you use, the rule is the same: **tell people you are recording, and follow your company's policy and local law.** In many places, recording a conversation without consent is not allowed. This article is not legal advice.

## Quick answers

**Can I record a Teams meeting if I am not the organizer?**
Yes, if you are in the same organization as the organizer, your license supports recording, and the meeting options allow it. Guests and external attendees cannot start a Teams recording.

**How long does a Teams recording take to appear?**
Usually a few minutes after the meeting ends. Longer meetings take longer to process.

**Can I record a Teams meeting without anyone knowing?**
No. Teams shows a recording notification to everyone. You should always tell participants you are recording, whichever tool you use.

**Why can't I download a Teams recording?**
By default only the recording's owners (usually the organizer) can download it. Attendees can watch but need the organizer to share a copy or grant edit access.

**Where do Teams recordings go?**
Regular meetings save to the organizer's OneDrive in the Recordings folder. Channel meetings save to the team's SharePoint site.

**Do Teams recordings get deleted?**
New recordings expire after 120 days by default unless your admin or the recording owner changes it. Downloaded copies do not expire.
