# Action Items: What They Are, How to Write Them, and a Free Template

Most meetings end with a sense that things were agreed. A week later, nobody can say who was supposed to do what. The fix is not more meetings. It is better **action items**.

This guide covers what an action item is, the four parts every good one has, examples you can copy, and a free action items template you can paste into any doc or tracker.

## What is an action item?

An **action item** is a specific task that comes out of a meeting or conversation, with **one owner** and a **due date**.

It is the bridge between talking and doing. A decision says *what* the team agreed. An action item says *who will make it happen, and by when*.

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **Discussion** | "Onboarding is too long, people are dropping off at step 3." |
| **Decision** | "We will cut onboarding from five steps to three." |
| **Action item** | "Priya to share a three-step onboarding mockup in #design by Thursday." |

Only the last one gets done on its own.

## The 4 parts of a good action item

Every action item that actually gets finished has these four parts:

1. **A verb.** Start with what will be done: *send, draft, book, fix, decide, review, share.* "Pricing page" is a topic. "Draft the new pricing page copy" is an action.
2. **One owner.** A single named person. Not "the team", not "marketing", not "Sam and Alex". If two people are involved, one of them owns it.
3. **A due date.** A real date, not "soon" or "next sprint". "By Friday 9 October" is better than "end of week".
4. **A clear finish line.** How will everyone know it is done? "Share in #launch channel", "Ticket moved to Done", "Sent to the customer".

A handy test: **could someone who missed the meeting read this action item and know exactly what to do?** If not, rewrite it.

### Bad vs good action items

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Look into the bug | Rahul to reproduce the checkout bug on Android and post findings in JIRA-412 by Wed 7 Oct |
| Follow up with client | Maya to email Acme the revised proposal with the 12 month pricing option by Tue 6 Oct |
| Marketing to think about launch | Leo to draft three launch email subject lines and share in #launch by Mon 5 Oct |
| Fix onboarding | Priya to share a three-step onboarding mockup in #design by Thu 8 Oct |
| Hiring | Jo to book the final round with candidate B for next week and confirm in the hiring channel by Fri 9 Oct |

### Table version

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Status options:** Not started, In progress, Blocked, Done.

### Plain text version (for chat or email)

```
Meeting: [name], [date]
Attendees: [names]

Decisions
- [decision 1]
- [decision 2]

Action items
- [Owner] to [verb + task] by [date]. Done when: [finish line]
- [Owner] to [verb + task] by [date]. Done when: [finish line]
- [Owner] to [verb + task] by [date]. Done when: [finish line]

Open questions (to discuss next time)
- [question]
```

Send it within an hour of the meeting. The longer you wait, the more people's memories drift apart.

## How to track action items so they actually get done

Writing good action items is half the job. The other half is making sure they do not die in a doc nobody opens.

1. **Read them back at the end of the meeting.** Take the last two minutes to read each action item out loud and get a "yes" from each owner. This catches most misunderstandings.
2. **Put them where the work happens.** If your team lives in Jira, the action item should be a Jira ticket. If it lives in a task tool, put it there. A separate list of "meeting tasks" gets ignored.
3. **Start the next meeting with last meeting's action items.** Five minutes of "done, not done, blocked" keeps everyone honest without nagging.
4. **Keep the link to where it was agreed.** Three weeks later, someone will ask "why are we doing this?" A link to the moment in the meeting where it was decided ends the debate.
5. **Close the loop.** When something is done, mark it done and tell the people who were waiting on it.

## Spreadsheet vs action item tracker vs AI

| Option | Best for | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| **Doc or spreadsheet template** | Small teams, few meetings | Someone has to take notes and type it all up, every time |
| **Project management tool** (Jira, Asana, Linear) | Tracking tasks once they exist | Does not capture the task from the conversation in the first place |
| **AI action item tracker** | Teams with lots of meetings | Needs consent to record, and you should still review what it captured |

The hardest step is not tracking. It is **capturing**: someone has to listen, notice "I'll send that by Friday", and write it down correctly while also taking part in the conversation. That is the step most worth automating.

## Quick answers

**What is the difference between an action item and a task?**
An action item is a task that came out of a meeting or conversation. Once it is captured with an owner and due date, it usually becomes a task in your project tool.

**Who should own action items?**
One named person per item, ideally the person who will do the work. The meeting organizer is responsible for making sure every action item has an owner before the meeting ends.

**How many action items should a meeting have?**
There is no magic number, but if a 30 minute meeting produces fifteen action items, many of them are probably topics, not tasks. If a meeting produces zero, ask whether it needed to be a meeting.

**Is it "action items" or "actions items"?**
"Action items" is correct. "Actions items" is a common typo.
