# Typing Is the Bottleneck. What Voice-to-Text Actually Fixes, and What It Doesn't.

*This story was written with AI assistance and edited by the Lynkk team.*

Most people speak far faster than they type. That gap is the whole pitch for voice-to-text: talk, and the words appear.

It's a good pitch, and for some work it's completely true. For other work it makes things worse. The difference has less to do with which app you pick than with what kind of writing you're doing.

## Where dictation genuinely wins

**First drafts.** The hardest part of writing is getting something down. Speaking lowers the bar. You ramble, you repeat yourself, and then you have 600 words to edit instead of a blinking cursor.

**Replies you've been avoiding.** That email you've been putting off for three days takes ninety seconds to say out loud. Editing it takes another two minutes.

**Notes right after a conversation.** Walking out of a meeting and saying "Okay, three things: Sam owns the pricing draft, the customer wants a case study, and we never agreed on the launch date" is faster and more complete than typing it later.

**Anyone whose hands are the problem.** Repetitive strain, injury, or just a long day. Dictation isn't a productivity trick here; it's an accessibility tool, and a good one.

## Where dictation loses

**Precise editing.** Changing one word in the middle of a sentence by voice is slower than reaching for the mouse. Most people end up dictating and then editing with the keyboard, which is the right split.

**Code and structured text.** Variable names, punctuation-heavy syntax and tables are painful to speak. There are tools for voice coding, but they take real practice.

**Open-plan offices.** Nobody wants to hear you dictate your performance review.

**Thinking on the page.** Some writing is a way of figuring out what you think. If you need to stare at a sentence and rearrange it, speaking won't help.

## You probably already own a good dictation tool

Before buying anything, try the free one on your device:

- **Mac:** Dictation is built in. Press the dictation key (or Fn twice, depending on your settings) in almost any text field. Our guide to [dictation on Mac](https://lynkk.ai/blog/dictation-on-mac) covers turning it on and six fixes for when it stops working.
- **Windows:** Press Windows key + H to start voice typing in most apps.
- **Google Docs:** Tools, then Voice typing, in Chrome.
- **Microsoft Word:** The Dictate button on the Home tab, for Microsoft 365 users. Here's how to [dictate in Microsoft Word](https://lynkk.ai/blog/dictate-in-microsoft-word), including the voice commands for punctuation and new lines.

For many people that's enough. The built-in tools have improved a lot, and they cost nothing.

## When a paid app is worth it

Free dictation tends to fall short in the same few places:

1. **It's tied to one app or one browser.** You want it everywhere, including in Slack, your email client and your notes app.
2. **It doesn't clean up.** You get "um, so, the, the thing is" verbatim. Better tools remove filler words and fix punctuation.
3. **It struggles with your vocabulary.** Product names, client names, medical or legal terms. A custom dictionary helps a lot.
4. **It handles one language at a time.** If you switch between, say, Hindi and English in the same sentence, many tools fall apart.

If two or more of those describe your day, a dedicated app is worth trying. The Lynkk team compared [7 voice-to-text apps](https://lynkk.ai/blog/voice-to-text-apps), from Apple Dictation and Windows voice typing to Dragon, on what each does well and what it costs.

## A simple way to start

Don't try to dictate everything. Try this for one week:

1. **Pick one kind of writing**: email replies, meeting notes or first drafts. Just one.
2. **Dictate the draft, edit with the keyboard.** Don't try to fix mistakes by voice.
3. **Speak in full sentences and say the punctuation** ("comma", "period", "new paragraph") until the tool learns your rhythm.
4. **Keep a list of words it gets wrong**, then add them to the custom dictionary if your tool has one.

At the end of the week, compare how long that kind of writing took. If it saved time, add a second kind. If it didn't, you've lost nothing but a week of slightly odd emails.

## The honest summary

Voice-to-text is a first-draft machine. It's excellent at getting words out of your head and bad at polishing them. Use it to start, use the keyboard to finish, and you'll get most of the speed with none of the frustration.
