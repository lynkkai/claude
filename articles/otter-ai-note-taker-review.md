## What is the Otter AI note taker and how does it work?

The Otter.ai note taker is an AI meeting assistant that records, transcribes and summarizes conversations, and it has been on the market for more than 5 years. Its bot, OtterPilot, joins Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams calls from your calendar and shows a live transcript while people talk.

After the call, the Otter.ai note taker writes a summary and lists action items, and you can chat with the transcript to ask follow-up questions. Otter also has apps for iPhone and Android, so people use it for lectures and in-person conversations as well as video calls.

That makes it a good fit if your main need is a readable transcript. Teams that need the follow-up done for them often start looking for an Otter.ai alternative, which is where Lynkk comes in.

### How do they record meetings?

Lynkk gives you 4 ways to record, and 2 of them use no bot at all. The [Lynkk Mac app](https://lynkk.ai/download) notices calls in Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, WhatsApp, FaceTime and Webex and records both sides on 2 channels, so your side is labeled "You".

The [Chrome extension](https://lynkk.ai/features/chrome-extension) records Meet, Zoom (web) and Teams in a browser tab with no extra participant. When a bot is fine, the [Lynkk meeting bot](https://lynkk.ai/features/meeting-bot) joins from your calendar for free, audio only. For in-person meetings, you can record your microphone in the browser at lynkk.ai.

The Otter.ai note taker relies mostly on OtterPilot for online meetings, which some clients and guests notice in the participant list.

### What do the notes look like?

Every Lynkk recording produces a Meeting Brief with 7 parts: title, abstract, key takeaways with timestamps, topics, action items, decisions with context and open questions. Each action item has an owner, a due date and a priority, and every timestamp plays the recording from that moment.

Otter's summaries are clear and quick to read, with an outline and action items. In our comparison, the difference was structure: Lynkk separates decisions and open questions, which is what people look for a week later.

### What happens before and during the call?

Lynkk sends a morning briefing email on days you have meetings, covering 3 things for each meeting once Google Calendar is connected: who you are meeting, the most recent note with those people and how many action items are still open between you.

During the call, the Lynkk bot shows a live transcript panel, and the Mac app shows live words in a slim column on the edge of your screen. Otter's live transcript is one of its best features and works well for reading along.

### What happens after the meeting?

Lynkk turns action items into tasks on a board (open, in progress, in review, done) and lets you send them to Jira from the note, 1 issue per action item. On Pro, [Ask Lynkk](https://lynkk.ai/features/knowledge-graph) answers questions from your own notes with citations that open the note at the right moment.

Otter's AI chat answers questions about your meetings too. Its Business plan adds unlimited file imports, which matters if you have old recordings to transcribe.

## How do you remove the Otter.ai note taker from Zoom or Teams?

You can stop the Otter.ai note taker joining your calls in 3 ways: turn off auto-join for OtterPilot in Otter's meeting settings, disconnect your calendar from Otter, or remove the bot from the participant list as the meeting host. The first option stops it for every future meeting.

If other people's OtterPilot bots keep joining your meetings, hosts on Zoom and Teams can admit people from a waiting room or lobby and remove unexpected participants. Ask colleagues to switch off auto-join if it happens often.

## Is it legal to record meetings with an AI note taker?

Recording is legal in most of the US when at least 1 person on the call agrees, but some states require every participant to consent. Federal law ([18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2511)) allows 1 party consent, while California ([Penal Code § 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632)) requires all parties to agree.

The safe habit with any AI note taker, Otter or Lynkk, is to tell everyone at the start that the call is being recorded. For vendor reviews, the [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework) is a practical checklist. Lynkk keeps recordings private to your account and never trains on your conversations.

*This is general information, not legal advice.*
