# What Gong Costs, and What a 10-Person Sales Team Actually Needs

*Conversation intelligence was built for big revenue teams. If you're small, start with the problem, not the platform.*

Welcome back to **After the Call**. This week we're talking about money: specifically, the moment a growing sales team decides it needs "something like Gong."

## The question behind the question

When a founder or first sales leader asks "how much does Gong cost?", they're rarely just asking about price. They're asking: *Do we need this yet? What would we get? What would we miss without it?*

Price is the easy part to research and the hard part to pin down, because Gong doesn't publish its pricing. Deals are negotiated, and reported figures typically involve a per-user license plus a platform fee, often on annual contracts. We pulled together what's publicly reported in [what Gong costs in 2026](https://lynkk.ai/blog/gong-pricing), including what that tends to mean for a 10-person team. Treat any figure you see (including ours) as an estimate, and get a quote.

The more useful question is what problem you're trying to solve.

## What conversation intelligence platforms actually do

Platforms like Gong do several jobs at once:

1. **Record and transcribe** sales calls.
2. **Summarize** them and extract next steps.
3. **Analyze patterns** across many calls: talk-to-listen ratios, competitor mentions, objections, topics that correlate with won deals.
4. **Coach**: managers review calls, leave comments, build libraries of good examples.
5. **Forecast and manage deals**: flag risk based on activity and what was said.

Jobs 1 and 2 are now available in many tools at a fraction of the price. Jobs 3 to 5 are where platforms earn their cost, and they get more valuable the more calls and reps you have. Pattern analysis on 40 calls a month says less than on 4,000.

## What a 10-person team usually needs

At around ten reps, the biggest problems tend to be surprisingly basic:

- **Context lost between calls.** The rep can't remember what the customer said three weeks ago.
- **Handoffs fail.** An SDR's discovery notes don't reach the AE; the AE's promises don't reach customer success.
- **Managers can't see deals.** Pipeline reviews depend on the rep's memory and optimism.
- **The CRM is stale.** Nobody wants to type notes after every call.

None of these require pattern analysis across thousands of calls. They require **every call to be captured, structured and searchable**, so anyone on the team can find what the customer said.

## A simple way to decide

Ask yourself three questions:

**1. Is your biggest problem memory or method?**
If reps forget what customers said, you need capture and search. If reps remember perfectly but don't know how to handle objections, you need coaching. Coaching is where full platforms shine.

**2. How many calls do you record a month?**
Below a few hundred, pattern analytics tend to be noisy. Above a few thousand, they start telling you things you didn't know.

**3. Who will use the insights?**
If nobody has time to review dashboards, you're paying for reports nobody reads.

If your answers are "memory," "not many yet" and "the reps themselves," start with a notetaker that captures every call and lets reps ask questions across past calls. Upgrade to a full platform when you have the volume and a manager with time to coach.

Buy for the problem you have now, not the one you'll have at 50 reps. For most small teams, that problem is memory, and memory is cheap to fix.
