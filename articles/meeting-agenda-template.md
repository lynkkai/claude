# Meeting Agenda Template: 10 Free Examples You Can Copy Into Word or Google Docs

*Last updated: 24 November 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A meeting agenda template is a one-page outline with 5 parts: the meeting goal, attendees, timed topics with an owner for each, decisions needed, and action items. Send it 24 to 48 hours before the meeting so people arrive prepared.

A **meeting agenda template** is a reusable outline that tells attendees what a meeting is for, what will be discussed, who leads each topic and how long it takes. Flowtrace found that [67% of professionals say a clear agenda is the most important element of an effective meeting](https://www.flowtrace.co/collaboration-blog/50-meeting-statistics), yet only 37% of meetings actively use one.

Below is a simple template you can copy, 10 example agendas for common meetings, and how to set it up in Word, Google Docs, Excel or PowerPoint.

## What should a meeting agenda include?

A meeting agenda should include 7 elements: title, date and time, goal, attendees, timed topics with owners, decisions needed, and action items. If an item has no owner or no time limit, it will run over.

| Element | What to write |
|---|---|
| Title, date, time, link | "Product sync, Tue 10:00 to 10:30, Zoom link" |
| Goal | One sentence: what must be true when the meeting ends |
| Attendees and roles | Facilitator, note taker, decision maker |
| Topics | 3 to 5 items, each with an owner and minutes |
| Pre-reads | Links people should open before joining |
| Decisions needed | The questions this meeting must answer |
| Action items | Owner, task, due date (filled in during the meeting) |

## What does a simple meeting agenda template look like?

A simple meeting agenda template fits on half a page and works for any meeting under 60 minutes. Copy this version and replace the bracketed text:

> **[Meeting name]**
> **Date and time:** [Day, date, start to end time]
> **Location or link:** [Room or video link]
> **Facilitator:** [Name] | **Note taker:** [Name]
>
> **Goal:** By the end of this meeting we will [decide / agree / plan] [outcome].
>
> **Pre-read:** [Link]
>
> | # | Topic | Owner | Time |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | Welcome and goal | [Name] | 2 min |
> | 2 | [Topic] | [Name] | 10 min |
> | 3 | [Topic] | [Name] | 10 min |
> | 4 | [Topic] | [Name] | 5 min |
> | 5 | Decisions and action items | [Name] | 3 min |
>
> **Decisions:** [Filled in during meeting]
> **Action items:** [Owner, task, due date]

## How do you write a meeting agenda in 5 steps?

You write a meeting agenda in 5 steps: define the goal, list the topics, add owners and times, attach pre-reads, and send it 24 to 48 hours ahead. The whole process takes about 10 minutes once you have a template.

1. **Define the goal.** Write one sentence that starts with "By the end of this meeting we will..."
2. **List 3 to 5 topics.** Ask attendees to add items, then cut anything that can be an email.
3. **Add an owner and a time to each topic.** Order them by importance, not by habit.
4. **Attach pre-reads.** Link documents so the meeting is spent deciding, not reading.
5. **Send it early.** Share the agenda with the calendar invite, at least 1 working day ahead.

Harvard Business Review found that [71% of senior managers say meetings are unproductive and inefficient](https://hbr.org/2017/07/stop-the-meeting-madness). A goal and a timed agenda are the cheapest fix.

## How do you make a meeting agenda template in Word, Google Docs, Excel or PowerPoint?

You can turn the template above into a reusable file in any of the 4 common tools in under 5 minutes. Paste it once, format the table, and save it as a template.

| Tool | How to set it up |
|---|---|
| Microsoft Word | Paste the template, then File > Save As > Word Template (.dotx) |
| Google Docs | Paste the template and save it in a shared "Templates" folder; type "@meeting notes" to insert a notes block linked to a calendar event |
| Excel or Google Sheets | One row per topic with columns for owner, minutes and notes |
| PowerPoint or Canva | One slide per topic for presentation-heavy meetings |

Word and Google Docs work best for most teams because attendees can comment on the agenda before the meeting.

## How long before a meeting should you send the agenda?

Send the agenda 24 to 48 hours before the meeting, and at least 1 week ahead for board or committee meetings that need pre-reads. Late agendas mean people arrive unprepared and the first 10 minutes become a briefing.

### How long should a meeting agenda be?

A meeting agenda should fit on 1 page. If it needs 2 or more pages, the meeting has too many topics, or the details belong in a pre-read document.

### Can a meeting agenda be changed during the meeting?

Yes, the facilitator can change the order or drop an item during the meeting if attendees agree. Note the change in the meeting notes and move dropped items to the next agenda so nothing is lost.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between an agenda and minutes?

An agenda is the plan sent before a meeting, listing topics and times. Minutes are the record written after it, listing decisions and actions. See our [meeting minutes format](https://lynkk.ai/blog/meeting-minutes-sample) guide for the second half of the loop.

### How many topics should a meeting agenda have?

A 30-minute meeting should have 3 to 4 topics and a 60-minute meeting no more than 6. Fewer topics with clear owners beat a long list nobody finishes.

### Who should write the meeting agenda?

The meeting organizer or facilitator writes the agenda, but attendees should be able to add items at least 1 day before. Shared documents make this easy.

### Do meeting agendas need to be approved?

Formal meetings, such as board, committee and HOA meetings, usually approve the agenda as the first item. Most internal team meetings skip this step.

### What makes a good meeting agenda?

A good meeting agenda has 1 clear goal, 3 to 5 timed topics, an owner for each, links to pre-reads, and space for decisions and action items. It is sent at least 24 hours ahead.
