Quick answer: a good meeting minutes template has five parts: the meeting basics (date, attendees, purpose), key decisions, an action items table where every task has one owner and one due date, open questions, and the next meeting date. Below you'll find a free template you can copy, a shorter meeting notes template for everyday team meetings, a filled-in example, and the six steps to write minutes people actually read.

Most minutes fail for one simple reason: they describe the conversation instead of the outcome. Three days later, nobody remembers who promised what, and the same topic comes back at the next meeting. Everything in this guide is designed to fix that.

What are meeting minutes?

Meeting minutes (also called minutes of meeting, or MoM) are the written record of a meeting: who attended, what was decided, and what happens next. They're sent to attendees and to anyone who missed the meeting, and they become the reference point when someone later asks "what did we agree?"

Minutes don't need to capture every sentence. Good minutes capture decisions and action items, plus just enough discussion to explain them.

What are action items in a meeting?

An action item is a specific task that someone agreed to do as a result of a meeting. It has a clear owner, a deadline, and a definition of done. "Look into pricing" is a topic. "Sara sends three pricing options to the team by Friday" is an action item.

They're the most important part of any minutes, because they're the only part that creates work after the meeting ends. That's why they get their own table in the template below.

Meeting notes vs meeting minutes: what's the difference?

Meeting notes Meeting minutes
Purpose Help the team remember and act Official record of what was decided
Style Informal, short, bullet points Formal, structured, sometimes approved
Typical use Team syncs, project meetings, client calls Board meetings, committees, associations
Action items Yes, the main focus Yes, alongside motions and votes

Rule of thumb: if the record might be read by a board, an auditor or a regulator, write minutes. For everything else, meeting notes are enough. This post gives you a template for each.

Meeting notes template (short version for team meetings)

For a weekly sync or a client call, the full minutes format is overkill. This shorter meeting notes template keeps only what your team needs to act on:

MEETING NOTES: [Meeting name], [Date]
Attendees: [Names]

Decisions
- [Decision]

Action items
- [ ] [Action] (Owner: [Name], due [Date])
- [ ] [Action] (Owner: [Name], due [Date])

Open questions
- [Question] ([Name])

Next meeting: [Date]

It fits in a chat message or an email body, which means people actually read it.

How to write meeting minutes in 6 steps

If you've never made meeting minutes before, follow these steps in order.

1. Prepare the template before the meeting

Fill in the meeting name, date, attendees and purpose in advance. If there's an agenda, add each agenda item under the discussion section. You'll spend the meeting listening instead of typing headers.

2. Listen for decisions, not dialogue

You don't need to write down who said what. Listen for phrases like "let's go with", "agreed", "so we'll" and "final answer". Those signal a decision. Write it in one sentence, with who proposed it if your organization records that.

3. Capture action items the moment they're spoken

When someone says "I'll do that" or "can you send...", write it down immediately. If the owner or date isn't clear, ask right then: "Who's taking that, and by when?" This one question is the biggest single improvement you can make to the quality of your notes.

4. Park off-topic questions

Unresolved questions go in the parking lot with the name of the person who raised them. This keeps the meeting moving and makes sure the question isn't lost.

5. Clean up and send within 24 hours

Spend five minutes fixing typos, merging duplicate tasks and checking dates. Then send the minutes to everyone who attended and everyone who was absent. Minutes sent a week later are rarely read.

6. Start the next meeting with the action items

Open the next meeting by going through the previous action items: done, in progress, or blocked. This creates accountability without anyone having to chase people.

How to write action items that actually get done

  1. Start with a verb. "Send", "Book", "Draft", "Review", "Decide". Not "Pricing" or "Website".
  2. One owner per task. "Marketing team" is not an owner. If two people share it, pick one lead.
  3. A real due date. "Friday 17 October" beats "next week". "ASAP" is not a date.
  4. Small enough to finish. If it will take more than a week, break it into smaller steps.
  5. Clear definition of done. "Draft the email" and "Send the email" are different tasks.
  6. Visible to everyone. Put tasks in a shared doc or task tool, not in one person's notebook.

FAQ

Should meeting minutes include action items? Yes. They're the most useful part of the minutes because they record who will do what after the meeting. Even formal board minutes usually list actions alongside motions and votes.

What is the difference between meeting minutes and action items? Minutes are the full record of a meeting: attendees, decisions and discussion. Action items are one section inside the minutes: the list of tasks with owners and due dates.

What is the best format for meeting minutes with action items? Meeting basics at the top, then decisions, then an action items table (task, owner, due date, status), then a short discussion summary, open questions, and the next meeting date. Decisions and actions go first because that's what readers look for.

How do you take good meeting notes if you're also in the meeting? Prepare the template in advance, write only decisions and tasks, and ask "who owns that and by when?" whenever a task comes up. Fill in the discussion summary after the meeting.

Can meeting minutes be changed after they're sent? Yes. Informal notes can be corrected at any time. Formal minutes are normally corrected by approving amendments at the start of the next meeting, which is why the template has an "approval of previous minutes" section.

How soon should minutes be sent? Within 24 hours, while everyone still remembers the conversation and before the first deadlines arrive.
