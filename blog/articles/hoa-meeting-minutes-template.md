# HOA Meeting Minutes Template for US Boards in 2026

*By Samarth Wagh, Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A homeowners association meeting minutes template should record 6 things: **meeting type, date, time and location**; **board members present, absent and quorum**; **approval of prior minutes**; **officer and committee reports**; **each motion** with who moved, who seconded and the vote; and **adjournment time**. Record what was done, not what was said. Check your state's rules on sharing minutes: in California, minutes or a summary must be available to members **within 30 days**.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant. This is general information, not legal advice; your governing documents and state law come first.*

HOA meeting minutes are the association's official record. Homeowners read them to understand decisions about dues, repairs and rules. Boards rely on them when a decision is questioned years later. Managers use them to show the board followed its own procedures.

This homeowners association meeting minutes template is built for US HOA and condo boards. It includes a copy-ready template, a filled-in sample, what state law often requires, and the mistakes that cause disputes.

## What should HOA meeting minutes include?

HOA meeting minutes should include 10 items. Most HOA boards follow [Robert's Rules of Order](https://robertsrules.com/) unless their bylaws say otherwise, and these items reflect that practice:

1. Association name and type of meeting (regular board, special, annual, executive session)
2. Date, time and location, or virtual meeting details
3. Board members present and absent, and confirmation of quorum
4. Others present, such as the manager or attorney
5. Call to order and who presided
6. Approval or correction of the prior meeting's minutes
7. Officer, manager and committee reports, summarized
8. Each motion: the exact wording, who moved, who seconded and the vote result
9. Homeowner forum or open comment period, noted briefly
10. Adjournment time and the secretary's signature

## Homeowners association meeting minutes template (copy and paste)

Copy this homeowners association meeting minutes template into Word or Google Docs.

```
[ASSOCIATION NAME]
MINUTES OF THE [REGULAR / SPECIAL] MEETING OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

Date: [ ]   Time: [ ]   Location: [Clubhouse / video link]

CALL TO ORDER
The meeting was called to order at [time] by [President name].

ROLL CALL AND QUORUM
Directors present: [Names and offices]
Directors absent: [Names]
Also present: [Manager, attorney, number of homeowners]
A quorum was [present / not present].

APPROVAL OF MINUTES
The minutes of the [date] meeting were [approved as presented /
approved as corrected: describe correction].

REPORTS
Treasurer's report: [Summary, e.g. operating balance, reserve balance]
Manager's report: [Summary]
Committee reports: [Summary]

HOMEOWNER FORUM
[Number] homeowners spoke on [topics].

OLD BUSINESS
1. [Item]
   MOTION: [Exact wording], moved by [Name], seconded by [Name].
   VOTE: [For / Against / Abstain]. Motion [carried / failed].

NEW BUSINESS
1. [Item]
   MOTION: [Exact wording], moved by [Name], seconded by [Name].
   VOTE: [For / Against / Abstain]. Motion [carried / failed].

EXECUTIVE SESSION
The board met in executive session to discuss [general topic,
e.g. delinquent accounts / litigation]. [Action taken, if any.]

ADJOURNMENT
The meeting was adjourned at [time].

Submitted by: [Secretary name], Secretary
Approved on: [date of approval]
```

## HOA meeting minutes example

Here is a short HOA meeting minutes example for one item of new business. The association and names are placeholders.

```
NEW BUSINESS
1. Pool resurfacing contract
   The board reviewed 3 bids for resurfacing the community pool.
   MOTION: To approve the bid from [Vendor A] for $[amount], to be
   paid from the reserve fund, with work completed before May 1.
   Moved by J. Rivera, seconded by K. Patel.
   VOTE: 4 for, 1 against (L. Chen), 0 abstain. Motion carried.
```

Notice that the minutes give the amount, the funding source and the deadline, but not the 20 minutes of discussion about each bid. That is the right level of detail.

## How do you write minutes for an HOA meeting?

Writing HOA minutes takes about 30 minutes after a 1-hour board meeting if you prepare well. Follow these 6 steps:

1. **Prepare the template from the agenda** before the meeting.
2. **Record attendance and quorum** at the start.
3. **Write each motion exactly** as stated, and confirm the wording aloud if unclear.
4. **Record votes** as counts, and names for any director who votes against or abstains if your board does that.
5. **Draft the minutes within a few days** while details are fresh.
6. **Present them for approval** at the next board meeting, and mark them "draft" until approved.

## When should HOA meeting minutes be distributed?

It depends on your state and governing documents. In California, under [Civil Code section 4950](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_4950), the minutes, draft minutes or a summary of any board meeting other than executive session must be available to members within 30 days, and distributed to any member on request.

Other states set their own rules. Florida's HOA statute, section 720.303, treats minutes as official records that members can inspect and requires them to be kept for years. Many associations post approved minutes on a member portal to make access easy. Check your state statute and your bylaws, or ask your association's attorney.

## Are HOA meeting minutes public record?

HOA meeting minutes are generally not public records in the government sense, because an HOA is a private organization. However, members usually have a legal right to inspect and copy them, and many states require associations to make them available to owners.

Practical rule: write every set of minutes assuming every homeowner, and possibly a judge, will read them.

## What should not be included in HOA minutes?

Leave out 5 things that create risk or noise:

- **Debate and opinions.** Record motions and outcomes, not who argued what.
- **Personal or financial details about owners**, such as names of delinquent homeowners in open session minutes.
- **Executive session details.** Note only the general topic and any action taken.
- **Legal advice** from the association's attorney.
- **Editorial language**, such as "heated" or "excellent presentation".

## What are common HOA minutes mistakes?

The most common mistakes are vague motions ("approved the proposal"), missing vote counts, no quorum statement, minutes that are never formally approved, and minutes stored on one volunteer's laptop. Each one makes it harder to prove what the board decided.

Fix all 5 with a template, a standard approval step at each meeting, and one shared place where approved minutes live.

## FAQ

### What should be included in HOA meeting minutes?

Meeting type, date, time and place, directors present and quorum, approval of prior minutes, reports, each motion with mover, seconder and vote, and adjournment time. Keep motion wording exact and record vote counts, because these are the details homeowners and lawyers check first if a decision is disputed.

### How do you write HOA meeting minutes?

Use a template based on the agenda, record attendance and quorum, write each motion exactly with the vote, leave out debate, and present the draft for approval at the next meeting. Mark the document as a draft until the board approves it, and store approved minutes in one shared place rather than on a volunteer's laptop.

### Are HOA meeting minutes public?

They are not public records like government documents, but members generally have the right to inspect them, and many states require associations to make them available. Ask your manager or board secretary how to request them. Many associations post approved minutes on a member portal to make access simple.

### How soon must HOA minutes be available?

It depends on the state. In California, minutes or a summary of board meetings must be available to members within 30 days. Check your state law and bylaws. Florida treats minutes as official records owners can inspect. Posting approved minutes on a member portal is the easiest way to meet any deadline.

### Who writes HOA meeting minutes?

The board secretary is usually responsible, though a community manager often drafts them. The board approves them at its next meeting. Rotate the task only if your bylaws allow it. Whoever drafts them should send the draft to the president for review within a few days of the meeting.

### Should executive session be in HOA minutes?

Record that an executive session took place, its general topic and any action taken. Do not record the confidential discussion itself. Keep confidential details, such as delinquent owners' names or legal advice, out of open-session minutes entirely, even when the board takes action on them.
