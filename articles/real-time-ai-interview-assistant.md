# Real-Time AI Interview Assistants in 2026: How They Work, and When It's Fair

By [Samarth Wagh](https://lynkk.ai/blog/authors/samarth-wagh) · 4 November 2026 · Employer policies and survey figures checked against the linked sources in November 2026

> **Quick answer:** A real-time AI interview assistant listens to an interview and shows suggested answers on screen as questions arrive. Using one secretly in a live job interview breaks the rules at many employers, and Amazon says it can lead to disqualification. The fair uses are prep, mock interviews and notes for the interviewer, which is where these tools are genuinely good.

A real-time AI interview assistant, sometimes sold as an AI interview copilot, is software that transcribes an interview as it happens and uses a language model to draft answers or prompts. The technology is the same one behind [AI meeting assistants](https://lynkk.ai/blog/ai-powered-meeting-assistants) on sales calls. The difference? Whether everyone in the room knows it's there.

> **Disclosure:** This guide is published by Lynkk, which makes a meeting recorder and an early-access live assistant called Spotter. Spotter is built to be visible in every screen share, so we have a view on the "invisible" tools covered below.

## What is a real-time AI interview assistant?

A live AI interview assistant is an app that hears interview questions as they're asked and suggests what to say, usually in under 5 seconds. Most run as a desktop app, a browser extension or an overlay on top of Zoom, Microsoft Teams or Google Meet.

There are 4 broad types. The type matters far more than the brand:

| Type | Who uses it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Live answer copilot | Candidates, during a real interview | Transcribes questions and shows drafted answers on screen |
| Mock interview coach | Candidates, before the interview | Asks practice questions and scores or critiques your answers |
| Coding interview helper | Engineering candidates | Reads a coding problem and suggests a solution |
| Interviewer assistant | Recruiters and hiring managers | Records the interview and writes notes and a scorecard |

## How does an AI interview assistant work?

It works in 3 steps: capture the audio, transcribe it, then send the question to a large language model that writes a suggested answer. The loop takes a few seconds. That's why answers appear while the interviewer is still finishing the question.

1. **Capture:** the app records your microphone and your computer's sound, so it hears both sides.
2. **Transcribe:** speech-to-text turns the interviewer's question into words.
3. **Answer:** a language model drafts a reply, often using your uploaded resume and the job description.

Some tools read your screen too, which is how coding helpers see a problem in a shared editor. It's also the part employers like least.

## Is it cheating to use an AI assistant during a job interview?

At most large employers, yes: using a live AI assistant without permission breaks interview rules. Amazon's 2025 recruiting guidance tells candidates "please do not use GenAI tools during your interview unless explicitly permitted", and warns that breaking it may lead to disqualification, as [ITPro reported in March 2025](https://www.itpro.com/business/careers-and-training/amazon-bans-ai-tools-during-job-interviews).

The reasoning is simple. An interview tests how you think, and a hidden copilot thinks for you. Amazon still lets candidates talk about how they've used AI at work, so the line isn't knowing AI, it's using it live.

### Which companies push back on AI during interviews?

Several large employers now design interviews around live AI use. Sundar Pichai said on the Lex Fridman podcast that Google will add "at least one round of in-person interviews", and McKinsey asks hiring managers to meet candidates in person before an offer, [according to Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/google-mckinsey-reintroduce-in-person-interviews-due-to-ai/496041).

Fraud is part of the pressure as well. In a Gartner survey of about 3,000 job candidates, 6% admitted to interview fraud, such as having someone else pose as them, and Gartner predicts 1 in 4 candidate profiles could be fake by 2028, [per Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/by-2028-one-in-four-candidate-profiles-will-be-fake).

## Can interviewers tell if you're using an AI interview assistant?

Often, yes. Amazon's 2025 guidance names 3 telltale signs, and they work even when the app itself is hidden from screen sharing: typing while being asked a question, appearing to read rather than respond naturally, and wandering eyes, per the same [ITPro report](https://www.itpro.com/business/careers-and-training/amazon-bans-ai-tools-during-job-interviews).

A tool sold as an "undetectable AI interview assistant" can hide its window from screen capture. It can't hide a 4-second pause before every answer, or an answer that sounds nothing like your follow-up. Follow-up questions are the oldest detection method there is. Ask "why?" twice, and a borrowed answer usually falls apart.

## Which real-time AI interview assistant is best?

The best AI interview assistant is the one that matches an allowed use: a mock interview coach for practice, or an interviewer assistant for hiring teams. For live use during a real interview, the right choice is none, unless the employer has said in writing that AI is allowed.

We haven't ranked named live-answer copilots. We can't verify their claims, and many are built to hide. Judge any tool on these 5 points instead:

- **Visibility:** does it show up in screen shares, or is it designed to hide?
- **Data:** where do your interview recordings and resume go, and are they deleted?
- **Practice mode:** can it run full mock interviews, not just feed answers?
- **Platforms:** does it work on Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet, and on Mac or Windows?
- **Cost:** many charge monthly subscriptions, so check the cancellation terms.

## Is there a free AI interview assistant?

Yes, a free AI interview assistant comes in 2 forms: free tiers of paid apps, and open-source projects. Free tiers give a few minutes of live help or a handful of mock interviews, while projects on GitHub run if you supply your own AI model key. Free tiers usually limit session length, so check before you rely on one.

For practice, you may not need a dedicated tool at all. Paste the job description into any general chatbot and ask it to interview you, one question at a time.

## How can AI help you prepare for an interview?

AI is most useful 1 to 3 days before an interview, not during it, much like any [meeting preparation](https://lynkk.ai/blog/meeting-preparation). Use it to rehearse out loud, find the gaps in your stories, and practise follow-ups until your answers sound like you. Not like a script.

A simple 4-step prep routine:

1. **Brief it:** give the AI the job description, your resume and the company's values page.
2. **Mock it:** ask for 10 likely questions and answer each one out loud.
3. **Push it:** ask for two tough follow-ups on every answer you gave.
4. **Review it:** record the session and listen back for filler words and rambling.

## How do recruiters use AI interview assistants?

The typical AI interview assistant for recruiters is a note taker that records the interview, so the recruiter can watch the candidate instead of a keyboard. It then writes a summary, key quotes and a draft scorecard for the hiring team.

Even Amazon does this. Its own [hiring page](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/artificial-intelligence-resume-jobs-hiring-amazon) describes a pilot that generates real-time transcripts so interviewers can be "fully present", and says it requires explicit consent from candidates before any recording begins. In some technical assessments, candidates are invited to work with an AI assistant openly.

This is the uncontroversial side of the category. As long as the candidate is told. Two rules keep it fair:

- **Tell the candidate:** say at the start that the interview is being recorded and why.
- **Keep humans deciding:** use the notes to compare candidates, not to let software make the call.

## FAQ

### How do you pass an AI interview?

An AI interview usually means a recorded video interview scored by software, not a live person. Treat it like a real interview: look at the camera, answer the actual question in the first sentence, and use specific examples with numbers. Practise with a mock interview tool first, so the format doesn't throw you on the day.

### Can you use ChatGPT during a job interview?

Only if the employer explicitly allows it. Most don't, and some, including Amazon, say using AI tools during the interview can get you disqualified. Using ChatGPT beforehand to research the company and rehearse answers is fine, and it's usually the more useful thing to do anyway.

### Does an AI interview assistant work on Zoom, Teams and Google Meet?

Most do, because they listen to your computer's audio rather than joining the call. Desktop apps tend to work with any video app, while browser extensions often support only Google Meet or calls running in Chrome. Check platform support, and whether your operating system is covered, before an important session.

### Is there an AI interview assistant for Mac?

Yes. Several interview assistants run on macOS, and Lynkk's Spotter is a Mac app with Interview prep and Mock interview modes. Spotter needs a Mac with Apple silicon on macOS 14.2 or later, Lynkk Pro, and early access while it's in development, and it stays visible in every screen share.

### Can you talk about using AI in a job interview?

Yes, and it often helps. Employers want people who use AI well at work, and even Amazon's guidance lets candidates discuss how they've used generative AI in past roles. Give a concrete example: the task, the tool, how you checked its output, and the time or quality it gained you.

### Are AI interview assistants legal?

The software is generally legal, but how you use it matters. Recording an interview can require everyone's consent depending on where people are, and using a hidden assistant can break the employer's rules even when no law is broken. Read the interview instructions, and ask the recruiter if you're unsure.

Used secretly in a live interview, an AI assistant is a powerful tool pointed at the wrong moment. Point it at prep, mock interviews and note taking instead. That's where it earns its keep.
