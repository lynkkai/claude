# What Is an All Hands Meeting, and How Do You Run One People Actually Want to Attend?

*Last updated: 24 November 2026*

> **Quick answer:** An all hands meeting is a 30 to 60 minute meeting where everyone in a company, or a whole department, hears the same update from leadership at the same time. It usually covers results, priorities, changes, recognition and live Q&A, and most companies hold one monthly or quarterly.

An **all hands meeting** gets "all hands on deck": every employee, in one room or one video call. A 10-person startup and a 10,000-person company use it for the same job: telling everyone the same story at once.

Below you will find the purpose, how it differs from a town hall and a 45-minute agenda. You also get 10 engagement ideas and a follow-up plan.

## What is an all hands meeting?

It is a regular meeting for every employee in a company or business unit, typically 30 to 60 minutes long. Leaders share updates, celebrate wins and answer questions, so everyone hears the same information at the same time.

The name comes from the naval order "all hands on deck," meaning every crew member is needed. So the all hands meeting meaning is simply "everyone attends." Some companies call it a company meeting, a monthly sync or a kickoff when it starts a new quarter.

## What is the purpose of an all hands meeting?

The all hands meeting purpose is to align everyone on the same goals and give employees direct access to leadership. Gallup reports that only [16% of US employees strongly agree their leadership communicates effectively](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/716321/leadership-communication-at-work.aspx), so a regular company-wide meeting closes a real gap.

A well-run all hands does 5 things:

- **Shares results.** Revenue, customer, product or mission metrics, with context.
- **Explains priorities.** What matters this quarter and why.
- **Announces changes.** New leaders, reorganizations, policies or launches, in person rather than by email.
- **Recognizes people.** Wins, launches, promotions and work anniversaries.
- **Answers questions.** An open Q&A where employees ask leaders directly.

The same Gallup report found those employees were 61% less likely to feel burned out.

## How often should you hold an all hands meeting?

Most companies hold one once a month or once a quarter. Startups under about 50 people often meet weekly or every 2 weeks, while large companies usually hold a quarterly all hands plus smaller department meetings in between.

| Company size | Suggested cadence | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Under 50 people | Weekly or every 2 weeks | 30 minutes |
| 50 to 500 people | Monthly | 45 minutes |
| 500+ people | Quarterly, plus department all hands | 60 minutes |

Pick a fixed slot, such as the first Thursday of the month, so people can plan around it. For distributed teams, rotate the time or record the session for other time zones.

### Are all hands meetings mandatory?

They are usually expected but not strictly mandatory. Most companies ask everyone to attend live when possible and share a recording, slides and a written summary within 24 hours for anyone who cannot join.

## What is the difference between an all hands meeting and a town hall meeting?

An all hands is a broad update for everyone, while a town hall meeting is built around questions from employees, often with an executive panel. In practice, all hands meeting vs town hall is a fuzzy line: the 2 overlap, and many companies use the terms interchangeably.

| | All hands | Town hall |
|---|---|---|
| Main goal | Share updates and align | Answer employee questions |
| Format | Presentations + short Q&A | Short update + long Q&A |
| Speakers | CEO and team leads | Executive panel |
| Typical frequency | Monthly or quarterly | Quarterly or as needed |

### When should you use a town hall format?

Use a town hall format when employees have more questions than leaders have updates, such as after a reorganization, a funding round, layoffs or a major policy change. Give at least 50% of the time to questions, collected in advance and live.

## What should an all hands meeting agenda include?

A good agenda has 6 blocks: a welcome, results, priorities, team spotlights, recognition, and Q&A. Keep presentations short and save at least 15 minutes for questions.

### All hands meeting agenda template (45 minutes)

| Time | Block | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Welcome and agenda | Host |
| 3 to 13 min | Results since last all hands | CEO or business lead |
| 13 to 20 min | Priorities and changes | CEO |
| 20 to 28 min | 2 team spotlights (4 min each) | Team leads |
| 28 to 30 min | Recognition and new joiners | Host |
| 30 to 45 min | Q&A (pre-submitted, then live) | Leadership |

Share the agenda 2 days before the meeting along with a link where people can submit questions anonymously.

## How do you run an all hands meeting?

To run it well, choose one host, prepare speakers a week ahead, collect questions in advance, and follow up within 24 hours. These 4 habits do more than any slide design.

1. **Name one host.** The host keeps time, introduces speakers and runs the Q&A.
2. **Prepare speakers early.** Give each presenter a time limit and review slides 2 to 3 days before.
3. **Collect questions in advance.** Use an anonymous form so people ask what they really want to know.
4. **Be transparent.** Share bad news as well as good. Vague answers erode trust faster than hard ones.
5. **End on time.** If Q&A runs over, answer the rest in writing.

### How do you run a virtual or hybrid all hands?

For a virtual or hybrid session, keep slides simple, put remote questions first, and use 1 moderator to watch the chat. Record the session and share it with time-stamped chapters so people can jump to the part they need.

## What are some all hands meeting ideas to make it engaging?

The best ideas give employees a role instead of an audience seat. Try 2 or 3 of these 10 ideas and rotate them each month:

1. **Customer story.** Play a 2-minute clip or read a customer quote.
2. **Demo of the month.** A team shows something they shipped.
3. **"Ask me anything" round.** The CEO answers unscreened questions for 10 minutes.
4. **New joiner introductions.** Each new hire shares one fun fact.
5. **Metrics quiz.** A 3-question poll before revealing results.
6. **Shout-outs.** Employees nominate peers in advance.
7. **Behind the scenes.** A team explains how they work.
8. **Guest speaker.** A customer, partner or board member for 5 minutes.
9. **Rotating host.** A different employee hosts each month.
10. **"What we learned."** One honest story about a project that went wrong.

## What should you do after an all hands meeting?

Send a written summary, the recording and answers to open questions within 24 hours. People who missed the call, or drifted during it, rely on that written follow-up.

A good follow-up includes:

- The 3 to 5 key messages in plain language
- Any decisions or changes, with dates
- Answers to questions not covered live
- Links to the recording and slides
- Where to send further questions

## FAQ

### Is it "all hands meeting" or "all-hands meeting"?

Both are used. "All-hands meeting" with a hyphen is the more formal style because "all-hands" modifies "meeting," but many companies drop it. Pick 1 style for your internal docs.

### How long should an all hands meeting be?

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes. Thirty minutes works for weekly startup syncs, 45 minutes for monthly meetings, and 60 minutes for quarterly meetings with a longer Q&A.

### Who should speak at an all hands?

The CEO or most senior leader usually opens, and 2 to 4 other speakers present team updates or spotlights. Rotate team speakers so different people get visibility each month.

### Why are all hands meetings sometimes seen as a waste of time?

They feel like a waste of time when they are one-way slide reviews with no Q&A. Cutting presentations to under 30 minutes and answering real questions fixes most complaints.

### Should you record your all hands?

Yes. Record them so people in 2 or more time zones, on leave or in customer meetings can watch later. Tell attendees at the start that the session is being recorded, and check the [rules on recording meetings](https://lynkk.ai/blog/is-it-legal-to-record-a-meeting) where you operate.
