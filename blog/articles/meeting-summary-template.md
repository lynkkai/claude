# Meeting Summary Template for 2026, With Real Examples

*By Samarth Wagh, Co-founder, Lynkk.ai · Last updated 17 October 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A good template for a meeting summary has **5 parts**: (1) a one-line purpose, (2) key decisions, (3) action items with owner and due date, (4) open questions, and (5) the next meeting. Keep it to **5 to 10 lines**, put decisions and actions first, and send it **within an hour** of the meeting.

*Disclosure: Lynkk publishes this guide and makes an AI meeting assistant that writes meeting summaries automatically.*

A meeting summary is the short recap people actually read. Full notes are for the record; a summary is for action. If someone missed the meeting, the summary should tell them what changed and what they need to do in under a minute.

This guide gives you a copy-ready template for meeting summary emails and docs, 4 real examples, and a 10-minute method for writing one.

## What is a meeting summary?

A meeting summary is a brief recap, usually 5 to 10 lines, that tells attendees and stakeholders what was decided, who will do what, and what is still open. It is less formal than minutes and shorter than full notes.

| | Meeting summary | Meeting notes | Meeting minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 5 to 10 lines | Half a page or more | 1 to 3 pages |
| Audience | Attendees and stakeholders | The team | The organization |
| Formality | Conversational | Informal | Formal, approved |

For the difference between the last two, see [meeting notes vs minutes](https://lynkk.ai/blog/meeting-notes-vs-minutes).

## What is the best template for a meeting summary?

Use this template for meeting summary emails, Slack posts or docs. It fits on one screen.

```
MEETING SUMMARY: [Meeting name], [Date]

Purpose: [One line: why we met]

Decisions
1. [Decision]
2. [Decision]

Action items
1. [Task] | [Owner] | [Due date]
2. [Task] | [Owner] | [Due date]

Open questions
1. [Question] | [Who will find out]

Next meeting: [Date and time]
Full notes: [Link]
```

Notice what is missing: the discussion. Link to full notes for anyone who wants detail.

## What does an email template for a meeting summary look like?

Here is an email template for a meeting summary. The subject line tells people what it is before they open it.

```
Subject: Summary: [Meeting name], [Date] (2 decisions, 3 actions)

Hi all,

Thanks for today's [meeting name]. Here is the summary.

Decisions
- [Decision 1]
- [Decision 2]

Action items
- [Owner]: [Task] by [date]
- [Owner]: [Task] by [date]
- [Owner]: [Task] by [date]

Open question: [Question]. [Name] will follow up by [date].

Next meeting: [Date, time]. Full notes: [link]

Reply here if I missed anything.

[Your name]
```

Putting the count of decisions and actions in the subject line lets people see at a glance that there is something for them.

## What does a good meeting summary example look like?

Here are 4 short meeting summary examples you can adapt.

**Team meeting summary example**

```
Summary: Marketing weekly, October 14
Decisions: Launch webinar on November 6. Pause paid social in Q4.
Actions: Maya: webinar landing page by Oct 21. Leo: draft invite email by Oct 23.
Open: Budget for speaker fee. Maya to confirm with finance by Oct 17.
```

**Client meeting summary example**

```
Summary: Acme Corp project review, October 15
Decisions: Phase 2 scope approved. Go-live moves to December 2.
Actions: Us: revised timeline by Oct 18 (Priya). Acme: test accounts by Oct 20 (Tom).
Next: Check-in on October 22, 10 am ET.
```

**Project kickoff summary example**

```
Summary: Website redesign kickoff, October 16
Purpose: Align on goals, roles and timeline.
Decisions: Launch target February 15. Weekly sync on Tuesdays.
Actions: Sam: project plan by Oct 20. Ana: brand assets by Oct 22.
Open: CMS choice. Dev lead to recommend by Oct 24.
```

**One-on-one summary example**

```
Summary: 1:1 Jordan and Alex, October 17
Topics: Q4 goals, conference request.
Decisions: Alex to lead the onboarding revamp.
Actions: Alex: proposal by Oct 31. Jordan: approve conference budget by Oct 21.
```

Acme Corp and the names above are placeholders for illustration.

## How do you write a meeting summary in 10 minutes?

Write the summary right after the meeting, while details are fresh. Follow these 5 steps:

1. **Write the purpose** in one line.
2. **List decisions** as single sentences.
3. **List actions** with 1 owner and 1 date each.
4. **Add open questions** and who will resolve them.
5. **Send it** to attendees and anyone affected, with a link to full notes.

Tools like Microsoft Teams can generate an AI recap for licensed users ([Teams recap](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/recap-in-microsoft-teams)), but the same 5 steps apply when you review it. If you cannot name a decision or an action, say so: "No decisions today; we will decide on X next week." That is useful information too.

## How do you summarize a long meeting or a recording?

To summarize a 1-hour meeting or a recording, skim for 3 signals rather than re-listening to everything: moments where someone says "so we agree", "can you take that", or "we still need to decide". Those mark decisions, actions and open questions.

A practical approach for recordings:

1. **Read the transcript at 2x speed** or skim the text, if your tool provides one.
2. **Search for decision words** such as "agree", "decide", "approve" and "go with".
3. **Search for action words** such as "will", "by Friday", "owner" and "follow up".
4. **Fill the template** with what you find, then check the 2 or 3 moments you are unsure about.

This takes about 15 minutes for an hour of recording, compared with an hour or more to listen to it all.

## Where should you share a meeting summary?

Share the summary where your team already works. For most teams that means 1 of 3 places:

| Channel | Best for |
|---|---|
| Email | Clients, executives, cross-team meetings |
| Slack or Teams chat | Internal team meetings |
| Project tool or wiki | Project meetings, with tasks linked |

Pick 1 channel per meeting series and stick with it, so people know where to look.

## What mistakes make meeting summaries useless?

Most ignored summaries make 1 of 4 mistakes: they recap the discussion instead of the outcome, list actions without owners, arrive a day late, or bury decisions under paragraphs.

A quick check before sending: could someone who missed the meeting act on this in 60 seconds? If not, cut until they could. For examples of clear action items, see our [action items guide](https://lynkk.ai/blog/action-items-examples-template).

## FAQ

### What should a meeting summary include?

A meeting summary should include the purpose, key decisions, action items with owners and due dates, open questions and the next meeting date.

### How long should a meeting summary be?

Keep a meeting summary to 5 to 10 lines. Link to full notes or a recording for anyone who needs detail.

### What is the difference between a meeting summary and minutes?

A summary is a short, informal recap for action. Minutes are the formal record of attendance, decisions and votes, usually approved at the next meeting.

### When should I send a meeting summary?

Send it within an hour of the meeting, or at least the same day, while people still remember the context.

### How do I start a meeting summary email?

Start with a one-line thank-you and the meeting name, then go straight to decisions and action items. Put the count of decisions and actions in the subject line.

### Can I use the same template for every meeting?

Yes. The 5-part template works for team, client, project and one-on-one meetings. Add or remove optional sections such as open questions as needed.
