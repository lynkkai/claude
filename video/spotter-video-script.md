# Spotter: product video script

For https://lynkk.ai/features/spotter

Target length: 60 seconds (hero cut). A 30-second vertical cutdown is at the bottom.
Format: 16:9 hero, 1:1 and 9:16 crops from the same footage.
**No voiceover.** On-screen text and sound design carry the whole film. It has to read
with the sound off and still land with the sound on.
Tone: calm, confident, no hype. Spotter is help you call for, not a bot talking over you.

Everything shown must be real app footage from the early build. Any shortcut on screen
has to match the real default binding.

---

## Text rules

- One idea per card. Never more than seven words on screen at once.
- Cards sit in the lower third on the 16:9 cut, centered on the vertical crops.
- A card is on screen for at least 1.5 seconds, longer if the viewer also has to read
  something inside the app in the same beat. Never both at once: either the card is up
  or the app answer is readable, not the two competing.
- Shortcut glyphs (⌘↩, ⌘⇧\, ⌘\) are their own element, small and separate from the
  cards, stamped the instant the key is pressed.
- Set the cards in the brand UI typeface, not a title font. This should feel like the
  product, not an ad.

## Sound design

There is no voice in this film, so the sound is the script's other half.

- 0:00 to 0:08 is near silent: room tone, and a muffled voice from the call as if heard
  through a laptop speaker. No music.
- The first deliberate sound is the key click at 0:05.
- The Spotter chime lands on the keypress, once. It is never used again in the film.
- Music enters under shot 4, low, rhythmic, no melody to speak of. It should feel like
  typing, not like a trailer.
- Call audio stays present but unintelligible throughout. We never make out words, so
  nobody has to wonder what was really said.
- Music drops out entirely for shot 6 (privacy). Silence does the work there.
- End card: one soft resolve, then nothing.

## Shot list

### 1. Cold open: the keyboard (0:00 to 0:05)

**Visual:** Extreme close-up, macro, shallow depth of field. A MacBook keyboard lit
from the side, screen glow spilling across the keys. We are tight on the bottom-left
cluster so the ⌘ key fills most of the frame. A thumb rests on it. Nothing moves.

**Sound:** Room tone. Muffled call voice underneath. No music.

**On-screen text:** nothing for the first three seconds. Then, small:

```
You are asked something
you do not have ready.
```

---

### 2. The press (0:05 to 0:08)

**Visual:** The thumb presses ⌘ and the other hand hits Return. Single clean key
travel, shot at 120fps and played slightly slow so you feel the click. On the press,
pull focus from the keys up to the screen behind.

**Sound:** One crisp key click. The Spotter chime lands on it.

**On-screen text:** the card clears on the press. `⌘↩` stamps in, bottom center, held
one second.

---

### 3. Spotter appears on the call (0:08 to 0:19)

**Visual:** Rack focus completes onto the laptop screen. A live call is running,
gallery view, a real face mid-sentence. The Spotter bar slides down from the top of the
screen, over the call, and an answer writes itself into it line by line.

The question on the call is the kind nobody has ready: a pricing objection, or a "how
does this handle our data?" The answer in the bar is specific and short.

Keep the camera on the physical laptop for the first beat, then cut to a clean screen
recording so the text is readable. Hold long enough to actually read it, around four
seconds, with no card on screen competing.

**On-screen text:** one card before the answer starts writing, then nothing:

```
Spotter reads your screen
and hears the call.
```

---

### 4. Three keys (0:19 to 0:34)

**Visual:** Three beats, cut tight. Each one match cuts from the macro keyboard press
to the result on screen, with the glyph stamped as the key goes down.

1. `⌘↩` Get help with this moment. Then: a typed question, Return, an answer.
2. `⌘⇧\` Start or stop listening. Show the mic label "Me" and the system audio label
   "Them" lighting up as both sides are heard.
3. `⌘\` Show or hide the bar. The bar snaps away, the call untouched underneath.

**Sound:** Music enters. Three key clicks, on the beat.

**On-screen text:** one card at the top of the sequence, then one word per beat.

```
Three keys.
```
Then, over each beat in turn: `Ask.` / `Listen.` / `Hide.`

Close the sequence with:
```
Nothing is heard
until you press Listen.
```

---

### 5. Modes (0:34 to 0:44)

**Visual:** The mode picker opens. Cursor moves down the list: General, Meeting, Sales,
Support, Lecture, Interview prep, Mock interview. Land on Sales, then cut to a custom
mode with the user's own instructions and notes pasted in.

Then the payoff beat: the same question from shot 3, answered in Sales mode and in
Support mode, side by side, visibly different. No card over this, let the two answers
be the point.

**On-screen text:**
```
Seven modes, and your own.
```

---

### 6. Visible, and private (0:44 to 0:55)

**Visual:** Pull back to a two-up: the presenter's screen on the left, the attendee's
view of the screen share on the right. Spotter is in both. No hiding, no trick.

Then a short beat on the session: the ask happens, the bar clears, nothing is left in
the notes list. Unless you choose to keep it, and we show that one click too.

**Sound:** Music out. Room tone only.

**On-screen text:** two cards, in sequence.
```
Visible in every screen share.
On purpose.
```
```
Nothing kept, unless you keep it.
```

---

### 7. One call, then the next (0:55 to 1:02)

**Visual:** The call ends. Within seconds the Lynkk notes view fills in: structured
notes, action items, the open question from the call still flagged. Then the next
meeting opens on the calendar and the context panel is already there.

**Sound:** Music returns, resolving.

**On-screen text:**
```
Today's call is tomorrow's context.
```

---

### 8. End card (1:02 to 1:08)

**Visual:** Black. Lynkk logo. Below it, the ⌘↩ glyph, then the URL.

**On-screen text:**
```
Spotter
Live AI help on your calls.

Early access on Lynkk Pro, Mac app.
Apple silicon, macOS 14.2 or later.

lynkk.ai/features/spotter
```

## Production notes

- The cold open only works silent. Resist the urge to put music under the first five
  seconds. The first sound in the film should be the key click.
- With no voiceover, pacing is the risk. The temptation is to fill the space with more
  cards. Do the opposite: fewer cards, held longer. Silence reads as confidence here.
- Shoot the macro keyboard and the screen recording separately, then match cut. Trying
  to get both sharp in one take will cost a day and look worse.
- Use a real call with a real second person. A fake call reads as fake within a second,
  and the whole claim here is that Spotter is in the room with you.
- Every answer shown on screen needs to be a real Spotter output. If an answer is too
  long to read in the cut, change the question, not the answer.
- Label the footage as the early build wherever a frame could be mistaken for the
  shipped product.
- Since there is no voice, there is nothing to caption. Still burn the cards in rather
  than using a caption track, so they survive every platform and crop.
- Shortcuts to keep accurate on screen: ⌘↩ ask, ⌘⇧\ listen, ⌘\ show or hide, ⌘R new
  chat, ⌘← and ⌘→ move the bar, ⌘⇧↑ and ⌘⇧↓ scroll the answer. These are the defaults
  and can be changed in Settings, so if the demo machine has custom bindings, reset it
  before shooting.
