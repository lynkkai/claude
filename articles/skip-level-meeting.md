# What Is a Skip Level Meeting, and How Do You Run One That Actually Helps?

*Last updated: 24 November 2026*

> **Quick answer:** A skip level meeting is a 30 to 45 minute conversation between a senior leader and an employee two levels below them, without the employee's direct manager in the room. Its purpose is to hear unfiltered feedback, spot blockers early and build trust. Most teams hold them at least quarterly, with a shared agenda and clear notes.

A **skip level meeting** is a scheduled conversation between a leader and the people who report to their direct reports. If you are a director, your skip levels are the engineers, designers or reps who report to your managers. The meeting "skips" one level of the org chart, which is where the name comes from.

Below you will find the purpose, the best format and a copyable agenda. You also get 30 questions for both sides, an invite email and a simple way to take notes.

## What is a skip level meeting?

It connects an employee with a leader two levels above them, usually for 30 to 45 minutes. The direct manager is not in the room, by design. That gap is what lets the leader hear things that rarely travel up a reporting line on their own.

People also call it a "skip," a "skip level 1:1" or a "boss's boss meeting." So the skip level meeting meaning is simple: a check-in that skips one layer of management. It is different from a regular [one-on-one meeting](https://lynkk.ai/blog/one-on-one-meeting), which happens between an employee and their own manager every week or two.

| | Skip level | One-on-one |
|---|---|---|
| Who attends | Senior leader + employee two levels down | Manager + direct report |
| Typical frequency | Quarterly | Weekly or every two weeks |
| Main goal | Unfiltered feedback, culture, strategy | Day-to-day work, coaching, priorities |
| Typical length | 30 to 45 minutes | 25 to 30 minutes |

## What is the purpose of a skip level meeting?

Its purpose is to give a senior leader a direct line to the people doing the work. Gallup estimates that managers drive at least [70% of the variance in employee engagement](https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/182792/managers-account-variance-employee-engagement.aspx). Leaders need a way to see how each manager's team is really doing.

A well-run skip does five jobs:

- **Surfaces blockers early.** Process problems, tool gaps and cross-team friction show up here weeks before they show up in metrics.
- **Checks manager health.** It tells a leader how well their managers coach, unblock and recognize people.
- **Connects work to strategy.** Employees hear the "why" behind company goals from someone closer to the decision.
- **Spots future leaders and flight risks.** Career goals and frustrations come out more easily without the direct manager present.
- **Builds trust.** People who are heard by senior leaders tend to raise problems sooner.

## Is a skip level meeting good or bad?

In almost every case, it is a good sign. GitLab asks its leaders to [hold skip levels at least quarterly](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/leadership/skip-levels/), or 4 times a year, with everyone on the team.

Searches like "am I in trouble" and "layoff" next to this term show how often people worry. A sudden invite with no agenda feels alarming. That is why leaders should explain the purpose in the invite. If the meeting is part of a regular cycle and comes with an agenda, it is about listening, not performance.

## How often should skip level meetings happen?

Most organizations hold skips once a quarter, or 4 conversations per employee per year. GitLab's handbook sets quarterly as the minimum. Leaders with too many skip levels can meet a new group each quarter instead.

Use this as a starting point:

| Leader's skip-level team size | Suggested cadence |
|---|---|
| Under 15 people | 1:1 skip every quarter |
| 15 to 40 people | Rotate 1:1s so everyone is seen twice a year, plus one group session |
| Over 40 people | Quarterly group skips of 4 to 6 people, with 1:1s on request |

### How long should a skip level meeting be?

Plan for 30 to 45 minutes. Thirty minutes is enough for a quarterly 1:1 with a clear agenda. Group skips of 4 to 6 people need closer to 45 to 60 minutes so everyone gets time to speak.

## What is the best skip level meeting format?

The best format depends on team size: a 1:1 conversation for teams under 15, and a small group format for larger teams. Both work if the agenda is shared in advance and the leader talks less than 30% of the time.

- **1:1 skip.** Best for honest, specific feedback and career conversations. It scales poorly past about 15 people.
- **Group skip.** Four to six peers from the same team. It covers more people and surfaces shared themes, but people share less about their own manager.
- **Skip level lunch or listening session.** Informal and good for rapport. Pair it with a written follow-up so feedback is not lost.

[SHRM recommends](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/employee-relations/senior-managers-hold-skip-level-meetings) a mixed approach for large teams: 1:1s with managers and supervisors, and small group meetings with everyone else.

## What should a skip level meeting agenda include?

A good agenda has 5 short blocks: a warm-up, the employee's topics, feedback on work and team, career goals, and next steps. Share it at least 2 days ahead so the employee can prepare.

Copy this 30-minute skip level meeting agenda template:

| Time | Block | What to cover |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Warm-up | Purpose of the meeting, confidentiality, how notes will be used |
| 5 to 12 min | Their topics | Anything the employee wants to raise first |
| 12 to 20 min | Work and team | What is working, blockers, how the team collaborates |
| 20 to 26 min | Growth | Career goals, skills, support they need |
| 26 to 30 min | Next steps | Agree on 1 to 3 follow-ups, owners and dates |

Let the employee's topics go first. If they come with nothing, move to the questions below.

## What questions should a manager ask in a skip level meeting?

The best skip level meeting questions are open-ended and specific to recent work. Pick 5 to 7 questions per meeting from the 20 below rather than running through a full list.

**To build rapport**
1. What are you working on right now that you are proud of?
2. What does a good week look like for you?
3. What is something about your work that most people do not see?

**About the work and blockers**
4. What is slowing you down the most right now?
5. If you could fix one process tomorrow, what would it be?
6. Which team do you depend on most, and how is that going?
7. Do you have the tools and information you need to do your best work?

**About their manager and team**
8. What does your manager do that helps you most?
9. What could your manager do more of, or less of?
10. How are decisions made on your team, and is that clear?
11. When did you last get useful feedback, and from whom?

**About culture and strategy**
12. Do you understand how your work connects to our goals this quarter?
13. What is one thing leadership could explain better?
14. What would make this a better place to work?
15. Is there anyone on your team who deserves recognition?

**About growth and retention**
16. Where do you want to be in 1 to 2 years?
17. What skills do you want to build next?
18. What would make you consider leaving?
19. Is there a project you would love to take on?
20. What should I have asked you that I did not?

## What questions should an employee ask in a skip level meeting?

The best questions to ask in a skip level meeting as an employee help you understand priorities and grow. Bring 3 to 5 of them. This meeting is a rare chance to hear how senior leaders think.

1. What are the top 2 or 3 priorities for our group this quarter?
2. How does our team's work affect the company's goals?
3. What does great performance look like at the next level?
4. What skills do you see as most valuable for people in my role?
5. Is there a project or team where I could learn something new?
6. What challenges do you see coming for our department?
7. How can our team make your job easier?
8. What feedback have you heard about our team's work?
9. What do you wish more people at my level knew?
10. How do you like people to share ideas or concerns with you?

## How do you prepare for a skip level meeting?

To prepare for a skip level meeting, spend 15 minutes reviewing context and writing 3 topics you want to cover. GitLab suggests leaders review the attendees' recent accomplishments and note them at the top of the agenda.

**If you are the leader:**
- Tell the direct manager first, explain the purpose, and ask if there is anything you should know.
- Review the employee's recent projects, wins and any notes from your last skip.
- Send the agenda and the questions you plan to ask at least 2 days ahead.
- Decide how you will handle feedback about the manager before the meeting starts.

**If you are the employee:**
- Write down 2 or 3 wins from the last quarter.
- List 1 or 2 blockers and a suggested fix for each.
- Prepare 3 questions about strategy or growth from the list above.
- Keep feedback about your manager factual and specific.

## How do you request a skip level meeting?

Send a short email of under 100 words that states the purpose, the 30 minutes needed and that the direct manager knows. A clear purpose removes most of the "am I in trouble" worry.

**Skip level meeting email template (leader to employee)**

> **Subject:** 30-minute skip level chat on [date]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm setting up skip level chats with everyone on [Manager]'s team this quarter. It's a chance for me to hear what's working, what's getting in your way and where you want to grow. Nothing is wrong, and [Manager] knows we're meeting.
>
> I've attached a short agenda. Feel free to add anything you'd like to talk about.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

**Request template (employee to leader)**

> **Subject:** Request for a 30-minute skip level meeting
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Would you have 30 minutes in the next few weeks for a skip level chat? I'd value your view on [our group's priorities / my growth in this role]. I've let [Manager] know I'm asking.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

## How should you take notes in a skip level meeting?

Take short notes that capture themes, decisions and 1 to 3 follow-ups, not a word-for-word record. Tell the employee at the start how notes will be stored and who will see them.

Good notes include:

- **Themes, not quotes.** Write "two people mentioned slow code review" instead of naming who said it.
- **Follow-ups with owners and dates.** A skip that ends without an action loses trust fast.
- **Context for next time.** What you discussed, what was still open and what you promised.

The hardest part is remembering what was said three months later. An AI meeting assistant such as [Lynkk](https://lynkk.ai/) produces structured notes and action items within about 30 seconds of the call. Before your next skip, it surfaces what was discussed and what is still open, so you start ahead instead of cold. You can also [search every past meeting](https://lynkk.ai/features/ask-your-meetings) to find themes across a quarter of skip levels.

Always ask for consent before recording. Check the [rules on recording meetings](https://lynkk.ai/blog/is-it-legal-to-record-a-meeting) where you work.

Close the loop within a week. A short [follow-up email after the meeting](https://lynkk.ai/blog/follow-up-email-after-meeting) that lists what you heard and what you will do shows the meeting mattered.

## FAQ

### Are skip level meetings confidential?

They are confidential in spirit, but not secret. Share themes with the manager within 1 week, without naming who said what. Say at the start what will and will not be shared.

### What is a skip level meeting with HR?

A skip level meeting with HR is led by an HR business partner instead of a senior manager. The HR partner meets employees 2 levels down to gather feedback. It follows the same agenda and confidentiality rules as a leader-led skip.

### What is another name for a skip level meeting?

There are at least 5 other names: "skip," "skip level 1:1," "skip level check-in," "skip level session" and "boss's boss meeting." Group versions are often called listening sessions.

### Should the direct manager know about a skip level meeting?

Yes. Tell the direct manager at least 2 days before, and explain why. Surprising a manager damages trust in both directions.

### What is the difference between a skip level meeting and a one-on-one?

A one-on-one happens every 1 to 2 weeks between an employee and their own manager about daily work. A skip happens about once a quarter with a leader two levels up, focused on feedback, culture and growth.
